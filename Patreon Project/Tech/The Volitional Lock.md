# TECHNICAL SPECIFICATION SHEET: THE VOLITIONAL LOCK (REVISED)

Official Classification: Cognitive Overwatch
System Status: Active / Non-Biomechanical Neuro-Linguistic Architecture

Author-side reference. No chapter or in-universe document may quote or state this sheet overtly. A chapter may allude to the lock only subtly: that someone is or is not a member, that members stay loyal, a member who stays silent, and the recruit-facing directive as drafted in A2.2. It never names the lock or the tiers, never uses the word "conditioned," and never says a member physically cannot betray (author ruling, 8 November 2026).

## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Volitional Lock is a cognitive conditioning protocol given to every main member of the organization in a session immediately after their first treatment. It does one thing. It makes betraying the organization impossible. It leaves a member's judgment, opinions, loyalties, feelings, and will intact. A conditioned member can disagree, argue, refuse an order, defy the Leader, or resign. A conditioned member cannot knowingly give the organization, its people, its sites, or its technology to anyone outside it. There is one exception: the hostage release valve (Section III-B), which lets a member whose family is held give up information on the lower tiers only.

The lock has two parts. The **Betrayal Block** (Sections II and IV) stops betrayal. The **Protection Tiers** (Section III) set a default order of whom to protect when a member must choose between lives. The tiers never govern disclosure, obedience, or any other act.

## II. THE BETRAYAL BLOCK

The block sits between a thought and its physical execution. It has two mechanisms.

**A. The Threat-Vector Matrix (TVM).** It continuously compares what the member is about to say, write, or do against a fixed set of protected things: the organization's nature and purpose, member identities, site locations, routes, assets, technology, and the Leader. It reads the audience. Speech to an outsider triggers it. Speech inside the organization passes freely.

**B. The Motor-Preemption Reflex (MPR).** If the TVM finds a match in the moment before the action, the motor command is cancelled before it reaches the muscles. The jaw locks, the vocal cords freeze, or the fingers stiffen. The member is aware they are being blocked.

**What the block covers.**
- **Speech and writing.** A member cannot tell, write, type, or signal protected things to an outsider. They still know the secret.
- **Delivery.** A member cannot knowingly sell, hand over, or help an enemy obtain any protected thing. This covers money, threats, drugs, and torture. Money, promises, and threats to the member alone change nothing. A held family member opens only the hostage release valve (Section III-B). Either way the member is free to tell the organization about the situation. The lock treats that as ordinary speech inside the organization, so the organization can act on it.
- **Leaving a post for an enemy.** A member cannot knowingly abandon or open a protected site because an enemy asked for it.

**What the block does not cover.**
- **Silence.** A member may refuse to answer. A member cannot give a false answer that exposes a protected thing.
- **Mistakes and deception.** The block works on knowing acts. A member who is tricked into handing over a fact without knowing it is not stopped.
- **Impersonation and capture.** A conditioned man can be killed, taken, or copied by a stranger.
- **Hired outsiders.** Anyone who was never treated carries no lock.

## III. THE PROTECTION TIERS

The tiers set one thing: the member's default order of protection when they must choose between lives, in a moment with no time to think. The default is a trained priority. It is not an order, and it is not a block. A member can override it by choice, and any member may refuse, defy, or disagree with anyone at any tier.

| Tier | Who or what | Protection default |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Apex | The Leader, and the people the Leader counts as his own (spouse, children, found family) | Protected first |
| 2. The Kinship | The families of all members, and the core inner circle (the leadership) | Protected second. No ranking inside the tier |
| 3. The Organization | The organization's sites, assets, and records | Protected third, as a tool that serves the tiers above it |
| 4. The Cohort | The individual member and their peers | Protected fourth. A member will put themselves at risk for Tiers 1 to 3 without being ordered, but is not required to |
| 5. Outsiders | Everyone else | No default. The member chooses freely. The tiers never weigh an outsider's life against a secret |

**Choice situations (who to save first).** The tiers give a lean, not a command. In a rescue where a member can save only one person, the lock works like this:
- **Same tier, no ranking.** A member's own family and the leadership both sit on Tier 2. The lock does not rank them. The member chooses freely, and a member may save their own child before any director. Their own family is also on their personal list, so the pull toward them is the natural one.
- **Higher tier, a lean only.** Between a Tier 1 person (the Leader or his loved ones) and anyone on a lower tier, the lock leans toward Tier 1 in the first moments. The member can override the lean and save the other person. The lock never freezes them or punishes the choice. It only makes the Tier 1 option feel like the nearer one.
- **Never a freeze.** The lock does not stop a member from acting in a choice situation, and no choice triggers the guardrail in Section V.

**Rules for the tiers**
- Tier 1 commands carry no extra force. The Leader's orders can be refused and defied like anyone's. Only the protection default ranks Tier 1 first.
- The tiers decide betrayal in one case only: the hostage release valve (Section III-B). In every other case the block stops betrayal at every tier. A member whose family is held can always report it to the organization, which the lock allows, so the organization can act on it.
- Peers on the same tier are not ranked by the lock. A choice between two colleagues is left to the member.
- A member's own family is protected out of the same default. Because the Leader and his circle sit on Tier 1, a member's family is never used as a lever against the Leader's.

## III-B. THE HOSTAGE RELEASE VALVE

**Trigger.** An enemy holds a member's own family and demands information. Only a held family opens the valve. Money, promises, torture, or drugs applied to the member alone never do.

**Rule.** While the family is held, the lock ranks each piece of information by the tier of what it is about, measured against the family's tier (Tier 2).
- **Below the family (Tier 3 and Tier 4).** The block relaxes. The member may disclose information about the organization's sites, assets, records, and technology (Tier 3), except for safe houses and any place where a Tier 2 or Tier 1 person is or may be, and about themselves and their peers in general terms (Tier 4), to save their family. For Tier 4 that means the member's own identity, role, and duties, and the general shape of the organization's units: numbers, training, equipment, and routines. It does not include any peer's name, face, address, or whereabouts.
- **The same level or above (Tier 2 and Tier 1).** The block stays at full strength. The member can never disclose anything about the core inner circle (the leadership), the families of any member, the Leader, the Leader's loved ones, or the Leader's whereabouts and orders. The same holds for every safe house and every place where any of them is or may be. The jaw locks, and the guardrail in Section V applies.

**The exposure rule.** Information is ranked by the highest tier it would expose, not only by its subject. Anything that would lead the enemy to a Tier 2 or Tier 1 person is treated as Tier 2 or Tier 1 information and stays blocked. A peer's name or location leads to that peer's family, so it is blocked. A member's own identity is allowed, because their own family is already held. A site's location is allowed, but a list of who works there is blocked. Safe houses are blocked, along with homes, hospitals, schools, routes, and any other place where a Tier 2 or Tier 1 person is, has been, or might be. If the member cannot tell whether a place is one of these, the block treats it as one.

**Example.** An enemy takes a member and her family and demands information on the Leader, the leadership, and the organization. She can disclose what she knows of the organization's assets, and of her peers in general terms, but not their names. She can say nothing about the Leader or the leadership, and the lock stops her trying.

**Limits.**
- The valve gives out only what the member knows. Because each member knows only what the role requires, what can leak is small and varies from member to member.
- The member can still tell the organization first, and the organization can act on the situation before the enemy gets anything.
- The valve does not reach a member who sells for money. That is the block in Section II.

## III-A. KEEPING THE TIERS CURRENT

The lock is set at first treatment, but the people on each tier change. The Leader marries. Members marry, have children, bury parents, and new recruits join. A lock that never updated would leave members holding different lists, and a member treated in 2051 would not know a spouse the Leader married in 2058.

- **One shared list, one personal list.** Tier 1, Tier 3, and the roster of Tier 4 peers are the same for every member. Tier 2 is personal to each member.
- **Updates ride on follow-up treatments.** Members already return for follow-up treatment for as long as they serve. At each one the lock is refreshed. The shared lists are brought up to date, and the member's own Tier 2 is rewritten to match their family now.
- **The gap.** Between visits, the lock holds the last list it was given. A new Tier 1 person is not yet protected by the default, and a new recruit is not yet a peer. The betrayal block is not affected, because it protects the organization as a whole and not a list of names.

## IV. BEHAVIORAL INTERFACE

| Situation | System response | Subjective experience |
|---|---|---|
| Disagreement, argument, refusal of an order, open defiance | Bypasses the filter | Fully autonomous |
| Formal resignation | Bypasses the filter | A member may leave and take their family. The lock stays for life. They leave as walking vaults |
| Conscious betrayal (speech, writing, typing) | Motor suppression | Jaw locks, fingers stiffen. The member knows a barrier is stopping them |
| Slips (drunken talk, sleep-talking) | Semantic deviation and sleep muting | The member veers to a harmless word or goes silent |
| Chemical coercion (truth serums, sedatives) | System hardening | The member becomes unresponsive or speaks only of safe topics |
| A member's family is held and the enemy demands information | Release valve. Subjects on Tiers 3 and 4 may be disclosed. Subjects on Tiers 2 and 1 stay blocked. Reporting inward passes freely | The member can speak of lower-tier matters and is clamped mid-sentence on anything higher |
| Money, promises, or threats to the member alone | Block holds | The member is silent or blocked |

## V. MAINTENANCE AND STRESS POINTS

- **Self-feeding.** The lock needs no maintenance. A member's own thinking keeps the pathways sharp for life.
- **Overdrive guardrail.** If a member tries to force a secret out by sheer will, the strain causes sudden fainting, a nosebleed, or a migraine before the secret leaves the body.
- **Apex conflict.** If the Leader and someone on Tier 1 turn against each other, the protection default has no ranking between them. Members must choose by their own judgment. They are not paralyzed, because the tiers govern no act except protection.
