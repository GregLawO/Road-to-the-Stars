# TECHNICAL SPECIFICATION SHEET: THE MOBILE INFANTRY POWERED SUIT

Classification: Models 1 and 2, public builds. Model 3, private design.
System Status: Model 1 Active. Model 2 Not yet built (date open). Model 3 Not yet built (reactor and date open)

Items marked **(decision)** are my gap-fills, open to overrule. Items marked **(open)** are unsettled.

## I. OVERVIEW

The Mobile Infantry Powered Suit is a wearable system that lets one person carry loads and fight in places a person could not survive unaided. It comes in three models.

1. **Armored Exoskeletal Suit (unpowered).** A steel exoskeleton with armor. It straps over the wearer's clothes and carries the weight of the wearer and the load down its legs to the ground. It lifts five times what a person can lift alone. Oyama Industries builds it and sells it. Kenji's fighters wear it.
2. **Power Suit (battery-powered).** An inner muscle suit worn against the body, and an outer powered armor worn over it, run on batteries. Oyama builds it and sells it.
3. **Power Suit (reactor-powered).** The same two-part suit run on a mini-reactor. It adds anti-gravity jump assist and other features the battery model lacks. The Foundation's own suit. Private, never sold.

Once the material is available, Oyama also sells Model 1 and Model 2 in Gundarium Alpha, beside the steel builds (Section IX).

## II. OPERATION

**Model 1.** The wearer steps into the frame and straps in at the legs, hips, and torso. No power is needed. The wearer moves and the frame moves with them. A load lifted by the wearer goes down the frame's legs into the floor instead of into the wearer's spine. The joints tick as they take weight.

**Models 2 and 3.** The wearer puts on the inner suit first, then steps into the outer armor, which closes over it. The power source in the outer armor runs both parts. The inner suit adds muscle power to the wearer's own movements, and the outer armor carries the weight and protects the body. The wearer controls both by moving. Lift capacity, speed, and runtime are **(open)**.

- **Model 2** runs on batteries. The batteries limit how long the suit works between charges. **(open: runtime)**
- **Model 3** runs on a mini-reactor. It works far longer than batteries allow and has the power for the anti-gravity unit. **(open: runtime)**

## III. FUNCTIONALITY

1. **Load carrying (all models).** The frame or armor takes the weight of the wearer and of the load through its legs to the ground.
2. **Lift multiplication (Model 1).** 5 times a normal person's capacity, up to a maximum of 250 kg (551 lb). A 310 lb crate is lifted from the floor, carried twelve paces at chest height, and set on a truck bed.
3. **Armor (all models).** The armor protects the wearer's body. On Models 2 and 3 the helmet resists rifle fire, and a bullet that strikes it glances off. Model 1's armor is steel plate over the frame. It is rated NIJ RF2. Models 2 and 3 are rated NIJ Level IV (RF3). **(decision: Model 1 has no sealed helmet.)**
4. **Muscle assist (Models 2 and 3).** The inner suit gives the wearer extra muscle power on every movement.
5. **Hardpoints (Models 2 and 3).** The armor carries several mounting points for optional equipment: a back rack for weapons, a combat knife, extra armor, and ammunition bags. Which weapons the fighters carry is **(open)**. **(decision: Model 1 has none.)**
6. **Helmet (Models 2 and 3).** Four cameras on the visor feed a display on the inside of the visor, with a heads-up readout. A light is mounted on each side. **(decision)**
7. **Fit (Models 2 and 3).** Inner suit and outer armor come in male and female builds. The female build is more streamlined.
8. **Standard parts (Models 2 and 3).** Every part is made to one standard, so a part from one suit fits any other suit of the same model.
9. **Personalization (Models 2 and 3).** Each wearer may paint their armor in their own color, pattern, or lettering, and add a personal emblem. Maker's marks are never allowed. **(decision)**

**Model 3 only:**

10. **Jump assist.** An anti-gravity unit in the armor takes weight off the wearer for a moment. The wearer makes very high jumps, softens a landing, and can hover briefly. The unit draws heavily on the reactor, so it runs in short bursts only. **(decision: grade and burst length)**
11. **Magnetic soles.** Each boot carries a magnetic sole that grips metal surfaces, including in low gravity. This suits work on hulls, yards, and stations. **(decision)**
12. **Sneak mode.** The wearer can switch the suit to silence its footsteps. **(decision)**
13. **Vehicle ports.** A large hardpoint on each elbow is made to connect the suit to vehicles and machinery.
14. **Weapon link.** The helmet can be linked to the weapon systems of a vehicle or ship. **(open: which vehicles and ships)**

## IV. TECHNOLOGIES EMPLOYED

1. **Steel exoskeleton frame (Model 1).** Hinged at hip and knee, strapped to the wearer. Cast and assembled by Oyama.
2. **Mechanical load path (Model 1).** Linkages carry the load down the legs to the ground. **(decision: this is how Model 1 reaches 5 times with no power.)**
3. **Inner muscle suit (Models 2 and 3).** A close-fitting layer that adds muscle power to the wearer's own movements.
4. **Outer powered armor (Models 2 and 3).** A shell worn over the inner suit. It holds the power source, the joints, the hardpoints, and the helmet.
5. **Batteries (Model 2).** The power source of the public Power Suit. Type and capacity are **(open)**.
6. **Mini-reactor (Model 3).** A small reactor in the outer armor that powers both parts and the anti-gravity unit. It is a different build from Oyama's tokamak plants. Kenji lacks the tools and money to build it. Type, output, and date are **(open)**.
7. **Helmet sensors (Models 2 and 3).** Visor cameras, display, and heads-up readout.
8. **Anti-gravity unit (Model 3).** A short-range lift unit in the armor, powered by the mini-reactor. It is the same technology as Oyama's anti-gravity sleds, at the private grade. **(decision)**
9. **Magnetic soles (Model 3).** Boot soles that grip metal.

## V. LOAD PATH AND POWER

1. **Model 1: from the load to the frame.** The load transfers into the frame's arms and torso.
2. **Model 1: down the legs.** The weight runs through the hip and knee joints into the feet.
3. **Model 1: into the ground.** The wearer's own body carries almost none of it.
4. **Models 2 and 3: power.** The batteries or the mini-reactor feed the outer armor's joints and the inner suit together, so the muscle assist and the armor's movement stay in step.
5. **Models 2 and 3: the same load path.** The armor still carries the weight down its legs. Power adds strength to it and does not replace it.

## VI. LIMITS

- Model 1 cannot lift a container.
- Model 1 needs no power and cannot give the wearer more than 5 times unaided capacity, 250 kg at most.
- Model 2 works only as long as its batteries last. It has no anti-gravity jump.
- Model 3 cannot be built until a mini-reactor exists.
- Model 3's jump assist is short range and draws high power, so it cannot be held for long. The suit cannot fly.
- No model does what only a higher model can do: Model 1 has no muscle assist, Model 2 has no reactor power or anti-gravity, and only Model 3 has both.
- Grades above these three are not defined in the project files.

## VII. SAFEGUARDS

- **Sale.** Models 1 and 2 are sold openly, per unit, delivered. Nobody is told who drew them, and a buyer who takes one apart is free to try to copy it.
- **Power.** Any powered suit Oyama sells to the public runs on batteries only. The mini-reactor is fitted only to the Foundation's own suits and is never sold.
- **Marking.** Oyama's plate carries Oyama's name and a blank Designer line.
- **Model 3.** Private. Not sold, and its design is never released.
- **Standard parts and capture.** A fallen fighter's suit has parts any other suit of its model will take. What stops an enemy from taking a Model 3 suit and its reactor is **(open)**.
- **Supply.** Oyama's plants cast the frames and armor from stock the Phased Disassembler Arrays produce.
