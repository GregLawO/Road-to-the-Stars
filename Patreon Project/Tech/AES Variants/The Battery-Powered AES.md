# TECHNICAL SPECIFICATION SHEET: THE POWER SUIT (BATTERY-POWERED)

Classification: Public build, sold by Oyama Industries.
System Status: Not yet in service

## I. OVERVIEW

The battery-powered Power Suit is a two-part suit. An inner muscle suit is worn against the body, and an outer powered armor is worn over it. Batteries power both parts. The inner suit adds muscle power to the wearer's own movements, and the outer armor carries the weight and protects the body.

## II. OPERATION

The wearer puts on the inner suit first, then steps into the outer armor, which closes over it. The batteries in the armor run the joints and the inner suit together. The wearer controls both by moving. The armor carries the weight down its legs to the ground.

## III. FUNCTIONALITY

1. **Load carrying.** The armor takes the weight of the wearer and of the load through its legs to the ground.
2. **Muscle assist.** The inner suit gives the wearer extra muscle power on every movement.
3. **Armor.** The outer armor protects the wearer's body. Rated NIJ Level IV (RF3). The helmet resists rifle fire, and a bullet that strikes it glances off.
4. **Jump.** A small anti-gravity unit in the armor takes weight off the wearer for a moment, so the wearer makes very high jumps and softens a landing. The unit draws heavily on the batteries, so the jump has a cooldown between uses and a limit on how many jumps one charge allows.
5. **Energy-dissipating shield (introduced later).** A thin shield dissipates incoming energy before it reaches the wearer. It protects the wearer from two RPGs. After that the battery is drained, and the shield stops until the suit is recharged.
6. **Hardpoints.** The armor carries mounting points for a back rack for weapons, a combat knife, extra armor, and ammunition bags.
7. **Helmet.** Four cameras on the visor feed a display on the inside of the visor, with a heads-up readout. A light is mounted on each side.
8. **Fit.** The inner suit and outer armor come in male and female builds. The female build is more streamlined.
9. **Standard parts.** Every part is made to one standard, so a part from one suit fits any other suit.
10. **Personalization.** Each wearer may paint their armor in their own color, pattern, or lettering, and add a personal emblem.

## IV. TECHNOLOGIES EMPLOYED

1. **Inner muscle suit.** A close-fitting layer that adds muscle power to the wearer's own movements.
2. **Outer powered armor.** A shell worn over the inner suit. It holds the batteries, the joints, the hardpoints, and the helmet.
3. **Batteries.** The power source for the suit, the jump unit, and the shield.
4. **Anti-gravity unit.** A short-range lift unit with a high power cost.
5. **Energy-dissipating shield.** A thin, partial shield with a heavy power draw.
6. **Helmet sensors.** Visor cameras, display, and heads-up readout.

## V. LIMITS

- Jump: a cooldown between jumps and a limit on jumps per charge.
- Shield: about two RPGs, then the battery is drained.
- Recharge: the suit returns to full power only on a charger.

## VI. SAFEGUARDS

- **Sale.** Sold openly, per unit, delivered.
- **Power.** The public suit runs on batteries only.
- **Marking.** Oyama's plate carries Oyama's name and a blank Designer line.
- **Supply.** Oyama's plants cast the armor from stock the Phased Disassembler Arrays produce.

## VII. GUNDARIUM ALPHA BUILD

Once the material is available, Oyama also sells the Power Suit with its armor made of Gundarium Alpha, a strong, light structural alloy. It is lighter and stronger than the steel build.
