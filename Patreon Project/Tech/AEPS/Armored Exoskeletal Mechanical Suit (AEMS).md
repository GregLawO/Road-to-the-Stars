SECTION 1: MATERIAL CHARACTERISTICS & SPECS

**A. Physical Specifications**

- **Volumetric Density:** 3.58 g/cm³ (Gundarium Alpha lattice alignment, balanced for high protection and optimal mobility).
- **Melting / Phase-Change Point:** 1,650°C structural threshold; vaporization matrix transition occurs at 3,200°C.
- **Electromagnetic Shielding Effectiveness:** Complete Passive Immunity. The absence of solid-state electronics, copper lines, or digital nodes renders the chassis fully impervious to EM tracking or degradation.

**B. Mechanical & Tensile Performance**

- **Tensile Yield Strength:** 4,800 MPa. Resists permanent deformation under high-velocity structural loading.
- **Ultimate Tensile Strength (UTS):** 5,400 MPa. Absorbs extreme strain before structural tearing occurs.
- **Modulus of Elasticity (Young's Modulus):** 310 GPa. High internal rigidity ensures minimal flex under high-stress shockwaves.
- **Fracture Toughness:** 180 MPa·m^0.5. Exceptional internal resistance to rapid crack propagation or fracturing from impact stress.

**C. Ballistic & Resilience Metrics**

- **V50 Ballistic Limit:** Rated at 2,150 m/s using a standard 20mm fragment-simulating projectile (FSP).
- **Thermal Shock Deflection:** Dissipates localized heat fluxes up to 16,500 W/cm² across the 0.75-inch isotropic matrix without crystalline degradation.
- **Isotropic Uniformity Metric:** Anisotropy Delta Coefficient is Δ < 0.001%, producing identical load tolerances along all three structural axes.
- **Material Fatigue Limit:** Rated to survive >10^9 heavy load-deflection cycles at 80% maximum yield strength.

SECTION 2: AEMS MECHANICAL CAPABILITIES

**A. Mobility, Strength, and Kinetic Dynamics**

- **Total Armor Plating Weight:** Calculated at a balanced 0.75-inch thickness over a 2-meter surface area to yield a dry armor mass of 136.43 kilograms.
- **Total Suit Mass (Standard Infantry):** Balances at **155 kilograms (approx. 341 lbs)** due to the optimized mid-weight plating, skeletonized structural bracing, and lightweight titanium-alloy linkages.
- **Total Suit Mass (Heavy Specialist):** Balances at **185 kilograms** when configured with mechanical stabilization anchors and heavy ballistic blast shields.
- **Effective Weight to User:** Advanced counterweight linkages and elastic load-bearing paths drop the effective physical weight to **9 kilograms** for the operator during sustained field maneuvers.
- **Deployment Jump Capacity:** Excellent impact attenuation. The mid-weight chassis utilizes internal, heavy-duty mechanical coil springs and dual-stage fluid-damped struts in the lower limbs to absorb unassisted vertical drops of up to 4.5 meters without disrupting forward momentum.
- **Locomotive Enhancement:** The mechanical linkage framework and elastic recoil bands amplify the pilot's natural leg drive, enabling a **sustained sprint speed of 18 to 22 km/h** and a steady cross-country tactical jog of 12 to 14 km/h.

**B. Deflection and Structural Protection**

- **Armor Deflection Layer:** Incoming fire hits the 0.75-inch Gundarium Alpha frame, rendering the chassis entirely immune to all standard infantry small arms, specialized black-tip Armor-Piercing (AP) rifle calibers (5.56mm and 7.62mm), and heavy artillery fragmentation.
- **Heavy Machine Gun Resistance:** The 0.75-inch shell provides reliable ballistic immunity against heavy machine gun fire, including **.50 BMG (12.7×99mm NATO)** and Soviet 14.5mm ball rounds at standard combat engagement distances.
- **Catastrophic Shock Absorption (Mechanical):** Features a progressive-rate fluid damper system within the structural joints that hardens mechanically under high-velocity impact loading, protecting the pilot's limbs from blast waves and kinetic transfer.

**C. Environmental and Thermal Deflection**

- **Extreme Heat Dissipation:** The Gundarium Alpha matrix withstands localized thermal energy fluxes up to 16,500 W/cm² before hitting its phase-change threshold.
- **Passive Sub-Cooling Grid:** Chilled via standard, manual-fill phase-change cooling mesh inserts integrated into the cockpit liner.
- **Total Climate Isolation:** Backed by ultra-dense synthetic aerogel blankets and a sealed cockpit, the suit maintains static interior insulation and provides a 3-hour self-contained breathing envelope.

SECTION 3: AEMS MECHANICAL LOCKOUT FALLBACK STATE

**A. Emergency Lockout Engagement**

- **System Isolation:** If extreme structural damage warps the chassis or jams the primary linkages, the suit disengages standard mechanical pivot channels and initiates the manual Lockout Reserve.
- **Kinetic Bypass Feed:** Emergency release pins allow the operator to instantly disconnect damaged limb segments from the main mechanical drive via manual internal pull-cords.

**B. Lockout Operational Capabilities**

- **Manual Disengagement Walk:** With damaged linkages bypassed, the operator can manually drag the unanchored framework at a emergency escape speed of 4 to 6 km/h due to the highly optimized weight distribution.
- **Zero Payload Capacity:** Complete loss of structural load-bearing assistance; the operator bears the raw physical weight of the uncoupled armor section.
- **Analog Systems Translation:** The suit utilizes a permanent, unpowered periscopic analog viewing visor and backup physical viewing slits.
- **Low-Priority Life Support:** The environment relies strictly on a passive, mechanical filtration mask built into the helmet assembly.

SECTION 4: AEMS VULNERABILITIES

**A. Mechanical and Structural Vulnerabilities**

- **Operator Fatigue and Exhaustion:** Because the suit features no motorized actuators, extended high-exertion sprint operations over 90 minutes induce rapid physical operator fatigue and severe metabolic spikes.
- **High-Velocity Kinetic Compromise:** Direct hits from dedicated, heavy armor-piercing anti-materiel rounds or hyper-velocity kinetic penetrators (traveling over 2,150 m/s) striking a flat surface at a perfect 90-degree angle will breach or stress-fracture the plate layer.
- **Lobster-Shell Joint Jamming:** A heavy explosion or dedicated missile blast directly on a flexing joint (elbow, knee, hip) can warp the sliding plates, permanently freezing the pilot's limb in place.
- **Abrasive Debris Accumulation:** High-grit environments can cause particulate matter to bypass the exterior seals, fouling the mechanical gear teeth and causing minor binding in the low-friction bearings.

**B. Thermal and Environmental Vulnerabilities**

- **Thermal Exhaust Vent Back-Pressure:** A well-placed incendiary or liquid-payload strike that blocks the passive ventilation slits will trap heat inside, posing an immediate risk of cooking the pilot.
- **Thermal Accumulation Risk:** Sustained heavy incendiary damage causes severe internal thermal build-up. Lacking an automated dump system, internal cockpit temperatures exceeding 60°C will cause immediate heat exhaustion or physical collapse of the human pilot.
- **Environmental Contamination Leakage:** Without active positive-pressure systems, damage to the cockpit seals exposes the interior directly to external atmospheric hazards once the 3-hour mechanical filtration limit expires.

**C. Operational Vulnerabilities**

- **Operational Lifecycle Boundaries:** **The framework operates on an indefinite tactical lifecycle limited entirely by the physical stamina, hydration levels, and metabolic endurance of the human operator.** If the pilot is not rotated or permitted a rest window within a 10-to-16-hour deployment phase, the system becomes stationary due to total operator exhaustion.
