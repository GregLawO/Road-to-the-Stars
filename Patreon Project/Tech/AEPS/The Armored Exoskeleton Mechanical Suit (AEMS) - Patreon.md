# TECHNICAL SPECIFICATION SHEET: THE ARMORED EXOSKELETON MECHANICAL SUIT (AEMS)

Classification: High-protection mechanical assault exoskeleton, unpowered. Public build, sold by Pellam Field Systems.
System Status: Not yet in service

## I. OVERVIEW

The AEMS is an unpowered exoskeleton with a continuous 0.75-inch (19.05 mm) solid-matrix Gundarium Alpha shell over a load-bearing framework. It has no motors, no batteries, and no electronics. The wearer's own legs drive it through tension bands, carbon-titanium linkages, and a fluid piston array. Counterweight linkages carry the suit's weight, so the operator feels about 9 kg.

## II. MATERIAL

- **Density:** 3.58 g/cm³.
- **Melting point:** 1,650°C. Vaporization at 3,200°C.
- **Tensile yield strength:** 4,800 MPa. **Ultimate tensile strength:** 5,400 MPa.
- **Young's modulus:** 310 GPa. **Fracture toughness:** 180 MPa·m^0.5.
- **V50 ballistic limit:** 2,150 m/s against a 20 mm fragment-simulating projectile.
- **Thermal shock:** up to 16,500 W/cm² without crystalline degradation.
- **Isotropy:** anisotropy delta below 0.001%. Identical load tolerance on all three axes.
- **Fatigue limit:** more than 10^9 load cycles at 80% of yield strength.
- **Electromagnetic immunity:** complete and passive. The chassis has no electronics, copper lines, or digital nodes.

## III. MECHANICAL CAPABILITIES

1. **Armor plating mass.** 136.43 kg over a 2 m² surface.
2. **Suit mass, standard infantry.** 155 kg (about 341 lb).
3. **Suit mass, heavy specialist.** 185 kg, with mechanical stabilization anchors and heavy ballistic blast shields.
4. **Effective weight to the operator.** 9 kg during sustained field movement.
5. **Drop capacity.** Heavy-duty coil springs and dual-stage fluid-damped struts in the lower limbs absorb unassisted vertical drops of up to 4.5 meters without breaking forward momentum.
6. **Speed.** The linkages and elastic recoil bands amplify the wearer's leg drive: sustained sprint 18 to 22 km/h, tactical jog 12 to 14 km/h.
7. **Weapon and tool mounts.** Modular mechanical weapon hardpoints on the shoulder and forearm, and a heavy physical tool latch array.

## IV. PROTECTION

- **Small arms and fragments.** The 0.75-inch shell is immune to standard infantry small arms, armor-piercing 5.56 mm and 7.62 mm rifle rounds, and heavy artillery fragmentation.
- **Heavy machine guns.** Reliable immunity to .50 BMG (12.7×99 mm NATO) and Soviet 14.5 mm ball rounds at standard combat distances.
- **Shock absorption.** A progressive-rate fluid damper in the joints hardens mechanically under high-velocity impact and protects the operator's limbs from blast waves and kinetic transfer.
- **Shock pads and geometry.** Dual-density mechanical shock pads and ballistic deflection geometry.

## V. ENVIRONMENTAL

- **Heat.** The shell withstands thermal flux up to 16,500 W/cm² before reaching its phase-change threshold.
- **Cooling.** Manual-fill phase-change cooling mesh inserts in the cockpit liner.
- **Climate isolation.** Aerogel blankets and a sealed cockpit hold a static interior temperature and give a 3-hour self-contained breathing envelope.

## VI. MECHANICAL LOCKOUT FALLBACK

1. **Lockout.** If extreme damage warps the chassis or jams the linkages, the suit disengages its pivot channels and enters manual lockout. Passive mechanical relays reset by hand.
2. **Limb release.** Emergency release pins let the operator disconnect damaged limb segments by internal pull-cords.
3. **Escape speed.** The operator drags the unanchored framework at 4 to 6 km/h.
4. **Payload.** None. The operator bears the raw weight of the uncoupled armor.
5. **Vision.** A permanent unpowered periscopic visor and backup viewing slits.
6. **Breathing.** A passive mechanical filtration mask built into the helmet.

## VII. LIMITS

- **Operator endurance.** Sprinting beyond 90 minutes causes rapid fatigue and severe metabolic spikes. Without a rest window in a 10 to 16 hour deployment, the suit stops when the operator is exhausted.
- **Hyper-velocity penetrators.** Heavy armor-piercing anti-materiel rounds and kinetic penetrators above 2,150 m/s, striking flat at 90 degrees, breach or stress-fracture the plate.
- **Joint jamming.** A heavy blast on a flexing joint (elbow, knee, hip) can warp the sliding plates and freeze the limb in place.
- **Debris.** High-grit environments can get past the seals and bind the gear teeth and bearings.
- **Heat build-up.** A strike that blocks the passive vents, or sustained incendiary damage, traps heat. An interior above 60°C causes heat exhaustion or collapse.
- **Seal damage.** Damaged cockpit seals expose the interior to the outside air once the 3-hour filtration limit expires.

## VIII. SAFEGUARDS

- **Marking.** Pellam's plate carries Pellam's name and a blank Designer line.
- **Supply.** Pellam's plants make the shell from stock the Phased Disassembler Arrays produce.
