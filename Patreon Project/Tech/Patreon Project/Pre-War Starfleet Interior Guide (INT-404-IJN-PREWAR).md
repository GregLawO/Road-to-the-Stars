### PRE-WAR IMPERIAL WARSHIP INTERIOR & ATMOSPHERE GUIDE (2122–2132 SOLARIS-4 ERA)

DOCUMENT REF: INT-404-IJN-PREWAR

PURPOSE: NARRATIVE & WORLD-BUILDING REFERENCE

1. OVERALL ARCHITECTURAL AESTHETIC: ARMORED NAVAL STEEL MEETS ARISTOCRATIC JAPAN
- Sloped Naval Geometry: Pre-war IJN- warships (IJN-KSH-, IJN-KNK-, IJN-KGE-, and IJN-DNK-) are built like hyper-dense naval vessels turned into armored void wedges. Every corridor follows the ship's angular hull lines—narrowing toward the prow, braced by exposed, dark-gunmetal neutronium-carbide ribs, heavy hydraulic blast doors, and matte-black anti-spall bulkheads.
- Early-Era Industrial Weight: Constructed during the 2122–2132 build-up in subterranean and newly raised orbital slipways, interiors are utilitarian, tightly packed, and martial. Exposed fiber-optic conduits run along overhead cable trays in bundled geometric channels, while recessed amber-gold LED strips cast warm, low-glare pools of light across brushed steel deck-plates.
- Subtle Japanese Heritage Accents: Passageway intersections feature minimalist dark-cedar and brushed-brass clan plaques bearing the Taira Crimson Banner (Akahata) and Himura crest. Officers' quarters and command spaces use sliding shōji-inspired frosted ballistic-glass partitions and tatami-textured shock-absorbing deck matting.
2. THE COMMAND BRIDGE (KANBŌ / SENTŌ SHIKISHO)
- Sunken Citadel Layout: Buried deep within the armored core of the hull with zero exterior windows, the bridge is a tiered, dimly lit tactical pit surrounded by wrap-around holographic photonic displays.
- The Captain's Dais: At the rear center sits a raised dark-lacquered command chair flanked by twin vertical katana racks holding the Captain's personal mono-blade sword and wakizashi. Directly in front of the Captain floats a golden-amber quantum-optical tactical globe projecting real-time enemy trajectories and firing solutions.
- Human Command Over Photonic Consoles: Living Japanese bridge officers in dark-indigo and crimson-trimmed naval uniforms man sunken gunnery, helm, and sensor stations. Because pre-war Solaris-4 cores cap out at 45% mass-shedding and 99.5% inertial dampening, bridge chairs are fitted with heavy magnetic-cushioned harnesses; during violent combat turns, officers feel a heavy gravitational tug.
- Deterministic Quantum-Optical VI Terminals: Shipboard VIs manifest as silent, cascading columns of golden-amber kanji and geometric telemetry across angled photonic glass slates, chiming with a single, resonant bronze temple-bell tone whenever a target lock or mass-modulation threshold is reached.
3. THE TACTICAL WAR ROOM & BRIEFING SANCTUM (SAKUSEN-SHITSU)
- Strategic Planning Chamber: Located aft of the Command Bridge, this secure planning chamber is where captains, squadron commanders, and retainer officers gather before an operation. A long, dark-lacquered table dominates the center, projecting a crisp golden-amber quantum-optical star map of the target system.
- Sensory Details: Heavy acoustic baffles deaden the ship's background hum to near-silence. Sliding frosted ballistic-glass partitions display ship schematics and enemy fleet dispositions, while armed Ashigaru-4 sentinels stand motionless beside the sealed blast doors.
4. THE CAPTAIN'S READY ROOM & TEA ALCOVE (KANCHŌ-SHITSU)
- Private Sanctum: Reserved for the ship's commanding officer, providing a stark cultural contrast to the cold gunmetal corridors outside.
- Sensory Details: Traditional woven tatami mats cover the deck, bordered by dark cedar paneling and a recessed tokonoma alcove displaying a calligraphy scroll and the Captain's personal armor stand. A low lacquer table holds an electric cast-iron kettle for brewing green tea during quiet moments between watches.
5. THE SOLARIS-4 REACTOR SANCTUM (CHŪSŪ-SHITSU)
- The Matte-Black Sphere: Housed behind triple-interlocked neutronium blast doors and guarded day and night by armed Ashigaru-4 sentinels, the Solaris-4 core (a 2.2m sphere on Frigates, 4.5m octahedron on Cruisers, or 7.0m dual-redundant core on IJN Amaterasu-no-Yaiba) hangs suspended inside a magnetic vacuum pit.
- Sensory Atmosphere: The Solaris-4 core is a silent, matte-black void-metal monolith. Heavy copper-gold phase conduits snake outward from its cradle into the bulkheads, pulsing with a slow, rhythmic golden-amber light. Standing on the observation catwalk, crew members feel a deep, sub-audible bass thrumming in their teeth and sternum, while the air smells faintly of ozone, cold machine oil, and chilled cryogenic coolant.
- Anti-Capture Scram & Self-Destruct Station: Beside the main engineering console stands a locked crimson-and-brass shrine-box housing the physical dead-man keys for Hearth-Quench (core slagging) and Hearth-Collapse (micro-singularity self-destruct), accessible only to the Captain and Chief Engineer.
6. GUNNERY SPINES & TORPEDO LOADING DECKS (HŌGEKI / GYORAI-SHITSU)
- Industrial Firepower Core: Running down the central axis of 750m–1,000m cruisers (housing the 550m–800m axial spinal Macrocannons) or clustered in the forward bow of 250m–500m escorts (housing the bow torpedo tubes).
- Sensory Details: Cavernous industrial bays filled with massive copper-wound electromagnetic accelerator rails, heavy hydraulic breeches, and overhead gantry cranes. During combat reloads, automated mechanical loaders slam multi-hundred-kilogram ferrous slugs or early gravimetric torpedoes into the breeches with deafening metallic clangs, filling the air with hot lubricant vapor and sharp ozone.
7. VENTRAL LAUNCH, SHUTTLE & BOARDING BAYS (HASSHIN-KANPAN)
- Flight & Deployment Decks: Located along the armored underside of the hull, these pressurized hangar decks handle courier shuttles, reconnaissance drones, and strike-team deployments (especially aboard 750m Interceptor Cruisers of IJN-KSH-, IJN-KNK-, and IJN-KGE-).
- Sensory Details: Scuffed neutronium deck-plates scored with magnetic landing strips and amber warning beacons. The bay smells of cold vacuum residue, hydraulic fluid, and scorched hull paint from returning shuttles, with racks of Silver Samurai-patterned exosuits lined up beside the airlock staging chutes.
8. CORRIDORS, ARMORIES & AUTOMATA ALCOVES
- Compact Pre-War Passageways: On 250m Corvettes and 500m Frigates, corridors are two meters wide, forcing human crewmen to turn sideways when passing towering, golden-bronze Ashigaru-4 or Ō-Yoroi-4 automata standing dormant in recessed wall alcoves.
- The Silent Sentinels: Inactive automata stand locked into magnetic wall-charging clamps along major junctions and airlocks, their angular samurai plates gleaming faintly in the amber corridor lights and their horizontal optical visors dimmed to a low, steady ember. When general quarters sounds, hundreds of optical slits flare bright golden-amber in unison with a synchronized metallic clack of unlocking clamps.
- Boarding & Marine Lockers: Near ventral airlocks and interceptor drop-bays, armored lockers house pre-war Silver Samurai-patterned exosuits (powered by Solaris-4M Ember cores), racks of early coil-rifles, and oiled mono-blade katanas.
9. THE INFIRMARY & TRAUMA WARD (IMU-SHITSU)
- Medical Sanctum: Armored near the hull's center of mass to protect irreplaceable living Japanese officers and sailors.
- Sensory Details: Bright, clinical white-and-steel surgical bays equipped with automated robotic surgical arms, trauma stasis cradles, and early cellular-regeneration tanks. The air is cold and antiseptic, lit by sharp white-blue diagnostic panels that contrast with the warm amber lighting of the rest of the ship.
10. CREW QUARTERS, MESS HALLS & SHRINE ROOMS
- Spartan Naval Discipline: Enlisted sailors sleep in triple-tiered, sound-dampened steel bunks folded flush into the bulkheads when not in use, while junior officers share compact two-berth cabins with sliding privacy screens.
- The Wardroom & Mess (Shokudō): Communal dining halls feature long brushed-steel tables bolted to the deck, serving hot rice, miso soup, grilled fish, and nutrient-dense ration packs dispensed from automated galley slots. Lacquerware bowls and green tea are standard issue across all hulls.
- The Shipboard Kamidana (Sanctuary Alcove): Every pre-war Imperial warship contains a quiet, cedar-paneled sanctuary alcove near the officer's deck housing a traditional Shinto-style kamidana shrine, the ship's battle honors scroll, and the crimson-and-gold standard of Clan Himura.
11. THE BRIG & SEALED CONTAINMENT VAULTS (RŌGOKU / FŪIN-SHITSU)
- High-Security Containment: Found on larger cruisers and especially aboard Kinki-Kyoku (IJN-KNK-) and Shadow Squadron (IJN-KGE-) vessels, these maximum-security decks hold captured foreign spies, domestic insurgents, or confiscated enemy prototypes.
- Sensory Details: Dimly lit corridors bathed in crimson security lighting, lined with seamless neutronium-alloy cell doors featuring narrow armored viewing slits and magnetic restraint locks. Every vault is wired directly into the ship's self-destruct circuit to ensure zero sensitive cargo or intelligence can ever be rescued.
12. BRANCH-SPECIFIC INTERIOR FLAVOR (2122–2132)
- Imperial Household Vanguard (IJN-KSH- / IJN Amaterasu-no-Yaiba): Staffed 100% by hereditary Himura retainers, bulkheads are trimmed in dark red lacquer and polished brass; corridors display ancestral clan mon (crests); and the Flagship's private throne-bridge features elevated tatami dais seating, hand-forged display blades, and a private tactical war-room for the Himura Patriarch.
- Bureau of Proscribed Technologies & Shadow Squadron (IJN-KNK- / IJN-KGE-): Interiors are bathed in dim red tactical lighting to preserve night vision, lined with sealed black-box vault racks for sequestered prototypes, and staffed by quiet intelligence officers and matte-black Kage-4 infiltration automata.
- Imperial Japanese Navy (IJN-DNK-): Crisp, disciplined, and relentlessly practical, focused on drill, gunnery readiness, and the 2132 War of Reclamation.
