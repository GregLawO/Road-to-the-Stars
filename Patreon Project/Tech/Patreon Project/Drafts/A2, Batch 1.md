### The Autumn Opening

"*When the Eurasian-Continental Pact launched its coordinated kinetic and missile volleys on the fourteenth of October, 2132, strategic planners in Moscow and Beijing anticipated a swift collapse of East Asian orbital pickets within seventy-two hours. Conventional doctrine held that high-altitude saturation strikes must inevitably overwhelm localized defenses. What the attacking task forces encountered instead over the Pacific was a complete inversion of terrestrial ballistics: heavily armored void wedges maneuvering without reaction thrusters, directional energy barrier planes that deflected relativistic slugs, and standoff torpedo salvos launched from deep shadow without thermal signatures.*"

— *Post-Settlement Naval Monograph, Kyoto*

**A2.1. High Censor Renji Kurosawa**   *2132 — Yokohama Harbor, Yokohama*
Rain slicked the black asphalt of Dock Four as High Censor Renji Kurosawa stepped into the shadow of Warehouse Fourteen. The sea air smelled of salt, heavy fuel oil, and cold iron. Beside the rusted corrugated doors, four Ashigaru-4 line automata stood in recessed wall alcoves, their dark-iron samurai plates motionless and their horizontal optical slits dimmed to a faint, steady ember.

Kurosawa adjusted the collar of his dark coat and checked his physical pocket cipher notebook. The handwritten ledger entries recovered from the illegal Kyushu AI rig pointed directly to this bay. Two Audit-Marshals in dark indigo uniforms stood on the catwalk above, their hands resting on their holstered compliance carbines.

"The cargo lighter *Kaiyo-Maru* finished loading twenty minutes ago," Audit-Marshal Sato reported in a low whisper. "She cleared customs with a manifest for agricultural fertilizers bound for Shanghai."

"Agricultural fertilizer does not require photonic glass storage crates," Kurosawa said. He signaled the lead automaton with a single tap of his thumb against his palm.

The Ashigaru-4's optical slit flared a sharp, golden-amber. The automaton stepped forward with a heavy metallic clack, its hydraulic shoulder joints silent as it drove its gauntleted palm into the warehouse side hatch. The heavy steel lock sheared with a muted pop.

Kurosawa entered first. Inside, six men in unmarked gray work coveralls were hauling silver-strapped shipping crates toward the open stern hatch of a docked coastal vessel. A lean man with a scar across his jaw turned, his hand reaching into his jacket for a suppressed sidearm.

"Compliance Directorate," Kurosawa said, his voice flat and conversational. "Stand down."

The scar-faced man pulled a heavy coil-pistol and brought the barrel up. The lead Ashigaru-4 closed the eight-meter gap in a fraction of a second, its Solaris-4M Ember core thrumming in a low bass hum. The automaton's gauntlet struck the man's wrist, snapping the radius bone before he could squeeze the trigger. The pistol clattered onto the concrete. The remaining five technicians dropped their tools and raised their bare hands into the air.

Kurosawa walked over to the open crate. Inside sat three military-grade Quantum-Optical VI slates etched with encrypted orbital telemetry, alongside a brass-bound authentication key. He picked up the key and turned it over under the dim overhead sodium light.

Imprinted into the polished brass was the official digital clearance seal of the Yokohama Port Authority Executive Office, dated three days prior. Someone high inside the sovereign port administration had personally signed the release forms for the Pact extraction team.

Sato stepped up beside him, holding a pair of magnetic restraint shackles. "Do we halt all outbound commercial traffic in the bay, High Censor?"

Kurosawa held the brass key in his gloved fingers. The harbor warning siren began its midnight test whistle across the water, a low, blaring moan that echoed off the warehouse iron. He reached into his coat for his fountain pen to sign the emergency lockdown order for the entire port district, holding his breath as the ink tip hovered a millimeter above the paper.

**A2.2. Admiral Boris Mikhailovich**   *2132 — Baikonur-Orbital Ring, High Earth Orbit*
The zero-gravity catwalk beneath Admiral Boris Mikhailovich's boots shuddered as a heavy structural welder sparked against the hull of Drydock Nine. Mikhailovich kept his thick arms crossed over his chest, his broad scarred hands gripping his sleeves as he looked down through the reinforced observation viewport.

Below the orbital ring, forty heavy kinetic-rod launch platforms hung in static alignment against the blue curve of Earth. Each platform was a brute-force slab of dark alloy, packed with twenty-meter solid tungsten penetrators and liquid-fueled booster stages. The air inside the catwalk smelled of scorched copper, machine oil, and the dry, metallic tang of recycled oxygen. Mikhailovich had spent thirty years in these orbital yards, rising from a dockyard welder's son to supreme naval command, and he knew every structural seam by heart. Outside the viewport, distant industrial tugs maneuvered heavy fuel bladders under halogen work-lights.

"The final diagnostic sweeps are complete, Admiral," Captain Volkov said, floating beside him with a magnetic data slate. "The Eurasian High Command in Moscow and the Chinese People's Federation defense ministry in Beijing have both transmitted primary launch confirmation. Zero-two-hundred hours tomorrow."

Mikhailovich turned his heavy-set frame toward the tactical briefing annex. Three dozen senior staff officers from Russia, the CPF, and the DPRK stood around the dark central holo-table, their faces lit by the pale blue glare of projected orbital tracks.

"Pay attention to the decoy layering," Mikhailovich said, his booming bass voice cutting through the quiet hum of the environmental scrubbers. "The Japanese pickets behind the Moon are not blind. If we launch forty bare kinetic platforms, their optics will track every hull before the boosters clear geostationary orbit."

He tapped a broad finger against the glass plot, highlighting three hundred commercial freighters and automated ore barges hovering in neighboring orbital slots.

"Three hundred high-albedo freighters will ignite their chemical thrusters simultaneously," Mikhailovich said. "We fire the kinetic-rod platforms from inside their sensor shadows. Their orbital radars will be flooded with three thousand false radar returns and thermal blooms within four minutes. The radar installations in Maui, Chile, and Cheyenne Mountain will see only an unnavigable wall of infrared fire."

"And if their shadow pickets fail to take the bait?" a CPF colonel asked from the end of the table. "Our optical intelligence suggests their new cruisers operate without thermal plumes or chemical exhaust."

Mikhailovich looked at the optical print clipped to his board—a grainy, high-resolution photo of a matte-black Japanese warship wedge drifting silently in lunar shadow, its hull showing zero heat signature against cosmic background cold. He remembered the catastrophic orbital defeats of his youth, the terrifying memory of watching three heavy fusion cruisers burn in ten seconds against weapons that left no debris trail or radar ping.

He opened his mouth to explain how he expected the Japanese shadow pickets to maneuver when the first saturation wave crossed the terminator line, but his jaw set hard. He turned his eyes back to the orbital chart and said nothing.

**A2.3. Fleet Admiral Kazuhiko Togo**   *2132 — IJN Amaterasu-no-Yaiba, Earth-Luna Corridor*
The Sunken Command Bridge of the pre-war flagship *IJN Amaterasu-no-Yaiba* was silent except for the low, rhythmic chime of the deterministic Quantum-Optical VI terminals. Buried behind ten meters of sloped neutronium-carbide citadel armor at the hull's center of mass, the bridge blended severe naval lethality with ancient aristocratic majesty. Dark-lacquered bulkheads trimmed in gold leaf framed woven tatami shock-absorbing deck matting, while twenty-eight hereditary Himura bridge officers sat strapped into sunken gunnery and helm consoles. Angled photonic glass slates cascading with golden-amber kanji telemetry chimed with a deep, resonant bronze bell tone as fire-control subroutines locked onto incoming vectors.

Fleet Admiral Kazuhiko Togo stood on the elevated command dais, his feet planted shoulder-width apart and his hands clasped firmly behind his back. Before him floated a three-meter golden-amber quantum-optical tactical globe projecting the entire Earth-Luna battlespace in real time. Beside the dais, ceremonial racks held master-forged ancestral blades and the crimson silk Taira Banner. Heavy copper-gold phase conduits ran along the bulkheads from the dual-redundant Solaris-4D reactor sanctum on Deck Nine, thrumming with a deep, sub-audible bass that resonated through the deck plates and into the officers' bones.

A cluster of three hundred thermal blooms flared along the Asian equatorial arc.

"Pact launch confirmed," the primary sensor officer called out from the sunken pit. "Target vectors show forty heavy kinetic-rod platforms concealed inside a high-density decoy envelope. Impact window for the Home Islands is eleven minutes."

Togo did not move. "Signal Line Division One and Frigate Squadrons Four and Five. Open Phase-Gated Firing Apertures. Execute standoff salvo."

Across the battlespace, twenty-four 1,000-meter Line Cruisers and sixteen 500-meter Frigates parted their faceted directional barriers for half a second. Twenty-four 800-meter and 750-meter axial spinal Macrocannons discharged simultaneously. Heavy ferrous slugs, accelerated to three percent of lightspeed, lanced across fifty thousand kilometers of void. Overhead gantry cranes and hydraulic recoil dampeners in the gunnery galleries slammed two-hundred-kilogram ferrous slugs into the spinal breeches, filling the ship's lower decks with hot lubricant vapor and sharp ozone.

The lead Pact decoy fleet disintegrated. Over two hundred false freighters and seventy kinetic-rod modules shattered into silent clouds of expanding vapor and molten aluminum.

"First wave neutralized," the gunnery chief reported.

Then the tactical globe flashed crimson. Behind the debris cloud, a second, staggered wave of twenty unspotted kinetic platforms—operating under solid-state cold-glide routines—ignited their liquid-fuel boosters at short range, diving straight toward the Earth-Luna transit line.

The inexperienced Imperial picket line, expecting a single linear salvo, failed to adjust their gravimetric mass-shedding vectors in time. Two 1,000-meter Line Cruisers took kinetic impacts along their forward glacis plating. Their faceted barrier planes flared blinding gold before buckling under the raw momentum of forty-ton tungsten rods.

The bridge deck shuddered under the distant shock, the 99.5 percent inertial dampening pulling heavily against the officers' magnetic harnesses.
A red warning line cut the globe.
"Line Cruisers *Hiei* and *Kirishima* report heavy structural buckling on Deck Three," the sensor officer shouted.
Togo clasped his hands tighter behind his back.
He stepped to the rail.
"Re-form the line," Togo said.

**A2.4. Corvette Captain, IJN-DNK-LE Wolfpack**   *2132 — Low Earth Orbit*
The bridge of the 250-meter Pre-War Corvette *IJN-DNK-LE-42* was buried behind three point five meters of sloped neutronium-carbide armor. Bathed in low-glare amber tactical light, the cramped six-man command pit smelled of ozone, machine oil, and cold green tea. Exposed dark-gunmetal ribs braced the bulkheads, overhead fiber-optic trays bathed the deck in warm light, and vertical katana racks flanked the captain's lacquered dais. Captain Sato kept his boots locked into the magnetic deck plates as the ship held station two thousand kilometers off the port quarter of Frigate *Kuchiku-12*. Beside him, the deterministic Quantum-Optical VI slates chimed with a single bronze bell tone as target coordinates synchronized across the division.

"Wolfpack Division Four in position," the helm lieutenant reported, his hands gripping the magnetic control yoke while keeping tight-beam laser transceivers locked onto the frigate's dorsal sensor mast. "Frigate *Kuchiku-12* has locked passive pings on the CPF dreadnought group off the Marianas."

"Maintain 1:4 formation," Sato ordered, eyeing the floating golden-amber tactical globe. "All four corvettes synchronize bow tubes with the frigate's VI uplink."

Across the five-ship pack (one 500m Frigate leading four 250m Corvettes, mounting thirty-six bow tubes in total), thirty-six Bow Type-90 Kage-Ya Gravimetric Torpedo tubes opened their armored blast shutters. At Sato's command, thirty-six Type-90C Kage-Ya torpedoes (seven hundred fifty kilograms rest mass, armed with depleted-uranium penetrator needles) surged from their tubes through microsecond Phase-Gated Firing Apertures. Inductive surge buses saturated the solid-state graphene capacitor banks in two milliseconds. Onboard Quantum-Optical gyroscopes and dorsal star-trackers locked background starfields into position. Shedding forty-five percent of their apparent mass down to four hundred twelve kilograms, the uncharged penetrators accelerated reactionlessly via forward gravitic-pull drives into relativistic flight.

The thirty-six silent torpedoes wove through the void in a tight, unlit salvo under "Whisper-Glide" ballistic coasting, emitting zero thermal or electromagnetic exhaust signatures.

Five thousand kilometers out, the CPF dreadnought commander spotted the subtle gravimetric distortion along his forward sensor pickets. Instead of deploying conventional point-defense lasers, the dreadnought launched a broadside of high-yield nuclear proximity shells directly into the salvo's intercept corridor.

The nuclear warheads detonated three seconds prior to torpedo arrival. Blinding white fireballs erupted in deep space, leaving a dense, expanding cloud of superheated plasma and millions of hyper-velocity tungsten flechettes hanging directly across the attack vector.

Eighteen Kage-Ya torpedoes, cruising at reduced apparent mass, struck the dense flechette cloud. Their stressed gravimetric lattices collapsed instantly under the severe momentum shear, shattering the airframes into uncharged dust before terminal mass-lock could trigger. The remaining eighteen rounds punched through the firestorm, snapped back to full rest mass at three hundred meters, and gouged massive craters into the dreadnought's broadside armor belt.

On the corvette's console, warning chimes shrieked.
The CPF dreadnought's escort frigates swung their heavy electromagnetic rail-turrets toward the wolfpack's vector.
Red light flooded the helm pit.
Heat alarms shrieked across the console.
"Thermal sinks at ninety-two percent," the engineer yelled.
Sato slammed the cold-gas purge valve.
The hull swung ninety degrees.
Black void swallowed the stars.

**A2.5. Sovereign Taiwanese Destroyer Captain**   *2132 — Taiwan Strait*
Spray from twenty-foot waves lashed the armored bridge windows of the Taiwanese guided-missile destroyer *Roc-Hsuing*. Captain Chen held fast to the command rail as his ship sliced through the churning, storm-darkened waters of the Taiwan Strait.

On the tactical radar scope, four damaged CPF landing ships and two escorting frigates were retreating west toward the mainland coast, trailing smoke and burning fuel after being repelled by joint coastal batteries.

"They are falling back toward the Fujian shore," the tactical officer called out. "If we do not intercept now, they will reach the cover of their continental umbrella!"

"All engines emergency flank," Chen ordered, setting his jaw. "Break formation and close distance. Engage with forward railguns."

"Sir, Imperial Starfleet protocols require us to hold the sector picket line alongside their 1:2 cruiser division," the navigator warned.

"The enemy is right in front of us," Chen barked. "Attack!"

The *Roc-Hsuing* surged out of the defensive line, its twin turbine shafts screaming as it pursued the fleeing landing craft. But as the destroyer crossed the fifteen-kilometer mark off the mainland coast, the sky lit up with harsh crimson flashes.

A hidden battery of CPF heavy electromagnetic coastal railguns, hardened inside subterranean granite bunkers along the Fujian cliffs, opened fire. Massive hundred-kilogram tungsten-carbide slugs tore through the storm clouds at Mach eight, splashing deadly geysers of water around the destroyer's hull.

A heavy slug clipped the *Roc-Hsuing*'s port quarter, tearing away the auxiliary rudder and leaving the vessel dead in the water.

Before the second coastal broadside could strike, a shadow passed over the storm clouds. A 750-meter Imperial Interceptor Cruiser (*IJN-DNK-SP*), executing a low-altitude gravimetric dive, snapped its bow directly into the firing lane between the coastal battery and the crippled Taiwanese ship.

Two massive 150mm coastal railgun slugs slammed straight into the Imperial cruiser's sloped neutronium-carbide chisel prow. The vessel's faceted directional barrier flared a blinding amber, deflecting the first slug into the sea in a shower of white-hot sparks. The second slug gouged a deep, smoking furrow across the upper glacis armor plating before bouncing off into the clouds.

The 750-meter warship held its position without yielding a meter, its Solaris-4C core thrumming with a bone-deep roar that shook the sea beneath *Roc-Hsuing*'s hull.

A tight-beam laser signal flashed from the cruiser's dorsal spine to the Taiwanese bridge console. The speaker chimed once with a crisp, unhurried Japanese naval tone.

"Taiwanese Destroyer *Roc-Hsuing*, this is IJN Interceptor *Tatsuta*," the Imperial captain's voice came through, calm, precise, and entirely unruffled by the smoking scar on his prow. "Explain your reason for abandoning the assigned sector vector."

Captain Chen looked up at the towering matte-black warship shielding his crew from annihilation. He raised his hand to the transceiver switch to account for his rash command, but his fingers froze on the toggle and he offered no reply.
