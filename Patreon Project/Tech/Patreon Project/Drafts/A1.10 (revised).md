**A1.10. Kinki-Kyoku Scout Captain**   *2132 — Deep Space / Sol System Perimeter*

The thermal gauge on the captain's armrest read five hours and ten minutes. The cruiser's solid-state sinks could hold the hull's heat for six, and the Pact satellites had not yet turned away.

The 750-meter Interceptor Cruiser *IJN-KNK-SP* drifted under full Eclipse stealth, a needle-nosed wedge of matte obsidian-charcoal plating with a chisel prow. Its skin temperature sat at 2.8 Kelvin. Inside the sunken command bridge, crimson and amber light fell across sixteen officers strapped into their harnesses. The golden-amber globe in the pit showed the sector as a sphere of dark space, and two bright points marked the hunter-killer satellites three hundred thousand kilometers off the starboard quarter.

"They are sweeping on a fixed pattern," the sensor officer said. He kept his voice low, though nothing out there could hear them. "Each ping passes over the hull and returns nothing, because the plating absorbs it. But the sinks are close to their limit, Captain. If they vent heat before we are clear, the satellites will see it."

"How long between sweeps?" the captain asked.

"Forty seconds when the two beams cross on the far side. They have done it three times since we arrived."

The captain kept one hand flat on his armrest and watched the gauge. Five hours and twelve minutes. He had planned the departure for the first crossing, and the first crossing had come and gone because one satellite had shifted its sweep toward the cruiser's heading. He had waited. Now he had one crossing left before the sinks forced his hand.

"Helm," he said. "Prime the fold pinch. Engage on the next crossing, and not before."

"Primed, Captain."

The two bright points swung slowly across the globe. A faint return flickered on the sensor officer's slate, and the officer went still.

"Port fin," he whispered. "A partial echo. The satellite is turning toward us."

"All stations, hold your positions," the captain said. "Nobody moves."

Sixteen officers froze in their harnesses. The helm officer's knuckles went white on his controls. On the globe, the satellite's beam crept across the hull's outline and slid off the stern.

The beams crossed on the far side.

"Now," the captain said.

The helm officer drove the pinch forward.

Space bent ahead of the prow.

A golden-amber shimmer rolled across the globe.

The hull shuddered once.

The satellites vanished from the pit, and the gauge stopped climbing.

The captain let his hand relax on the armrest. The fold drive carried the cruiser at eighteen light-years a day, and the run to the planetoid would take a little over a day. For the first hours the bridge officers spoke only in short reports. Then the sinks began to cool, the gauge fell, and the helm officer rubbed his eyes. The captain walked the pit once, checking each station, and said nothing about the satellites.

The cruiser dropped out of the fold in the shadow of a rogue planetoid twenty-five light-years from Sol. Its surface was black basalt, scarred and pitted, and its interior had been hollowed by Kinki-Kyoku crews in earlier years. The ventral drop-lock opened with a hydraulic hiss, and four courier shuttles slid out into the dark.

"Container manifest confirmed," the chief engineer reported. "Three sealed black-box containers, Kinki-Kyoku seals intact. Shuttles are loaded and ready."

The captain had signed the manifest at the dock. It listed container numbers and a seal pattern and nothing else. He had never asked what the containers held. He took the lift down to the drop-lock bay and followed the first shuttle through the planetoid's entry gallery on foot, his boots ringing on the basalt floor, until the crew set the first container down in a chamber cut deep into the rock.

Triple-interlocked neutronium blast doors closed behind each one. The seals engaged in turn. The chief engineer counted them aloud, and the captain laid his palm flat on the first door and felt it settle into its frame.

"Third container sealed," the chief engineer said. "The Proscribed Vault Sanctuary is seeded."

The sentinels went in last. A squad of Ashigaru-4 automata stepped from the shuttles in silence and crossed the vault corridor in single file. They did not speak and they did not slow. Each took its post beside a blast door and locked into a magnetic wall clamp. Their optical slits dimmed to a low ember.

The captain watched the last one settle.

"Pull the shuttles back," he said. "Seal the drop-lock and return to Sol. Lay the same stealth profile on the way home."

The return leg took another day. The cruiser dropped out of the fold in the outer Sol perimeter and slid into Eclipse stealth again, the sinks fresh and cold. One of the hunter-killer satellites passed at a distance, pinging, and returned nothing. The captain stayed on the bridge. When the cryptographic annex chimed with a low bronze bell tone, he was already walking toward it.

"A tight-beam array has intercepted a wide-band Pact military burst," the signal officer said. He held out a printed glass slate. "It decrypted on the second pass."

The captain read the transcript. It was brief, a string of orders and acknowledgments between Pact fleet commands, and it said that several task forces were moving to forward readiness stations. It named no date. It named no target. It was a mobilization, and the transcript was the only copy.

He read it again.

Kinki-Kyoku regulations sent every intercept to Kinki-Kyoku headquarters first. But the transcript concerned fleet readiness, and Fleet Admiral Togo commanded the fleet. The cruiser would reach home port in nine hours. Whoever met it at the dock would hold the transcript first, and the captain had to choose who that was. He could not send it by tight beam from this position, because a satellite lying along the beam would trace it back to the hull.

"Captain," the signal officer said. He waited by the annex console with the courier channel open. "Which office should I have the courier shuttle meet at the dock?"

The captain opened his mouth.

He looked at the slate in his hand. He did not answer.
