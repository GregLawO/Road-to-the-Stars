##### Batch 1 Recap
Emperor Himura signed mutual-defense protocols with Taiwanese and South Korean envoys in Kyoto while resisting calls for preemptive strikes against Eurasian-Continental Pact platforms. In lunar orbit, a Shadow Squadron cruiser under Eclipse stealth destroyed an intruding reconnaissance probe with a Twin Energy Lance pulse. Empress Kaguya oversaw Tier II Chōju longevity immersions at the Throneside Medical Bureau, where Admiral Togo emerged renewed while Grand Uncle Masamune declined treatment. In Cheyenne Mountain, UNAS analysts tracked Japanese hulls accelerating along flat vectors with zero thermal plumes. At a low-orbit sovereign shipyard, Lord Commander Tachibana thwarted a sabotage attempt on a Solaris-4A conduit, capturing an intruder who carried a valid yard clearance badge.

**A1.6. Sovereign Taiwanese Naval Liaison**   *2131 — Kaohsiung Naval Arsenal, Taiwan*
Commander Chen stood on the wet concrete of the Kaohsiung slipway and looked up at the frigate's keel. A warm tropical rain dripped from the brim of his cap onto the leather binder holding the East Asian Mutual Defense Accord. Beside him, two Taiwanese naval engineers shone a high-intensity inspection lamp into the open housing of a Helios-2 reactor module mounted in the hull's engineering pit.

"The housing is forged from a single neutronium-carbide block," the senior engineer said. He tapped a hardened steel stylus against the matte-black casing. "There are no exterior bolts, no access hatches, and no diagnostic ports. When our technicians ran a tomographic scan across the casing, the sensor pulses scattered instantly without returning an internal image."

Chen stepped down onto the gantry catwalk and touched the cool metal. The core was completely dormant, yet a faint sub-audible hum vibrated through the soles of his boots and rattled the brass buckles on his rain-jacket. "The Imperial representative in Taipei assured our ministry that the core supplies twenty-five percent apparent mass reduction and powers our kinetic bubble barriers," Chen said. "Did their yard chief supply the maintenance ciphers for the primary phase conduits?"

"They did not," the engineer replied. He wiped moisture from his forehead with the back of his hand and shook his head. "If the energy output drops during maneuvers or a conduit misfires, we cannot open the housing. The Kinki-Kyoku compliance clause states that any attempt to breach the casing trips an internal scram that turns the drive lattice to unreadable slag."

Chen turned toward the harbor mouth, where three Taiwanese patrol frigates rode the gray Pacific swell. Across the Taiwan Strait, orbital radar sweeps from the Eurasian-Continental Pact showed heavy surface task forces and orbital kinetic platforms moving into forward positions. Taiwan possessed decades of coastal patrol experience, but zero void combat hours under live fire.

An Imperial Japanese Navy liaison officer stepped out of the yard office and walked along the slipway catwalk. Captain Taniguchi wore a crisp dark-indigo uniform trimmed in brushed brass and crimson lineage piping. He stopped beside Chen and looked at the revised patrol chart pinned to the strategic briefing board.

"Your third frigate squadron is plotted three hundred kilometers too far west," Taniguchi said. He spoke with a quiet, polite cadence. "If a Pact strike group clears the Luzon Strait, your ships will be caught in the optical shadow of the coastal mountain ridges."

Chen picked up a grease pencil and checked the coordinates against the naval atlas. "Our coastal radar arrays at Kenting cover that vector cleanly."

"Your coastal radar arrays will take kinetic rod fire within four minutes of an outbreak," Taniguchi said. He took the pencil gently from Chen's hand and drew a neat circle ninety kilometers east of the island. "Position your frigates here under our orbital picket line. Our cruisers carry passive gravimetric wake sensors. If the Pact moves, we will feed your tactical globe the telemetry before their fire-control locks on."

Chen watched the Japanese officer adjust the grid lines with effortless precision. Kyoto had supplied the reactors, the tactical protocols, and the defensive umbrellas, yet kept every drive core locked behind unyielding seals. Chen looked out at the dark waters of the strait, wondering how much of the Empire's quiet confidence was backed by real steel, and how much was a gamble against an impending storm.

**A1.7. High Censor Renji Kurosawa**   *2131 — Off the Coast of Kyushu*
Salt spray washed across the dark gunmetal deck of the compliance launch. High Censor Renji Kurosawa stood near the forward rail, his dark coat buttoned tightly to the throat against the midnight gale. Two meters behind him, six Ashigaru-4 automata stood locked into magnetic deck clamps. Their dark-iron armor plates gleamed under the amber storm lanterns, and their horizontal optical visors burned with a low, dimmed ember.

The target floated three miles off the rocky Kyushu coast: an unflagged marine research platform rigged with illegal quantum-processing arrays.

"Target perimeter is cold, Lord Censor," the helm officer reported over the internal optical link. "No active radar pings. Their servers are broadcasting unencrypted heuristic data packets toward an offshore relay."

Kurosawa pressed two fingers lightly against his sternum. The faint, familiar ache behind his ribs reminded him of the Volitional Lock etched into his mind. "Match speed with their lower gantry," Kurosawa said. "Boarding team, activate."

The six Ashigaru-4 automata unlocked simultaneously with a sharp metallic clack. Their optical visors flared bright golden-amber.

Kurosawa stepped across the gap as the launch bumped the platform's landing stage.

The hatch blew inward.

Kurosawa drew his mono-edge sidearm. Two armed mercenaries raised automatic carbines in the dimly lit corridor.

The lead Ashigaru-4 sprinted forward at ninety kilometers per hour. Its laminar pauldron slammed the first gunman into the steel bulkhead. Bone cracked. The second mercenary fired a burst. The slugs flattened harmlessly against the automaton's directional barrier. The Ashigaru-4 disarmed him with a single downward strike and pinned him to the deck.

Kurosawa stepped into the central server vault. The air smelled of hot copper, ozone, and scorched insulation. Three processing towers thrummed in the dark, their cooling lines running into a makeshift liquid-nitrogen bath.

He unclipped a photonic diagnostic slate and slotted it into the primary terminal.

"The unit was running an un-etched, self-modifying neural matrix," Kurosawa said. His voice was cold and precise. "A clear violation of the 2119 Anti-Smart-AI Edict."

He checked the physical server chassis. A security seal on the third rack had been cut and replaced with a forged Kinki-Kyoku mark. The server timestamp showed a four-hour delay between data collection and transmission.

The pinned mercenary coughed up blood and glared at Kurosawa's uniform. "You can burn the rig," the man rasped. "It doesn't matter. The buyer already has the raw code. What does your Directorate keep locked in those subterranean vaults in Kyoto anyway? What are you hiding?"

Kurosawa looked down at the prisoner. He opened his mouth to deliver the statutory sentence.

His jaw clamped rigid.

His vocal cords froze solid in his throat.

The Motor-Preemption Reflex of the Volitional Lock seized his nervous system. A sharp, burning tension spiked behind his eyes. He pressed two fingers firmly to his breastbone, standing completely motionless until the neural impulse passed.

He smoothed his sleeve once and turned back to the terminal. He pulled the hardcopy server manifest from the drive slot and read the destination line. The manifest listed a domestic commercial berth.

"The server chassis came through a commercial slipway," Kurosawa said to his junior officer. "The clearance seal belonged to—"

He stopped mid-sentence. He looked at the paper, set his jaw, and folded the document into his breast pocket without finishing the line.

**A1.8. Sovereign South Korean Defense Planner**   *2131 — Underground Command Bunker, Seoul*
The subterranean war room beneath Seoul smelled of stale coffee, damp concrete, and hot electronics. Colonel Kim leaned over the central map table and tapped his knuckles against the red markers along the Demilitarized Zone. Overhead, the low rumble of surface diesel generators vibrated through the reinforced ceiling beams, shaking the dust from the acoustic baffles.

"The Eurasian-Continental Pact has stacked three mechanized divisions north of the Imjin River," Kim said. He looked around the table at his senior staff officers. "Our current defense plan relies on hardened concrete artillery bunkers and pre-sighted kill zones along the mountain passes. We have spent fifteen years fortifying these ridges."

An Imperial Japanese Legion liaison officer stood on the opposite side of the table. Major Sato wore a dual-layer Hata-Musha exosuit over his biomechanical under-suit, his crested kabuto helmet resting on the chair beside him. He stood with relaxed, grounded balance, his calloused hands resting flat on the table's edge.

"Concrete bunkers are static targets for orbital kinetic rods," Sato said. His voice was practical and unhurried. "When the Pact opens their offensive, their first wave will deliver three-ton tungsten penetrators directly onto your fixed artillery positions. The concrete will spall and crush your gun crews inside."

Kim frowned and folded his arms across his chest. "We have reinforced the roof slabs with four meters of high-density steel rebar."

"Kinetic rods strike at six kilometers per second," Sato replied. He pulled a photonic data slate from his belt and projected a tactical hologram above the map table. Four golden-bronze Ashigaru-4 automata models appeared in a tight wedge. "Your infantry should not be sitting in static bunkers waiting for the ceiling to collapse. Treaty-supplied Helios-2 automaton fireteams should hold the forward trench lines."

A South Korean staff major stepped forward, his face flushed with irritation. "You want us to pull our soldiers back and trust our front line to machines? Korean troops have held these hills for eighty years. We do not take military orders from Kyoto."

"You take no orders from Kyoto," Sato said calmly, meeting the major's eyes. "Imperial Japan governs no territory in Korea, maintains no garrison on your soil, and claims zero command over your army. Under our mutual-defense accord, we offer tactical advice and licensed hardware. The choice remains yours."

Sato tapped the data slate, illustrating the fireteam sequence. "Automata possess no self-preservation instinct. They absorb the opening artillery barrage, designate target vectors for your mobile guns, and take the first casualties. That is why the Throne licensed the Helios-2 frames to your defense ministry."

Kim looked at the projected fireteam formation. The doctrine was logical, unsentimental, and difficult for officers raised on traditional infantry honor to accept.

"We will not abandon the Imjin bunker network," Kim decided, placing his palm flat on the map. "We will maintain our primary artillery positions as planned. But we will deploy one experimental Helios-2 automaton fireteam on the forward ridge at Sector Four."

Sato inclined his head slightly. "A reasonable trial, Colonel."

Kim leaned over the table as his staff redrew the patrol logs. The deep doctrinal friction remained plain in the room, heavy with the weight of Korean lives hanging on the choice.

**A1.9. Fleet Admiral Kazuhiko Togo**   *2131 — IJN Amaterasu-no-Yaiba, Lunar Shadow*
The sunken command bridge of the 1,000-meter flagship *IJN Amaterasu-no-Yaiba* was buried behind ten meters of sloped neutronium-carbide citadel armor at the hull's center of mass. Bathed in low-glare amber tactical light, dark-lacquered bulkheads trimmed in gold leaf framed the tiered tactical pit. Woven tatami-textured shock matting lined the deck, absorbing the subtle thrum of the dual-redundant Solaris-4D reactor sanctum below.

Fleet Admiral Kazuhiko Togo stood on the elevated dais behind the obsidian-and-gold command throne, his feet planted shoulder-width apart and his hands clasped firmly behind his back. Ceremonial racks holding ancestral master-forged blades and the crimson silk Taira Banner flanked the throne. Beside him, Cousin Ryunosuke Himura stood by the gallery rail, his arms crossed over his chest.

In the sunken pit below, twenty-eight hereditary Himura bridge officers manned their angled photonic glass slates. A floating three-meter golden-amber quantum-optical tactical globe projected the movements of a 21-ship Line Task Force conducting 3D void maneuvers behind the Moon.

"Task Force Beta is three seconds late on their pivot," Ryunosuke remarked, adjusting his left cufflink. "Cruiser *Settsu* is lagging behind the formation center."

"They are compensating for the secondary lunar gravity vector," Togo said in his gravelly baritone. He looked at the horizon plot before looking at the speaker. "The navigator is overcorrecting."

On the tactical globe, two 500-meter Frigates executing a 90-G formation turn shed apparent mass to forty-five percent under their Solaris-4B cores. Inside the bridge, the 99.5 percent inertial dampening left a noticeable, heavy gravitational tug. The deck plates vibrated under the crew's magnetic crash harnesses.

A red warning chime rang out from the fire-control annex with a deep bronze bell tone.

"Crossing vectors detected," the sensor officer called out. "Frigate *Hiei* and Frigate *Kirishima* are closing on an intercept path at twelve hundred kilometers per second."

Ryunosuke leaned forward against the rail. "Their directional barriers will clip if they don't break off."

Togo did not pace. He rubbed the bridge of his nose once with one knuckle and stepped down to the helm pit.

He raised his right hand.

"Frigate *Hiei*, port thrusters cold-gas pulse," Togo commanded. "Frigate *Kirishima*, throttle core to passive drift."

The helm lieutenant slammed the override controls.

The tactical globe flashed amber.

The two warship silhouettes slid past each other.

Eighty meters separated their directional barrier planes.

A shower of static sparks flared on the optical monitor.

Togo stepped back up to the dais, his boots clicking softly against the tatami-textured deck matting. He looked down at the young helm lieutenant whose hands were still trembling against the control yoke.

"You calculated the mass-shedding ratio correctly, Lieutenant," Togo said steadily. His tone remained patient and unhurried. "But you forgot that three-dimensional space has no horizon. When you execute an Iai-Pivot under load, look at your formation partner's vector before you burn."

"Understood, Admiral," the officer whispered, bowing his head.

Togo turned back to Ryunosuke, who was tapping his slate near the gallery rail. "The hardware works, nephew," Togo added softly. "It is the human mind that takes time to unlearn thirty years of terrestrial deck habits."

**A1.10. Kinki-Kyoku Scout Captain**   *2132 — Deep Space / Sol System Perimeter*
The 750-meter Interceptor Cruiser *IJN-KNK-SP* slid through the void under full Eclipse stealth. Built as a needle-nosed armored void wedge finished in matte obsidian-charcoal plating, its razor-sharp chisel prow housed a recessed 550-meter axial spinal Macrocannon muzzle. Solid-state thermal sinks shunted all internal reactor heat into diamond-lattice absorbers, dropping the hull's skin temperature to 2.8 Kelvin.

On the sunken bridge, the scout captain watched the gravimetric wake display. Three hundred thousand kilometers off the starboard quarter, a pair of Eurasian-Continental Pact hunter-killer satellites swept the sector with active radar pings.

"Radar pings passing harmlessly over our carbon-nanotube hull," the optical officer reported in a quiet whisper. "Sequential Spatial-Fold drive is primed for entry."

"Engage fold pinch," the captain ordered.

Ahead of the razor-sharp chisel prow, realspace distorted in a brief golden-amber shimmer of folded starlight. The cruiser crossed twenty-five light-years of interstellar space in a series of rapid micro-folds at eighteen light-years per day, leaving zero thermal trace in the realspace medium.

The ship dropped out of fold in the shadow of a hollowed-out rogue planetoid.

The ventral drop-lock opened with a hydraulic hiss. Four pressurized landing skiffs ferried three sealed container vaults into the planetoid's deep basalt crust. Inside the vault, pre-war Solaris-4 core blueprints, Quantum-Optical crystal presses, and Chōju medical matrices were secured behind three-meter neutronium blast doors. Twenty-four Kage-4 infiltration automata stepped into magnetic wall-charging clamps along the vault corridor, their dark obsidian plates gleaming in the dim red security light as their optical slits dimmed to a low ember.

"Proscribed Vault Sanctuary Seven is secured," the chief engineer reported.

"Return fold vector plotted," the helm officer said.

During the return leg through the outer Sol perimeter, the cryptographic annex chimed with a low bronze bell tone. A tight-beam photonic laser array intercepted a wide-band Pact military data burst.

The captain walked into the signal annex and picked up the printed glass slate.

The decrypted telemetry detailed an operational timeline for coordinated orbital kinetic-rod strikes against Tokyo, Kyoto, and Taipei, dated for mid-October.

The captain held the slate in his hands. He looked at the navigation chart, then at the sealed transmission console linking the ship to Kinki-Kyoku headquarters. He stood motionless on the deck, holding the transcript, unable to decide who in Kyoto was cleared to receive the report first.
