# Byron Pyrrus: Character Profile

Oyama Industries and Byron Pyrrus come from the author (a Mass Effect canon corporation and owner). Age, home town, family, and history are placeholders. Items marked **(decision)** are invented gap-fills.

## 1. Basic Identity & Demographics
- **Name & nicknames.** Byron Pyrrus. Employees say "Mr. Pyrrus" to his face and "B.P." behind his back. Kenji says "Byron." After his death is staged he is Anthony Strauss. **(decision)**
- **Age.** Born 1984; sixty-eight in July 2052, dying of lung cancer. **(decision)**
- **Appearance.** Big, stooped, large hands, gray crew cut. A steel wedding ring worn thin on one side (his wife died in 2047). Plain gray suit with a plant badge clipped inside the jacket, which he never lets staff see him take off. **(decision)**
- **Voice & mannerisms.** Flat Ohio accent; short, concrete sentences, numbers given in units and days. Turns his worn ring; when it is bad he puts both hands flat on the table and looks at them. Dry plant-floor humor ("If it's a good design, it'll still be good on Friday"); never jokes about a man who lost a finger.

## 2. Psychology & Internal World
- **Core goal.** Immediate: the board's approval of the Janus contract before Monday. Overall: Oyama outliving him with its nine thousand workers still paid.
- **Needs vs. wants.** He wants to have built something that lasted, not run a plant until it was sold. He needs a successor he trusts.
- **Internal conflict.** Oyama against Kenji's secrecy: he refuses three offers for the patents (2058) and cannot tell his board who stands behind the foundation or why. **(decision)**
- **Flaws.** Assumes anyone who is not a maker is slow and is caught out by lawyers and financiers; slow to see a buyer who wants to close a plant; underrates how much of Kenji's caution is fear. Honesty 3 of 5: keeps secrets about the company and his illness but does not lie about pay.
- **Fears & secrets.** Fear: his workers on the street. Secret: he hid his cancer from his board for a year and keeps the doctor's letter in the desk of the plant he started in. **(decision)**
- **Worldview.** Loves the new designs and does not ask where they come from. **(decision)**

## 3. Backstory & History
- **Upbringing & family.** Toledo, Ohio factory town (glass, auto suppliers, rail yards); his father ran a parts line until the owners shut it. **(decision)**
- **Defining moment.** At fifteen new owners shut his father's plant in a month and chained the gate; his father stood outside it an hour with his hat in his hand. Object: his father's time-clock card, in his wallet. **(decision)**
- **Education & occupation.** Manufacturer; second-best, tool-and-die maker. Owns Oyama Industries outright, nine thousand workers, plants in several countries. When the Akron plant burned he went in before the fire crew and brought out two men. **(decision)**
- **Prior relationships.** Owes Kenji for his lungs and years, and the foremen who kept the plants running.

## 4. Story Role & Stakes
- **Function.** Main-cast POV (A2.3, A2.12); the factory.
- **Facts.** Recruited July 2052 in a private hospital room with Vance and four operators in the corridor. His death is staged and he returns as Anthony Strauss to run Oyama for Kenji. Oyama builds the industrial Phased Disassembler Arrays (so the organization never runs short of materials), the Primitive Med-Pods (Kenji builds none), Level 1 robots (later Levels 2 and 3), Level 1 powered suits, Tokamak reactors, Level 1 anti-gravity, and Gundarium Alpha. Kenji gives him the security-droid design for Oyama's use, not for sale, and Byron keeps the drawings in his own safe.
- **External conflict.** Cerberus: Oyama is its first target. Offers arrive January 2058 (when the Primitive pods go on sale), April (forged audit), and October. He refuses all three.
- **Stakes.** A plant closing on his watch, or Oyama hurting the workers he keeps safe.
- **Rules for the text.** When someone says a thing cannot be built he asks what would have to change and goes to look at the machine; when his workers are threatened he goes to them first. No rich-man-only or comic-boss scenes, no sudden gratitude speeches.

## 5. Dialogue Voice Guide **(decision; built from this profile; sample lines are mine, not canon)**

**1. Vocabulary & Word Choice.**
- Flat Ohio plant talk: line, press, shift, payroll. Every number comes with a unit or a day. "Yep." "Nope." "Hm."
- He asks about the object, not the idea. Dry humor in one flat line, never about a man who lost a finger.
- Samples: "How many boxes? Which shift needs them? Give me those two and I'll find the key." / "That's a drawing. Bring me the part." / "Tuesday works. Wednesday doesn't."

**2. Sentence Rhythm & Punctuation.**
- Short concrete sentences with a number in them. He cuts in on abstractions: "Skip that. What does it cost?" He repeats his date when offered a later one.
- He restates a deal once in his own words and waits for yes or no.
- Reply length: usually two sentences, the second a number or a day. A one-word answer is about one in five, never more than two in a row.

**3. Nervous Habits & Tells.**
- Asked about his health, he answers with the schedule. Asked about his workers, he gives their pay.
- Cornered, he asks what a part costs. Angry, fewer words, and "my workers" appears. He turns the ring while he thinks.
- He never lies about pay.

**4. Dialogue Traps (What to Avoid).**
- No "I built this from nothing," no talk of his father, no speeches of thanks.
- No fluency in finance or law.
