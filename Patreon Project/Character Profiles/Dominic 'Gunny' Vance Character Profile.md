# Dominic "Gunny" Vance: Character Profile

Name and rank are placeholders. Items marked **(decision)** are invented gap-fills.

## 1. Basic Identity & Demographics
- **Name & nicknames.** Dominic Vance, Gunnery Sergeant, USMC, retired. **(decision)** Operatives and Kenji call him "Gunny." Brooks says "Sergeant" at first, "Vance" later. He dislikes "sir": "Don't call me sir. I work for a living."
- **Age.** Born 2002; forty-nine and dying in a hospice in 2051. **(decision)**
- **Appearance.** Broad, heavy frame, gray buzz-cut, a scar across the knuckles of his left hand. A gray coat even in July, hiding the sidearm he will not enter a room without. Breathes deeply and slowly, noticing every breath, because he once could not.
- **Voice & mannerisms.** Eastern Carolina accent that flattens when working; some Spanish. Plain, economical, complete: the position first, the reason second; explains military terms to civilians. Takes a slow breath through the nose and his shoulders drop; when it is bad his right hand rests on the sidearm without drawing it until the room is clear. Dry gallows humor at his own expense; never jokes about the dead.

## 2. Psychology & Internal World
- **Core goal.** Immediate: the next site secured before the rollout date. Overall: a unit again, people to train and look after.
- **Needs vs. wants.** He wants to die on his feet, not in a bed. He needs to accept a role that is not a command.
- **Internal conflict.** His duty to the people on the ground against the secrecy he agreed to. He kept it even when it meant not telling hospice staff the cure worked. **(decision)**
- **Flaws.** Expects enemies with weapons and is slow to see attacks of contracts and money. Assumes people stay from loyalty to the unit when many stay from gratitude or fear. Underrates how much of Kenji's caution is fear. Honesty 4 of 5, but bad at lying: he says nothing and volunteers nothing.
- **Fears & secrets.** Fear: a threat he cannot see; he can hold any ground he can walk but not defend against paper, money, or a lawyer's office. Secret: he watches the sentries train at night; their movements are his own, copied from his memories, including how his dead squadmates moved.
- **Worldview.** Grateful and wary: every gift is a weapon in someone's hands.

## 3. Backstory & History
- **Upbringing & family.** Jacksonville, North Carolina, a base town beside a Marine camp. **(decision)**
- **Defining moment.** His squad walked into an ambush on bad intelligence and he came home with the survivors; later he was dying in a bed with no one to pay for his care. **(decision)** Memory: the gully before the ambush and where each man stood. Object: dog tags from his old squad in the gray coat pocket. **(decision)**
- **Education & occupation.** Small-unit trainer and site defender; second-best, range safety officer. **(decision)** Small pension and large medical debt at the start; later well paid, spends almost none.
- **Prior relationships.** Owes Kenji for his lungs and years, and the men of his old squad. Brings in Ashby.

## 4. Story Role & Stakes
- **Function.** Main-cast POV (A2.7 ledger, A3.1). First recruit and first leader of the network; Kenji's security detail. He taught Kenji where to sit in a room.
- **Facts.** Summer 2051 in the hospice he trades his memories for treatment; the copy becomes the droids' fighting style, and only Kenji and Vance know. Brings in Ashby, expands the team twice before Byron (four fighters in winter 2051, four more plus three intelligence staff in spring 2052; fighters wear Level 1 frames). Walks behind Kenji with Ashby until the two Level 5 guards are finished in 2055. Recruits Danny in 2052 and writes his death into the ledger in 2059. Hears "Cerberus" from Marchetti the night of the third offer. Conditioned from the start (author ruling, 5 November 2026).
- **External conflict.** Cerberus, who attack through paper he cannot defend against; he opens a file on Brooks after the October 2061 forged letter.
- **Stakes.** Losing the people he trained to a threat he never saw coming.
- **Arc.** He finds exits and the best position before he sits, puts himself between danger and those in his care, and asks to see the site before guarding anything. He teaches anyone who asks and stays until the last person is out.
- **Rules for the text.** A3.1 must match every gesture of A1.5. No muscle-only or tough-guy-cliché scenes; he does not explain the setting and is not smarter than everyone about money or law.
