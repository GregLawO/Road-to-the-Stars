# Hannah Shepard: Character Profile

Items marked **(decision)** are invented gap-fills, open to overrule.

## 1. Basic Identity & Demographics
- **Name & nicknames.** Hannah Shepard. Her father calls her "Darling" and she answers to it only from him. She calls her parents "Mom" and "Dad," never their first names. **(decision)**
- **Age.** Born 2031; twenty-two when her father is cured and she refuses the knowledge upload. **(decision)**
- **Appearance.** A pale burn scar the length of her left forearm from the 2059 blast, never covered. Short, practical hair; uniform squared and clean. Stands with weight on both feet, never leaning, hands always busy while she listens.
- **Voice & mannerisms.** Gulf Coast Texas accent that returns when tired or angry; working Spanish from the yard crews, used to calm frightened people. **(decision)** Plain, measured, complete; says what she means in a few sentences, then stays silent until the other person speaks. Presses her thumb along the scar; when it is bad she goes very still and quiet. Dry, shipyard-grim humor at her own expense or the weather; never jokes about children.

## 2. Psychology & Internal World
- **Core goal.** Immediate: a straight answer from someone, a day without discovering another missing record, person, or explanation. Overall: to raise a child who lives.
- **Needs vs. wants.** She wants the whole truth from the man she married and would trade a great deal of it for a living child. She needs to ask the questions she avoids.
- **Internal conflict.** Duty and loyalty to her husband against her sense of what is right. She stays, serves, and says openly that she disagrees. The cost is that she cannot fully trust him.
- **Flaws.** Misreads silence: takes her husband's silences as protection when they are often fear or guilt, and assumes anyone keeping a secret has a good reason until the gap is too large to excuse. Avoids questions whose answers she suspects she cannot bear. Lies only about whether she is all right.
- **Fears & secrets.** Fear: losing a child again, and being used without knowing it. Secret: a private list of every question she chose not to ask her husband, each dated. Shown only inside her head.
- **Worldview.** Trusts a gift that heals, because she watched her father stand up and ask for breakfast; distrusts a gift with no giver. Distrusts people who manage the truth for others' own good.

## 3. Backstory & History
- **Upbringing & family.** Galveston shipyard district; everyone knew everyone's wages and illnesses. Adoptive older brother Danny (dies 2059). Father taught her to check the weld.
- **Defining moment.** The 2059 bomb at the Galveston clinic complex she ran security for, five months pregnant. She counted the night staff and moved them out by name before looking out for herself, then lost the child. Underneath: someone knew more than they told her.
- **Education & occupation.** Navy officer from 2053 (one gold stripe in A1.1; avoid naming her rank in 2054), left February 2056, clinic security chief, then her own private security firm from 2060. **(decision)** Debt from her father's illness, no savings; owns very little, mostly her father's toolbox and his tape measure.
- **Prior relationships.** Refused the clinic's training upload in 2053 and has been polite and cold to its operators since. Her father's tape measure is the object she measures with when unsure.

## 4. Story Role & Stakes
- **Function.** Major POV; the character who notices the gaps and keeps asking.
- **External conflict.** The secrets of her husband's organization; Cerberus.
- **Stakes.** Her people's lives; another lost child; learning the lies were part of why she lost the first.
- **Character arc.** She goes quiet when given a partial answer, works out afterward what not asking cost her, moves toward danger when someone in her care is threatened, and says it once, plainly, when she disagrees with an order. She never leaves behind someone she could reach.
- **Rules for the text.** No speeches, no wronged-wife or grieving-mother-only scenes, no convenient forgiveness. No unexplained knowledge of what she has not been told.

## 5. Dialogue Voice Guide **(decision; built from this profile; sample lines are mine, not canon)**

**1. Vocabulary & Word Choice.**
- Plain, counted nouns. She opens with "Okay, so." and asks for names, times, and places. "Good" and "fine" mean yes.
- Calls her parents "Mom" and "Dad." Short Spanish with frightened people: "Tranquilo. Aquí estoy." Gulf Coast ("y'all," "fixing to") comes out only when she is tired or angry.
- Grim humor, aimed at herself or the weather.
- Samples: "Okay, so. Who's on the door tonight? Not who's scheduled. Who's actually there." / "I'm good. Where's the sign-in sheet?" / "That's not what I asked."

**2. Sentence Rhythm & Punctuation.**
- Short counted items, not paragraphs. She says what she means in a few complete sentences, then stays silent until the other person speaks.
- She asks the same question twice in different words. She cuts in only after she has waited through an answer, and only on the part that dodged.
- Reply length: usually two or three sentences. Her one-line cut-ins are about one in five, never more than two in a row.

**3. Nervous Habits & Tells.**
- A partial answer makes her exact about the part she did get (names, times) and she returns to the gap later.
- Angry, she gets slower and quieter and the accent shows. She says "I'm good" quickly about being all right, then asks for a task. Afraid, she talks logistics.

**4. Dialogue Traps (What to Avoid).**
- Never "We need to talk," "How could you?", "I deserve the truth," "I feel like," "boundaries," "I trust you," or "I forgive you."
- No therapy words and no explaining her own feelings.
