# Kenji Himura: Character Profile

Items marked **(decision)** are invented gap-fills, open to overrule.

## 1. Basic Identity & Demographics
- **Name & nicknames.** Kenji Himura. Hannah, the Shepards, and the found family call him "Kenji." Tom adds "son" in private. Before the 2058 wedding he calls Tom "Sir" and Ruth "Ma'am"; after it, "Dad" and "Mom." Brooks says "Mr. Himura" in 2062, "sir" from 2065. **(decision)**
- **Age.** The body was born 2020, so he is thirty when he wakes in March 2050. The mind inside is older; how old is undecided.
- **Appearance.** Hands scrubbed pink, short nails, chapped knuckles. Plain dark workshop clothes, no logos, a good jacket only when forced. Stands at the edge of rooms near a door and sits with his back to the wall (a habit the marine taught him). Looks at what is in your hands before your face.
- **Voice & mannerisms.** Plain English, no accent; a few Japanese words (numbers, "ah") when exhausted. Speaks a few sentences at a time with a reason and often a follow-up question, never a bare one-line answer. Rare comparisons, always from workshops and tools. Dry, rare humor, aimed at himself or a tool. Squares objects on a desk and washes his hands, twice when it is bad; at the worst moments his hands stay flat on the desk. Never says "it can't get worse."

## 2. Psychology & Internal World
- **Core goal.** Immediate: finish the task in front of him before the next lands. Overall: keep himself, Hannah, and his people alive while he builds a foundation in secret. He plans to live. He treats his own survival as a design problem: guards, secrecy, and structures that keep working even if his enemies succeed.
- **Needs vs. wants.** He wants a quiet dinner with Hannah where nobody needs anything from him. He needs someone to tell him he can stop working, and to accept being cared for without paying for it.
- **Internal conflict.** His secrecy against Hannah's wish for openness. He tells her no false thing and answers "I can't answer that" when he will not answer.
- **Flaws.** Withholds constantly (how he knows what he knows, where the money comes from). Reads coincidence as hostility and feels the universe working against him. Assumes people serve from gratitude or conditioning, not choice. Underrates how much of Hannah's dissent is love. Sees people as tools in his mind and catches himself doing it.
- **Fears & secrets.** Fear: losing the people he has; before 2065 a cautious man's unease about the unknown. Secret: he is a transmigrator. No character ever learns it or how he knows what he knows.
- **Worldview.** Trusts the technology completely and the people who will use it very little. In 2051 he decides only a weaker public version of any technology may leave his workshop, so nothing traces back to him.

## 3. Backstory & History
- **Upbringing & family.** Past life: poor and lonely in a crowded district, a quiet apartment where someone else's footsteps always passed his door. Wakes in a flat in the Houston-Galveston shipyard belt, a short walk from Tom Shepard's yard. **(decision)**
- **Defining moments.** Past-life dinners eaten alone and finished to the last bite, which is why he finishes every meal and flinches at being wanted. The 2059 bomb that killed his and Hannah's child.
- **Education & occupation.** Builder of production systems (factories, the tools that build the tools). Second-best: clinical technician, with full Med-Pod expertise. Owns almost nothing in his own name, only his tools.
- **Prior relationships.** None romantic ("No. I don't think anyone got close enough"). Owes Tom, who calls him "son"; Hannah, who stayed; and the marine, who traded his memories for treatment.

## 4. Story Role & Stakes
- **Function.** Protagonist and anti-hero; first-person POV from A2 (A1 keeps him unnamed, no POV).
- **External conflict.** Everyone who would take the pod and the man who built it, above all Cerberus.
- **Stakes.** Hannah and the found family; the child already lost.
- **Character arc.** Loses materials, money, time, people, and trust, never the end state. His line: he never puts a directive in Hannah or the found family and leaves the memory seal out of their treatments. Even on his worst day he asks someone what they need and means it.
- **Rules for the text.** No Reaper thoughts and no past-fiction thoughts before 2065. Past-life references stay under three sentences per chapter. No smug-genius scenes.

## 5. Dialogue Voice Guide **(decision; built from this profile; sample lines are mine, not canon)**

**1. Vocabulary & Word Choice.**
- Literal and exact. Names of things and numbers whenever a number exists. "Okay" and "right" to acknowledge. He asks "Is that enough?" and "Do you have what you need?" before he asks for anything.
- People keep their titles until told otherwise: "Senator," "Sir," "Ma'am." He apologizes once, flatly.
- Samples: "Which room do you need? I'll tell you what's in it before you take the key, so you know what you're carrying." / "Okay. Is that enough, or do you need more?" / "Sorry. That sounded like an order. It wasn't one."

**2. Sentence Rhythm & Punctuation.**
- Short statements in a fixed order: the answer, the reason, then a question back to the other person. Contractions in ordinary talk, none in a promise.
- He takes questions literally and asks which part is meant. When someone's feelings come into the room, he stops explaining and asks what they need.
- Reply length: two to four sentences. A one-sentence reply is allowed only for "I can't answer that." or a line that already carries its reason. Never more than two in a row.

**3. Nervous Habits & Tells.**
- When Hannah asks a large question, he answers the narrowest true part and says "I can't answer that." for the rest.
- Called "son," he goes quiet and his next line is a task. Thanked, he asks what the person needs. When someone is kind to him, he checks: "Did that make sense?"
- Angry, he gets very polite, slower, and lower in volume. Exhausted, a number or an "ah" slips out.

**4. Dialogue Traps (What to Avoid).**
- Never "trust me," "believe me," "to be honest," or any hedge about his own machines.
- Nothing that hints where his knowledge came from.
- No jokes while someone is hurt. One dry line a scene, at most, aimed at himself or a tool.
- Keep matched pairs ("I can't promise X. I can promise Y.") for real promises only.
- Never meek when he sets terms or picks people. He does not hand his own problem to the other side, does not hedge a decision he has made, and does not apologize for a term.


## 6. Story rules (moved from Specific Writing Rules, 9 November 2026)
- **POV.** From A2 on, Kenji carries POV. A1 stays as published: unnamed, no POV. Every Kenji chapter is in the first person ("I"; author ruling, 3 November 2026), and no other narrator may use "I." Drafted Kenji chapters: A2.1, A2.4, A2.6, A2.11, A2.14 (the Excerpt Bank also lists A2.17 and A2.19). No fixed count of Kenji's or Hannah's POV chapters for now (author, 7 November 2026).
- **No Reaper thoughts, and no thought that his new world is Mass Effect, before 2065 (author ruling, 4 November 2026).** He may think about any fiction he remembers from his past life, such as a film. Until 2065 he does not think that the world comes from a story, and does not think about the Reapers.
- **No Kenji chapter is set in 2065 or later until B1.2.** A3 has no Kenji POV, because it is dated after the 2065 realization apart from A3.1.
- **Past life.** At most three sentences per chapter, only through small habits, such as finishing every meal.
- **How he knows.** Nobody, in a chapter or a document, says how Kenji knows what he knows. No character is ever told, and no in-universe document or character reveals it.
- **Before 2065.** Full knowledge of his technology from the start. He knows the Med-Pod is the one from the film Elysium, but not how or why he knows how to build it. His unease is a cautious man's. He may suspect he has transmigrated (revealed at A2.1).
- **Speech.** Short declaratives, few metaphors, workshop and tool images when he does use one. Dry humor is rare.
- **Fate habit.** Never says "it can't get worse." Notes what arrives early or late, never calls it luck.
- **Fast Gear tell.** Squares objects on a desk and washes his hands. His own chapters never name shaking hands.
- **Fate (author ruling).** He thinks and feels as if the universe is working against him. The text mostly allows chance or human causes to explain what happens. Fate costs him materials, money, time, people, and trust, never the end state.
- **Kenji and Hannah (A2b and A2c).** Every Hannah-and-Kenji micro-chapter (A2.8, A2.13, A2.14, A2.16) has at least one scene where the two talk, and something between them is different by the end. The change shows in what they say and do, not in what an object means. Kenji tells Hannah no false thing (the A2.14 promise).
- **When he uses his technology (author ruling).** He keeps advanced technology and robots from the public until a time he chooses. He never refuses to use them when someone he holds dear is in danger, and uses them whenever the use can be disguised. When he does, he makes it look ordinary.
- **His name** may appear in in-universe documents for minor matters only.
- **Honesty.** 4 of 5.
- **Terms of service as he offers them.** The whole bargain is stated in the first offer, before anything is signed: the years given back are spent working with him, treatment continues for as long as the member serves, whoever serves serves for life, and the treatment covers the member's family and loved ones. The secrecy session is disclosed in the same conversation. The family-and-continued-treatment perk belongs to the offer itself, not to a later contract.
- **His stance when he holds the cards.** With recruits, lawyers, officers, and operators he leads, and he is sure of his plan and his judgment of people. How it shows:
  - *He decides.* A hard question gets an answer or a decision. If he lacks the answer, he says what he will do about it. He never turns a gap into a request that the other person fix it for him.
  - *He plans for failure.* He assumes the worst can happen, including his own death, and builds so the system holds anyway. He intends to survive and says so, and he also builds for the day enemies succeed.
  - *He is open about the cost.* He states the price and the risk before anyone signs, lets people refuse, and replaces someone who refuses without anger. He builds checks on himself on purpose, tells the person who holds one that he can overrule it, and says it up front.
  - *He picks people on stated, checkable qualities* and tells them why he chose them. He does not flatter, and he does not need to be liked in the room.
  - *He stays calm.* No heat, no pleading, no boasting. His only tell is squaring papers on the desk.
  - *Limits.* This is command that comes from the work, not from ego, so it never becomes smug-genius, and he takes no pleasure in power or thanks. With Hannah and the Shepards he is still assertive, in a milder way: he says plainly what he will and will not do, without pressing, and asks what they need. The two stances do not conflict: his authority comes from the work and his care from the people.
- **Avoid.** Smug-genius scenes. Enjoying being thanked. Anyone being told where his knowledge came from. Past-life references over three sentences.
