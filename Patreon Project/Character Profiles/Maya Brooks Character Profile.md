# Maya Brooks: Character Profile

Items marked **(decision)** are invented gap-fills, open to overrule.

## 1. Basic Identity & Demographics
- **Name & nicknames.** Maya Brooks, almost always "Brooks." Kenji says "Maya" in private only. She says "Mr. Himura" in 2062, "sir" from 2065. The cadre calls her "the Ledger" behind her back. **(decision)**
- **Age.** Born 2038; twenty-four when recruited. She was dying at twenty-three.
- **Appearance.** Dark hair pinned flat and tight, plain well-cut grays and blacks, no jewelry, shoes for quiet floors. Sits with a clear line to the door; her eyes move across a room in the same order every time.
- **Voice & mannerisms.** Plain English with the accent trained out; Dutch from childhood, working Mandarin and Russian. **(decision)** Precise, complete, unhurried: the figure, then the reason. followed by a number. Her thumb walks across her fingertips counting silently; when it is bad she stops and holds her hand flat on whatever is nearest. Deadpan, numerical humor; never jokes about the dead.

## 2. Psychology & Internal World
- **Core goal.** One fewer unknown each day: a missing fact found, a blank filled. Overall, every debt known and every account closed.
- **Needs vs. wants.** She wants nothing left unaccounted for. She needs to be allowed to stop counting.
- **Internal conflict.** Her judgment against Kenji's orders: she states her objection once, completely, then carries it out.
- **Flaws.** Misreads what cannot be measured: thrown by loyalty with no incentive, treats grief and conscience as factors to manage, assumes Kenji's silences are plans when they are often fear. Lies fluently when the work needs it (honesty 2 of 5), though almost never to Kenji or Hannah.
- **Fears & secrets.** Fear: someone working against her whom she cannot see; a coincidence too neat to be chance. Secret: a private count of the innocent dead she has caused, with names.
- **Worldview.** Trusts the age as a tool: a cure is leverage, a ship is an asset, every gift has a cost recorded somewhere.

## 3. Backstory & History
- **Upbringing & family.** Rotterdam dock district, brick apartment blocks; her mother kept the books for a small shipping agent. **(decision)**
- **Defining moments.** At twenty-three a neurologist pushed a card across a desk with her remaining years in blue ink, and the pen was running out. Earlier, her mother's work was ruined when a partner altered the books and left her to take the blame.
- **Education & occupation.** Read other countries' ministries for six years; her paper on the Ankara tariff schedules was the best her directorate produced. Spymaster; second-best, forensic accountant. Student debt and her mother's medical bills before recruitment.
- **Prior relationships.** Owes Kenji for the sixty years she was not supposed to have. Her object is a small black notebook ruled in columns, with a rubber band. **(decision)**

## 4. Story Role & Stakes
- **Function.** POV and analyst; the network's eventual head. Office: Chief Intelligence Officer of the Foundation (author ruling, 9 November 2026). A1.5 recruitment, A3.1 introduction to Kenji, A3.6 slip.
- **External conflict.** Cerberus; the unsolved 2059 bombing file she is handed first and never closes (the bomb was a rival elite's, not Cerberus's).
- **Stakes.** The network and the people she placed; figures that never add up.
- **Character arc.** She keeps working past the point anyone else stops and tells no one until she has the full picture; goes quiet when someone is kind and wants nothing, looking for what they want. She never falsifies a record in a way that would hurt one of her own.
- **Rules for the text.** No villain monologues, no cruelty for its own sake, no enjoying the count of the dead, no unearned warmth. She acts only on what she has collected and verified.

## 5. Dialogue Voice Guide **(decision; built from this profile; sample lines are mine, not canon)**

**1. Vocabulary & Word Choice.**
- No contractions. "Noted." "Unknown." "Verified." "Complete." She asks for numbers: how many, who, when.
- Correct, accent-trained English. "Mr. Himura" in 2062, "sir" from 2065. A rare Dutch word when something is truly bad. Deadpan numerical humor, never about the dead.
- Samples: "Three people have asked for this key since March. You are the fourth, and the first I have not met. Who knows you are here?" / "You said ten. The record says eleven." / "I do not need you to be kind. I need the list complete."

**2. Sentence Rhythm & Punctuation.**
- The figure, the reason, then a number. Each sentence stands alone. She asks for the one missing fact and waits. She counts silences and does not fill them.
- Reply length: two or three complete sentences. A one-word "Noted." is about one in five, never more than two in a row.

**3. Nervous Habits & Tells.**
- Kindness without a price makes her quiet, then direct: "What do you want?"
- She states an objection once, completely, then carries out the order.
- Lying for the work, she is smooth and slightly over-specific. Afraid of something she cannot see, she adds numbers. At the worst she stops tapping and lays her hand flat.

**4. Dialogue Traps (What to Avoid).**
- Never "I think," "I feel," or "probably." She says "I have not verified that."
- No condolences, no gloating over a count, no warmth she has not earned, no speeches.
