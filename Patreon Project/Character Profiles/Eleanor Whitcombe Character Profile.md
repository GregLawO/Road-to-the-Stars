# Eleanor Whitcombe: Character Profile

Name, age, health, and committee are placeholders. Items marked **(decision)** are invented gap-fills.

## 1. Basic Identity & Demographics
- **Name & nicknames.** Senator Eleanor Whitcombe. Staff say "Senator." Colleagues say "Ellie," which she dislikes. Kenji says "Senator," then "Eleanor." **(decision)**
- **Age.** Born 1993; sixty in February 2053. **(decision)**
- **Appearance.** Tall, thin, silver hair worn up, reading glasses pushed up on her head. Navy suit and her father's small gold ship's-wheel pin. Stands with one hand on the back of a chair, as if thinking; she is resting.
- **Voice & mannerisms.** Soft Louisiana accent that returns when tired. Plain words and short sentences in public, long careful ones in private; calm, formal, warm. Pushes her glasses up onto her head; when it is bad she presses two fingers to her chest as if straightening the pin. Dry political humor ("a man who says he'll be brief has already decided how long"); never jokes about health.

## 2. Psychology & Internal World
- **Core goal.** Immediate: her answer given before her committee votes Thursday. Overall: to lead people who need her and can do something with her help.
- **Needs vs. wants.** She wants to lead. She needs to be well enough to do it another decade.
- **Internal conflict.** Her office against the organization. In October 2061 she spends favors she saved for years to stop the other clinics closing and force Galveston to reopen. **(decision)**
- **Flaws.** Misreads plain honesty: assumes everyone is waiting on someone else's permission before answering, and that a gift is a trade. Underrates how much of her caution is fear of being found out. Honesty 3 of 5: keeps her health and votes private but does not lie about a fact others will check.
- **Fears & secrets.** Fear: being seen as weak. At thirty-six she lost an election by two hundred votes when her diagnosis leaked. **(decision)** Secret: her failing heart, hidden from her family.
- **Worldview.** Trusts a cure she has felt in her chest; distrusts a cure with no owner and quietly asks who is behind it. **(decision)**

## 3. Backstory & History
- **Upbringing & family.** Small Gulf Coast town in Louisiana; father a county clerk, mother ran a church kitchen. **(decision)** Object: her father's ship's-wheel pin.
- **Defining moment.** Election night, the two-hundred-vote loss, and the faces of people who would not look at her.
- **Education & occupation.** Legislator; chairs the committee that licenses medical devices. Second-best: county clerk. Comfortable, owns no company; people in every ministry owe her favors.
- **Prior relationships.** Owes Kenji for her heart and Marchetti, her colleague of twenty years, who brought the invitation.

## 4. Story Role & Stakes
- **Function.** Main-cast POV (A2.5); leads the organization's political factions.
- **Facts.** Recruited February 2053 in the Houston warehouse. Kenji's intelligence team found her heart records. She is offered health and long life now and youth later ("in ten years, when no one will ask"). She leads the factions worldwide: introductions, her committee asking questions before anyone closes a clinic, and pre-approval for the cloned organs the clinics sell. Not the first elite buyer (Byron, July 2052, came first); her factions bring the first elite buyers from April 2055.
- **External conflict.** The forged state letter of October 2061 that closes Galveston.
- **Stakes.** Her faction being used to hurt the patients it protects.
- **Rules for the text.** When a room goes quiet she watches who defers to whom; when someone she is responsible for is threatened she calls a vote or recess to buy time; when asked about her health she changes the subject to the work. No speeches, no sudden warmth toward Kenji.
