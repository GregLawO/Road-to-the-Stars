Specific Rules

Rules that govern how this story's chapters, sources, secrets, technology, and casting are handled. Each rule is stated in general form, with the story's current case as the example. Story facts live in PRM-001-HIMURA-PREMISE, CHR-101-HIMURA-ANNALS, CLN-001-HIMURA-HOUSEHOLD, GEO-2122-2150-FACTIONS, REG-774-HIMURA-IMPERIAL, SEC-401-IJN-LOCK, and the core technology specifications. Style rules live in General_Writing_Rules.md.

Status labels. An entry the author states or approves is fact. (inference) is a consequence of settled facts. (open) is unsettled. (author ruling) supersedes earlier text. (decision) is my choice, open to overrule.

1. Process and format
- Order. The story is written chapter by chapter, in date order across the three historical eras (2122-2132 Pre-War, 2132-2136 World War III, 2136-2150+ Post-War and Charon Relay). A chapter that breaks date order is declared as an exception and ordered by its start date.
  - Current exceptions: (open) — declare any flashback or parallel-timeline POV segments here before drafting.
- Labels. Acts are chapters (A1, A2, A3). A POV segment is a single-POV piece coded by its chapter (A1.1, A1.2). "Excerpt" means only the quoted epigraph that opens a chapter.
- Release plan. POV segments release in batches of five, starting with the first batch of A1 (A1.1 to A1.5). A chapter has no cap on its number of batches (author ruling). Batch counts per chapter are (open).
- POV segment length (author ruling). Each POV segment runs 500 to 1,500 words. It may hold two or three dated scenes in one POV.
- Page length. Every chapter runs 2 to 4 pages.
- Layout. Byline and place line follow the previous chapter's layout.
- Excerpt limits. General Writing Rules 7 and 8 do not apply to the excerpt text. The excerpt's tone does not limit the emotion in the POV segments.
- Batch opening (author ruling). Only the first batch of each chapter opens with its excerpt. Every later batch opens with a recap of the previous batch instead. The opening sits at the very top of the batch, above any title or byline.
  - Excerpt header (first batch only). The excerpt is taken word for word from the Excerpt Bank:
    1. The excerpt's title only, as a heading, with no code and no "Excerpt" label.
    2. The excerpt text in italics inside quotation marks.
    3. An attribution line: a dash, then the work's title (Annals of the Chrysanthemum Restoration or Post-Settlement Naval Monograph, Kyoto). No author is named.
  - Recap (every later batch). The recap tells only what the previous batch's POV segments showed the reader (inference), so it reveals nothing the reader has not yet seen. Its length, voice, and format are (open).
- Tech list at the end of a batch (author ruling). Every batch that reveals a new technology to the audience lists those technologies at the very end, after the batch's last POV segment. A technology counts as new when the batch is the first to show it to the reader. The list names only technologies the batch showed (inference), so it reveals nothing the reader has not yet seen. Its format is (open).
- Story files carry story content only. Revision commentary does not belong in the outline or other story files.

2. Sources inside the story

Anything written inside the world (an excerpt, a record, a report, a letter) follows one rule set.

- A limited author (author ruling). The source is written by someone who can see only part of events. It may be incomplete or wrong, and the POV segments may complete or contradict it. Excerpts are the account of an independent scholar who cannot see what the POV segments show.
- No voice, no author. Excerpts and documents carry no first-person voice and no sign of who wrote them.
- No secrets. A source never contains a secret the story has not yet released: the clandestine Solaris-4 Crown Foundries (2122-2132), the hidden Solaris-9 core upgrades (2136+), internal Kansatsu-In / Kinki-Kyoku directives, or Emperor Himura's foreknowledge. A POV may show the truth where that POV could know it. See rule 4.

3. Plain language

A term the story has set is used exactly as set and never shortened, swapped, or turned into shorthand (Solaris-4, Solaris-7, Solaris-9, Helios-2, Helios-5, Helios-8, Quantum-Optical VI, Choju, Gin-Musha, Kikai-Bushi, Oni-Kikai, Type-90 Kage-Ya, the ending types). A place, object, or threshold that several chapters show uses identical wording in every one (such as the Sunken Bridge Kanbo, the kamidana alcove, or the crimson-and-brass dead-man key shrine).

4. Secrecy and reveal discipline

Every secret has three things on record: who may know it, the point where it is revealed, and the route by which it is learned. A chapter follows all three.

- No early knowledge, no second route. No chapter shows a character holding a secret before its reveal point or learning it by any route other than the one set. Where a secret is learned through a particular person or scene, it is not also learned from money trails, documents, or captured hands.
- Outsiders do not gesture at it. A character outside the circle of knowledge never refers to the hidden thing, even indirectly, and no document or exchange hints at it.
- A sealed mind stays sealed. A character who cannot hold a fact (or is blocked from speaking it) is never shown transmitting it, even under pressure. They may talk freely about everything else they know.
- A hidden mechanism is named only where a POV who knows of it is present.
- Private interior material stays interior. A list, count, or notebook that a character keeps in their head is shown only inside that character's POV.
- A mystery resolves only where the plan says so. No chapter shows foreign intelligence (UNAS, Eurasian-Continental Pact, Jack Harper, or Henry Lawson) knowing something the outline has not yet given it.
- Naming rules for a hidden opponent. The opponent is called by the one form the story sets and never another.
- Scope exclusions. No human meets a living alien, and the Citadel appears nowhere during the 2122-2150+ timeline (the First Contact War does not occur until 2157).

Current secrets and where they are set:

At present only Emperor Himura (born 2097) holds foreknowledge of the wider galactic threat and the Reapers, and he does not reveal its origin on the page. The knowledge is in his head, reflected only through his pragmatic policies (the 2119 Anti-Smart-AI Edict, the Solaris buildup, and the 2149 Charon Relay quarantine). No chapter, document, or character outside the inner circle may refer to an unnamed alien enemy that the fleet drills are for prior to the 2148 Mars Prothean Archives discovery.
- Jack Harper and Henry Lawson (Cerberus Precursors). Active as UNAS intelligence/mercenary operatives and corporate geneticists during World War III (2132-2136) and the 2140s, attempting to penetrate Kinki-Kyoku blackbox seals and Choju longevity protocols before Cerberus is formally established in the 2150s (GEO-2122-2150-FACTIONS Part III).

Hereditary Daimyo and Vassal Clan titles belong exclusively to Japanese noble houses on sovereign Japanese territory and Japanese space colonies. China, Korea, and Taiwan remain fully independent sovereign nations—never Imperial-administered territories—and Imperial forces intervene in neighboring affairs strictly when formally requested by their sovereign governments (GEO-2122-2150-FACTIONS Part I).

It never names the lock or the tiers, never uses the word "conditioned," and never says a carrier physically cannot betray. Sheet: SEC-401-IJN-LOCK.

5. Technology and force
- Technology acts at its tier. A lower-tier machine never does what only a higher tier can. Tiers and hard mass-shedding limits are set in PWR-001-HMR-CORE and DOC-882-IJN-DOCTRINE:
  - Pre-War (2122-2132): Solaris-4 (45% mass-shedding cap, 99.5% inertial dampening, 18 LY/day Sequential Spatial-Fold FTL, no tactical micro-jumps).
  - World War III (2132-2136): Mid-war Solaris-7 transition (60% mass-shedding cap, seamless External Shear Barriers, Single-Fold Tactical Micro-Jumps).
  - Post-War (2136-2150+): Solaris-9 (75% hard limit, 25% minimum apparent mass).
- What leaves the private sphere is the lower grade. Only licensed, Kinki-Kyoku-blackboxed Helios-2 (25% mass-shedding), Helios-5 (35%), and Helios-8 (50%) reactors may be exported to foreign powers (UNAS, EU, Systems Alliance, and sovereign neighbors) after 2136 and 2149. Domestic Solaris-9 cores, Choju longevity protocols, and Singularity Cannons never leave sovereign Imperial custody (Hearth-Quench and Hearth-Collapse protocols prevent capture).
- Capabilities appear in the order the timeline sets. Example: Single-Fold Tactical Micro-Jumps do not appear on 2122-2132 Solaris-4 warships; 250m Close-Support / Hospital Corvettes (IJN-TNU-LE(S)) operate only in atmosphere after orbital superiority is secured; the 1,500-ship fleet cap holds fixed from 2136 onward.
- Automated guards hold posts (author ruling). Shipboard Ashigaru-4 and garrison Kikai-Bushi / Oni-Kikai automata do not patrol casually or idle. They hold assigned posts (corridor alcoves, reactor sanctum blast doors, Crown Foundries, Proscribed Vaults) in complete silence with dimmed ember optical slits. They move only to take a post, execute an authorized command, or answer a security trigger, and make noise only when they move. They stand inside sovereign Imperial sites and vessels only.
- Emperor Himura never doubts his technology (author ruling). He and his inner circle (High Censor Kurosawa, Kinki-Kyoku chief engineers) have full working mastery of every Solaris, Quantum-Optical VI, and Choju system they deploy. They know a lattice works before they switch it on, and never hope, wonder, or hedge about the physics. Never write Emperor Himura unsure of his own machines or his hands acting without him. His risks come from other people: foreign espionage, human betrayal, political escalation, and the physical limits of human crews inside high-G hulls.
- Where the specifics live. Fleet hull counts and ratios (1:2:4 battle lines, 1:2 wolfpacks), ground legion counts (25,000,000 troops), exosuit/under-suit specs, and warship deck layouts live in REG-774-HIMURA-IMPERIAL, DOC-882-IJN-DOCTRINE, DOC-885-IJN-GROUND, TEC-910, TEC-915, TEC-920, and the five SCH- warship files.

6. Casting rules
- POV eligibility. A file character carries POV only from age 8, only while alive, and only for what they could see or know.
- Per-character casting. Each character's POV chapters, and whether they carry POV at all, are tracked in the master character roster (GEO-2122-2150-FACTIONS) and chapter outlines.

7. Names for a group and its ranks
- A group has one register, applied to speech and narration. Words the group avoids are out of members' mouths and out of the narration. Rank and file, and the unit's own name, are set once and used consistently.
  - Current case (author ruling): No Imperial Starfleet officer uses "AI" for shipboard systems—they are strictly "VIs" or "Quantum-Optical VIs" (enforcing the 2119 Anti-Smart-AI Edict). Compliance Directorate field officers are "Audit-Marshals" (Kansatsu-Kan). Automated soldiers are "Ashigaru" or "Kikai-Bushi" (never "droids" or "robots"). Escort wolfpacks are "divisions" or "packs," and ground formations are "Legions" or "Divisions."
- Imperial naval and combat vocabulary. The author's reference list of Imperial combat, bridge, and protocol labels lives in DOC-882-IJN-DOCTRINE, DOC-885-IJN-GROUND, INT-404-IJN-PREWAR, and REG-774-HIMURA-IMPERIAL.
