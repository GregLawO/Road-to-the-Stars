A2.1

**Emperor Kenji Himura**  
**Kyoto-Heian Imperial Citadel, Kyoto — Autumn 2132**

Warm morning sunlight slanted through the cedar lattice screens of the private dining gallery, catching the thin curls of steam rising from two porcelain bowls of red-miso soup and a glazed platter of salt-broiled mackerel. Emperor Kenji Himura sat cross-legged on a woven rush mat at the low keyaki-wood table in a plain dark cotton tunic, lifting his stoneware teacup with both calloused hands while Empress Kaguya reviewed a neat stack of provincial agricultural yield ledgers beside him.

"The northern terraced farms in Nagano came in twelve percent above autumn quota," Kaguya said, her voice calm, warm, and unhurried as she marked a column with a fine-tipped sumi brush. "Even with the subterranean foundry expansions beneath the Kii mountains and the new rail spurs feeding Sasebo, the imperial granaries will stay full through next spring without touching emergency reserves."

"Good," Kenji said, setting his teacup down and picking up his lacquered chopsticks. He broke off a generous piece of the broiled mackerel, savoring the crisp, salted skin before glancing out through the open shoji panel toward the inner courtyard maples, whose leaves had just begun to turn deep crimson along the stone water basin. "Remind me to send three casks of good sake and a crate of dried persimmons down to Foreman Sato's shift at Kii-Yamato tonight. Those foundry crews have been pulling double turns since August without a single complaint."

"I already added them to the afternoon supply train," Kaguya replied with a faint, knowing smile, not looking up from her ledger. "Along with extra wool blankets for the night welders."

"You're three steps ahead of me as usual," Kenji said, reaching for his rice bowl.

Before Kaguya could answer, the heavy sliding cedar door at the far end of the gallery parted on silent brass rollers. High Censor Renji Kurosawa stepped across the threshold, his dark wool coat buttoned cleanly to the throat and a sealed crimson dispatch cylinder held flat against his left forearm. Behind him in the shadowed corridor, two Ashigaru-4 automata stood motionless beside the screen posts, their tarnished-bronze armor plates dull in the morning light and their horizontal amber optical slits dimmed to a low, steady ember.

Kenji did not drop his chopsticks or tense his shoulders. He finished chewing, swallowed calmly, and set his chopsticks neatly across their ceramic rest. "That looks like an early morning, Renji. Sit down, pour yourself some tea, and tell me who finally blinked."

"Both of them, Your Majesty," Kurosawa said, kneeling briefly to lay the crimson cylinder on the table before taking a seat at the edge of the tatami mat. "At zero-four-ten hours, Eurasian Pact heavy amphibious task forces crossed the median line in the Taiwan Strait under cover of an autumn squall while three mechanized shock divisions opened rocket artillery fire across the Korean Demilitarized Zone. Six minutes later, NORAD-Orbital Command at Cheyenne Mountain broke radio silence and launched forty-eight unmanned kinetic bombardment sleds and six nuclear-thermal cruisers out of Lagrange One toward Japanese high orbit."

Kaguya's brush paused half an inch above her paper ledger, her dark eyes steady and clear as she looked across the low table at her husband.

Kenji let out a short, quiet breath through his nose and rested one forearm comfortably along the table edge. "Well," he said, his voice level and entirely free of surprise or drama. "At least they saved us the trouble of guessing which week they'd move. Did Taipei and Seoul's coastal grids hold the first wave?"

"Taiwanese cutters running Helios-2 cores sank four Pact amphibious transports off Penghu in the first twenty minutes," Kurosawa replied, consulting a slim photonic slate from his coat pocket as golden-amber kanji scrolled softly across the glass. "General Kang's mobile railgun batteries along the Imjin River stopped the northern armored vanguard cold before sunrise. However, Beijing has committed two high-altitude bomber wings and three nuclear-thermal orbital destroyers out of Jiuquan to suppress Kaohsiung from above, and the UNAS cruiser group is ninety minutes out from geostationary insertion over Honshu."

Kenji nodded once, tapping one knuckle twice against the keyaki wood as the tactical picture locked cleanly into place in his mind. "They think we're isolated, strapped for steel, and still fighting with twentieth-century surface hulls. Let's not disappoint them too fast."

He looked over at Kaguya, his expression softening into easy, unforced warmth. "Sorry to cut breakfast short. Looks like I'm spending the morning in the command pit instead of the machine shop."

"Go," Kaguya said softly, reaching across the table to squeeze his wrist once before setting her brush cleanly beside the inkstone. "Keep our captains steady, and come back before dinner gets cold."

Kenji stood up, adjusting the cuffs of his dark cotton tunic with unhurried assurance, and turned to Kurosawa as they stepped out into the cedar corridor. "Signal Fleet Admiral Togo on *Amaterasu-no-Yaiba* to lift from the Mare Moscoviense trench with First Battle Squadron, and tell Captain Takeda's Shadow cruisers to drop Eclipse stealth only when the UNAS vanguard enters gunnery range. Nobody fires a Singularity Cannon—keep the war strictly inside Solaris-4 parameters, target propulsion bells and weapon mounts only, and let them see what our steel can actually do."

Inside the darkened bridge, the only sound was the steady hiss of rain against the armored glass and the soft hum of the cooling vents. Six months earlier, Li had commanded a conventional diesel-electric patrol boat that burned thirty tons of fuel every sprint and lit up mainland thermal scopes from sixty miles away. "Forward electromagnetic railgun turret charged to one hundred percent," the gunnery officer reported from his station, checking the green capacitor bars on his screen. "Passive optical lock established on *Kunlun Shan*'s port waterline."

"Fire a three-round armor-piercing salvo, then break hard starboard at full twenty-five percent sprint," Li commanded. "Keep our bow angled into the chop."

The forward deck shook with a sharp, dry metallic crack as the twin electromagnetic railguns punched three fin-stabilized tungsten penetrators across fourteen kilometers at Mach seven. There was no muzzle flash to give away their position—only a brief blue static discharge along the barrel rails that vanished instantly in the driving rain.

Four seconds later, bright orange fire bloomed low along the massive amphibious transport's armored waterline, followed almost immediately by a secondary fuel-bunker explosion that sent a thick column of black smoke boiling upward into the low cloud deck.

"Direct hits amidships!" the optical lookout shouted from the starboard periscope. "She's listing twenty degrees to port and losing headway fast! Her escorting destroyers are turning hard to port to lay smoke!"

Before the two Type-058 destroyers could slew their heavy vertical-launch missile cells toward the coastal shoals, *Cheng Kung* banked sharply inside her own wake, skimming silently out of the storm squall at fifty-five knots with zero infrared exhaust plume for their targeting seekers to lock onto. Two salvos of Pact anti-ship missiles streaked blindly into the empty rain squall behind them, splashing harmlessly into the gray swells.

"Enemy drone screen is breaking formation and climbing to search altitude," the sensor operator reported, adjusting his passive optical filters. "No radar lock on our hull. They have no idea where the shots came from."

Li exhaled slowly, wiping cold salt spray from his chin with the back of his sleeve. Outnumbered ten to one in hulls and tonnage across the Strait, Taipei's small flotilla of gravitic cutters was turning the water between Fujian and Penghu into a graveyard for Beijing's invasion fleet—buying every precious hour until Kyoto's orbital hammer fell from above.
