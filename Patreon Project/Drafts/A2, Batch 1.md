### The Autumn Opening

"*When the Eurasian-Continental Pact launched its coordinated kinetic and missile volleys on the fourteenth of October, 2132, strategic planners in Moscow and Beijing anticipated a swift collapse of East Asian orbital pickets within seventy-two hours. Conventional doctrine held that high-altitude saturation strikes must inevitably overwhelm localized defenses. What the attacking task forces encountered instead over the Pacific was a complete inversion of terrestrial ballistics: heavily armored void wedges maneuvering without reaction thrusters, directional energy barrier planes that deflected relativistic slugs, and standoff torpedo salvos launched from deep shadow without thermal signatures.*"

— *Post-Settlement Naval Monograph, Kyoto*

**A2.1. Audit-Marshal Sano Mitsuko**   *2132 — Yokohama Harbor, Honshu*
The cold salt wind off Tokyo Bay carried the sharp stench of diesel exhaust, wet concrete, and sea-foam as Audit-Marshal Sano Mitsuko walked down the damp timber catwalk of Pier Nine. She maintained a precise half-pace behind High Censor Kurosawa Renji, her right hand resting near the holster of her sidearm. Ahead of them, six Ashigaru-4 sentinels moved in absolute, silent synchronization, their heavy gauntlets carrying magnetic breach clamps and their crimson optical slits dimmed to narrow threads of ember light against the heavy fog.

Yokohama’s commercial shipping basin had been placed under Directorate quarantine at midnight. Across the dark water, commercial container ships swayed against their mooring lines under the glare of yellow harbor floodlights. Kurosawa led his detail past rows of rusted shipping containers directly toward a corrugated iron repair bay stamped with the faded logo of a regional logistics contractor.

"The port registry listed this facility as an idle maintenance yard for coastal barges," Kurosawa said, his voice flat, low, and unhurried. He did not turn his head as he walked, his dark woolen cloak pinned with the silver chrysanthemum badge of the Kansatsu-In. "Yet the electrical grid draw over the last forty-eight hours matches the power consumption of a high-altitude transmission array."

Sano glanced up at the iron roof trusses, where wet sea fog hung in heavy drops from rusted girders. "The local port authority approved their power permit three weeks ago, Kurosawa-sama. The signature came from the regional registrar's office in Nagoya."

"The signature was forged," Kurosawa replied simply. "The Nagoya registrar died two months ago."

He raised his right hand, making a brief gesture with two fingers. The lead Ashigaru-4 sentinel stepped forward without a sound, placed its gauntleted palm flat against the iron sliding door, and triggered a magnetic pulse. The heavy locking pins inside the track sheared through with a sudden, violent metallic clack.

The iron door slammed open, rattling along its guide rails. Inside the damp bay, eight men in dark operational jump-suits stood around a long steel assembly table. In the center of the table rested a hardened satellite data terminal, its fiber-optic cables running up toward an illegal laser transceiver mounted on the roof. Three armed operatives turned immediately, their hands diving inside their coats for suppressed submachine guns.

"Compliance Directorate! Drop your weapons!" Sano shouted, stepping laterally to clear Kurosawa’s line of fire as she drew her weapon.

The lead operative raised his weapon and fired a four-round burst. High-velocity kinetic slugs cracked into the wooden doorframe behind Sano, spraying sharp splinters across her cheek and uniform collar.

The six Ashigaru-4 sentinels surged forward in a silent, coordinated rush of hydraulic steel. The nearest sentinel closed four meters in less than a second, its gauntlet striking the lead shooter's chest with a hollow thud that shattered his ribs and pinned him flat against the concrete floor. A second operative raised his rifle, but the sentinel beside him grabbed the barrel with its left hand, twisted the steel receiver until the metal snapped, and swept the man's legs out with a low sweep.

Sano lunged past the falling guards, grabbed the third operative by his collar, and slammed his face against the edge of the steel assembly table. She twisted his wrist behind his back, forcing his arm up until the joint popped, and snapped heavy steel cuffs across his wrists.

"All personnel flat on the deck!" Sano barked, her fingers gripping the cold steel cuffs tightly as her boots braced against the gunman's thrashing legs.

The remaining technicians dropped their slates and raised their hands in immediate surrender, their faces pale under the flickering overhead lamps. The Ashigaru-4 sentinels instantly resumed static guard posts around the bay, their optical slits dimming back to quiet crimson threads as the space fell silent.

Kurosawa walked unhurriedly to the central steel table. He ignored the groaning operatives on the floor and picked up the active data slate connected to the roof transceiver. He tapped the optical display, pulling up the raw transmission logs.

Sano wiped a streak of damp sea-spray from her forehead and stepped up to the table, her breath coming in steady, measured draws. Over Kurosawa's shoulder, she scanned the top line of the glowing ledger.

"The clearance seal on this signal stream," Sano said, her eyes narrowing as she recognized the digital cryptographic pattern. "It matches the forged customs seal we seized in Hakata last year, Kurosawa-sama. The key rotation uses the exact same delayed timestamp structure."

Kurosawa looked down at the display without moving a muscle. "It does. The origin node is identical."

He tapped the slate’s secondary memory bank, unlocking an encrypted file folder that had been queued for transmission to an orbital receiver over the Western Pacific. The screen flashed amber as the Directorate decryption cipher scrubbed the headers, revealing a master military launch schedule stamped with the authorization codes of the Eurasian-Continental Pact.

Sano felt her pulse quicken as she read the top line of the document. "A coordinated strike schedule... set for October fourteenth at zero-four-hundred hours."

Kurosawa’s jaw clamped shut with sudden, rigid tightness. His vocal cords locked, and his left hand rose instinctively, his fingers pressing flat against his breastbone over his dark wool tunic as his chest went rigid for three long seconds.

Sano watched his face in quiet tension, recognizing the physical tell. She stood silent, waiting as the High Censor's breathing gradually smoothed out and his hand returned to his side.

Kurosawa looked at the timestamp on the slate, which showed less than forty-eight hours remaining before the planned strike. He turned his eyes toward Sano, looking at her with cold, absolute clarity.

"This launch schedule was authorized using an internal clearance cipher from the Naval General Staff in Kyoto," Kurosawa said, his voice dropping into a razor-thin tone. He opened his mouth as if to name the senior officer whose digital seal had stamped the document. Then he looked at the captured operatives being held on the floor by the sentinels.

"Kurosawa-sama?" Sano asked softly, holding her breath.

Kurosawa closed the slate and placed it inside his inner cloak pocket. "We lift for Kyoto immediately. Heika must hear this before the sun sets." 

**A2.2. Admiral Boris Mikhailovich**   *2132 — Baikonur-Orbital Station, High Orbit*
The magnetic catwalk of Assembly Bay Four vibrated under the soles of Admiral Boris Mikhailovich’s heavy leather boots. Two hundred kilometers above the steppes of Kazakhstan, the vast, unlit cavern of the Baikonur orbital shipyards stretched through the void like a ribcage of structural steel. Down below the catwalk, the dark gunmetal hull of a five-hundred-meter Pact siege cruiser rested inside its docking cradle, surrounded by hundreds of glowing plasma torches held by zero-gravity armorers.

Mikhailovich stopped beside Frame Two-Hundred, reached out with his gloved thumb, and pressed his palm against a fresh hull weld. The high-tensile steel seam was still warm through his leather glove, radiating heat into the freezing air of the unpressurized gantry.

"The bead is three millimeters wider than specification along the keel," Mikhailovich said, his voice deep, gravelly, and flat. He did not look up at the yard chief floating beside him in a maneuvering harness. "If this hull takes a lateral gravimetric pulse from a Japanese line cruiser, this seam tears open like wet paper."

The yard chief adjusted his tether nervously, his breath fogging inside his helmet visor. "The shipyard director ordered the welding shifts doubled yesterday, Admiral. We were given forty-eight hours to clear the docks before the launch window opens. The foundries in Magnitogorsk are pushing steel plates without secondary heat treatment."

"Speed does not compensate for poor metallurgy, Comrade," Mikhailovich replied bluntly, his voice showing zero sympathy. "If a ship breaks at the seam before it fires its salvo, the hurry was useless. Tell the shift foreman to grind the heel smooth and re-pass the weld before the cradle releases."

He turned and walked down the gantry toward the pressurized transit tube, his heavy steps clacking against the steel grate. Floating yard workers scrambled out of his path, pulling their tool trays tight against their chests. Mikhailovich glanced out the viewing port at the adjacent assembly docks, where six cargo freighters were being retrofitted with massive, dish-shaped microwave emitters and passive radar reflectors. He noted the crude mounting brackets with a quiet scowl, knowing that under close optical scrutiny, the decoys would not hold up for more than ten minutes.

He stepped inside the airlock chamber, holding the handrail as the pressurization cycle hissed. Beside him, his junior aide wiped sweat from his forehead with a sleeve. "The Chinese delegation is waiting in the tactical theater, Admiral. General Lin arrived from Beijing two hours ago with the final missile allocation numbers."

"Let them wait," Mikhailovich muttered, unbuttoning his high collar. "A plan built on hasty numbers is worse than no plan at all."

An hour later, Mikhailovich stood at the head of the long steel briefing table inside Baikonur’s central command bunker. Before him sat thirty senior flag officers from the Russian Void Fleet, the Chinese People's Fleet, and the Korean People's Army, their heavy wool uniforms decorated with campaign ribbons and golden state badges. At the back of the room, standing silently beside the steel viewport rail, sat Senior Liaison General Lin of the Beijing Military Directorate.

Mikhailovich activated the primary strategic projection with a thumb press on his slate. A three-dimensional holographic map of the Western Pacific popped into light above the table, displaying the orbital trajectories of the Japanese home island defense pickets.

"The Japanese Imperial Starfleet holds forty-two warships in high orbit between Tokyo and the lunar limb," Mikhailovich said, laying both palms flat on the table edge. "Their primary line relies on four-hundred-and-fifty-meter reactionless hulls equipped with directional energy barriers. If we engage their battle line directly with conventional salvo tactics, their gravitic agility allows them to defeat our kinetic salvos before our missiles clear the upper atmosphere."

A Russian fleet captain leaned forward from his chair, his hands resting on his knees. "Then how do we breach their orbital barrier plane, Admiral?"

Mikhailovich tapped a key on his console. The holographic plot shifted, revealing two distinct clusters of radar icons advancing toward Earth.

"We do not attempt to breach their line with armor," Mikhailovich said, his voice cold and analytical. "We launch four hundred automated cargo freighters from Baikonur and Tianjin twelve hours before the main assault. Each freighter carries a high-power radar reflector and a dummy thermal emitter that matches the gravimetric signature of a seven-hundred-and-fifty-meter interceptor cruiser."

He leaned over the table, pointing to the secondary vector lines glowing in green light. "While their young picket captains deploy their barrier planes to intercept what they believe is a four-hundred-ship battle line, our actual kinetic strike platforms advance behind the decoy cloud at low sub-light velocity under complete radar silence. By the time their fire-control VIs distinguish between a steel decoy and a live kinetic rod carrier, our salvos will already be inside their upper atmospheric re-entry corridor."

The room remained silent for several seconds as the officers studied the trajectory calculations. The plan was elegant, ruthless, and entirely reliant on operational timing.

"And their capital fleet under Fleet Admiral Togo?" a Chinese commodore asked from the middle row. "What do you expect the Japanese flagship to do when the decoy line breaks?"

Mikhailovich straightened his tall frame and clasped his hands behind his back. He looked past the table, meeting the sharp, calculating eyes of General Lin at the back of the room.

"Fleet Admiral Togo is a master of spatial positioning," Mikhailovich said plainly, his face showing zero emotion. "He will not panic, and he will not move his flagship until he sees our primary vector. But his junior captains have never fought a live war. When four hundred warship signatures appear on their displays simultaneously, hesitation is inevitable."

A staff officer raised his slate from the corner desk. "The Kremlin and the Central Military Commission have both transmitted final launch authorization, Admiral. The DPRK vanguard has already fueled its booster stages along the DMZ."

The staff officer looked up from his glowing screen. "What do you expect their pickets to do when our first wave clears the atmosphere tomorrow morning, Admiral?"

Mikhailovich turned his head toward the reinforced viewport, looking out at the dark curve of Earth below.

"We will see tomorrow," Mikhailovich said softly. 

**A2.3. Fleet Admiral Togo Kazuhiko**   *2132 — Sunken Bridge of IJN Amaterasu-no-Yaiba, High Orbit*
Inside the quiet, copper-lined command citadel of the 1,000-meter Flagship *IJN Amaterasu-no-Yaiba*, Fleet Admiral Togo Kazuhiko stood on the elevated tactical dais with his hands clasped firmly behind his back. Above his head, the central golden-amber tactical globe rotated slowly, displaying forty-two Imperial warships holding station across three orbital sectors above Tokyo, Kyoto, and the Sea of Japan.

The bridge was completely silent except for the low sub-audible thrum of the Solaris-4D flagship core operating beneath the woven tatami deck mats. Down in the sunken helm pit, six flight officers and sensor analysts sat in magnetic crash harnesses, their screens displaying real-time gravimetric sweeps across the Pacific Rim. On the forward observation screen, the dark silhouette of the Moon hung motionless against the starry backdrop, its limb traced by the faint sun-glint of distant Imperial picket escorts.

Suddenly, a deep, resonant bronze temple-bell tone chimed from the primary fire-control terminal.

"Pact launch confirmed across all sectors," the sensor chief reported from his sunken console, his voice steady despite the rapid red telemetry lines spreading across the globe. "Four hundred and twenty contacts lifting from Baikonur, Tianjin, and the Korean peninsula. Velocity is Mach fourteen and accelerating."

Togo did not move. His weathered face remained set like carved granite under the amber glow of the displays. "Confirm target mass classifications."

"Sensors report four hundred heavy warship signatures," the sensor chief replied, his fingers flying across his glass slate. "Mass signatures match line cruisers and heavy strike platforms."

"That signature is artificial," Togo said, his gravelly voice calm and unhurried. "No continental foundry has the raw steel capacity to put four hundred line cruisers into orbit simultaneously. It is a decoy wave. Maintain barrier standby and hold main battery fire until the second plane clears."

Down on the lower helm pit, the tactical coordinator hesitated, his hand hovering over the fleet broadcast control. "Togo-sama, Division Two is requesting permission to open fire. Their picket corvettes are closing on the lead contact cluster over the Marianas line."

"Tell Division Two to hold their station," Togo commanded sharply. "Objective: preserve barrier energy. Constraint: do not engage until target mass is verified."

Before his order could be retransmitted across the network, three junior corvette captains in Division Two panicked. Seeing hundreds of incoming radar tracks diving toward the upper atmosphere, they broke formation and fired their forward railgun batteries into the decoy cluster.

The broadside lanced through space. The leading contacts shattered into cloud clusters of aluminum foil and passive radar reflectors.

Milliseconds later, behind the expanding decoy cloud, forty dark, unlit Pact kinetic-rod carriers slipped out of the sensor shadow. They unleashed eighty heavy depleted-uranium penetrators directly at Division Two's open flanks.

"Second wave confirmed live!" the tactical chief shouted. "Kinetic impactors inbound at Mach twenty-eight!"

"Hard port trim! Engage faceted barrier planes!" Togo ordered.

The *Amaterasu-no-Yaiba* turned its chisel prow forty degrees to port. On a Solaris-4 hull, the axial spinal Macrocannon could not fire without turning the entire 1,000-meter ship to align the 800-meter barrel with the target vector. The mass-shedding lattice engaged at forty-five percent, and the 99.5 percent inertial dampeners pulled hard against Togo’s crash harness straps as the flagship completed its turn.

Five meters off the flagship's starboard bow, a shimmering golden-amber External Shear Barrier lanced into existence. Three kinetic rods struck the energy plane, their 100-ton masses sliding off the sloped barrier and dissolving into white-hot showers of molten sparks.

"Line Cruiser *IJN-DNK-LC-02* reports heavy armor scarring along Frame Three-Hundred!" the comms officer called out. "Corvette *IJN-DNK-LE-09* has taken a direct kinetic hit to her starboard engineering bay!"

On the tactical globe, two green icons flickered and dimmed as the Pact’s second wave lanced past the picket line, scorching the outer armor plates of two Imperial escorts. Togo watched the telemetry feeds calmly, noting how the unexpected nuclear flak bursts from the enemy rear guard created secondary radiation interference across their forward sensor grids.

He glanced down at the secondary diagnostic slate on his armrest. The flagship’s Solaris-4D core was maintaining baseline energy output, its mass-shedding lattice operating cleanly at forty-five percent. But the tactical globe showed that Division Two’s unauthorized movement had opened a five-hundred-kilometer gap between the central battle line and the eastern orbital picket.

"Line Cruiser *IJN-DNK-LC-04* is moving to cover the gap," the helm officer reported. "Captain Ryunosuke has brought his ship into firing position."

Togo stood motionless on the dais, his hands clasped so tightly behind his back that his knuckles turned white against his gloves. He looked at the red casualty lines on his screen, his jaw set in cold, unyielding discipline. Beside him, the junior flag lieutenant lowered his head slightly, staring at the flashing red hull alerts from *IJN-DNK-LE-09*.

"The damage to Division Two was self-inflicted, Admiral," the lieutenant muttered, his voice tight. "If Captain Oka had held his picket line as ordered, the Pact strike would have lanced harmlessly into empty space."

Togo did not turn his head, but his gravelly voice cut through the young officer's words with absolute authority. "Division Two failed to anticipate the secondary vector because our training drills emphasized linear surface threats instead of three-dimensional orbital envelopes. The fault lies with our operational doctrine, Lieutenant, not the courage of those crews. We do not condemn men for failing a test we did not prepare them to pass."

He stepped down from the dais onto the lower helm pit, his magnetic boots clicking softly against the deck mats. His eyes swept across the remaining thirty-nine green warship icons on the golden-amber globe, calculating spatial vectors with instant, practiced clarity. He reached out and touched the primary tactical slate with his right hand, adjusting the fleet's orbital inclination angles to close the gap left by the damaged escorts.

"All ships, adjust inclination by fifteen degrees dorsal," Togo commanded, his voice reaching every station on the bridge. "Close up the wolfpacks, align spinal batteries on Sector Four, and re-form the line." 

**A2.4. Lt. Aoki Shun**   *2132 — 250m Corvette IJN-DNK-LE, High Orbit*
Inside the narrow, instrument-packed bridge of the 250-meter Corvette *IJN-DNK-LE*, Lieutenant Aoki Shun stood strapped into his magnetic crash harness, staring at the thermal-sink gauge mounted on his console. The digital LED bar glowed dark amber, showing two hundred and ten minutes of capacity remaining on their solid-state Eclipse sinks. The hum of the vessel's internal life-support systems was low and steady, punctuated only by the soft click of relay switches as the fire-control computer calculated silent approach vectors in the dark.

Outside their sloped prow, the corvette was locked in a pre-war 1:4 wolfpack formation—four corvettes trailing three hundred meters behind a 500-meter frigate leader. The five ships glided silently through deep shadow, their hulls dark and their engines running on internal battery power.

"CPF dreadnought group identified at sector three-zero-nine," the helm officer—Nakamura-san, the same young officer Aoki had corrected during the lunar shadow drills—reported in a hushed whisper. "Distance: fourteen thousand kilometers. They are deploying two surface landing ships under escort."

"Hold thermal stealth," Aoki said, his voice tight and steady. "We wait until the frigate opens bow shutters."

Ahead of them, the lead frigate’s bow shutters parted for three milliseconds. Twelve four-meter Type-90C Kage-Ya gravimetric torpedoes lanced into the void without thermal plumes or exhaust fire, inheriting the wolfpack's sub-light momentum. Aoki's corvette and the three escorting corvettes ripple-fired simultaneously, their bow shutters opening and closing in rapid three-millisecond intervals to saturate the target vector with thirty-six dark torpedoes.

The thirty-six Type-90C torpedoes glided silently across fourteen thousand kilometers of void under complete Eclipse stealth, their mass-shedding lattices shedding mass to forty-five percent while their solid-state gravitic engines pulled them steadily toward the Chinese dreadnought group without giving off a single heat signature.

Suddenly, the lead Chinese dreadnought’s thermal sensors picked up the faint gravimetric distortion of the incoming salvo.

"Hostile defensive salvo!" Aoki's tactical officer yelled. "The enemy is detonating nuclear proximity flak!"

Three kilometers ahead of the Chinese dreadnought, two heavy defensive missiles exploded in blinding white flashes of atomic fire. Mega-joules of thermal radiation and millions of high-velocity radioactive shrapnel fragments expanded across the torpedoes' approach corridor.

The nuclear shockwave slammed into the incoming salvo. Twelve Type-90C torpedoes were vaporized instantly by the direct thermal blast, while twenty more had their forward gravitic sensors blinded by the dense ionization cloud.

"Salvo disrupted!" the tactical officer shouted. "The shockwave is washing back toward our position!"

The thermal gauge on Aoki’s console flashed bright red. The sudden burst of intense nuclear radiation from the nuclear flak hit the corvette's hull, causing their solid-state Eclipse thermal sinks to absorb heat at four times their normal rate. The digital timer plummeted from two hundred minutes straight down to twelve minutes.

"Thermal sinks redlining!" Nakamura-san panicked, his hands trembling over the yoke. "We have twelve minutes before reactor heat cooks the bridge!"

Aoki grabbed his harness buckle with one hand, his thumb slamming down on the manual purge control. "Do not dump heat into space! If we vent thermal exhaust now, their optical gunners will railgun us out of the sky!"

He leaned over the console, looking at Nakamura-san's wide, frightened eyes. "Execute seventy-degree dorsal dive! Use Earth's upper atmosphere as a thermal shield! Full throttle on gravitic lattice!"

Nakamura-san hesitated for a fraction of a second, then pulled the yoke back hard.

The corvette snapped seventy degrees upward, its Solaris-4 core screaming at maximum output. The 99.5 percent inertial dampening system strained under the violent one-hundred-and-ninety-G turn, pinning Aoki against his harness straps with crushing gravitational force.

Outside the viewports, the dark curve of Earth surged toward them as the five ships of the wolfpack dived into the upper atmosphere. The hull plating glowed bright orange as re-entry friction washed over the sloped prow, masking their reactor heat signature behind a wall of natural atmospheric plasma.

Inside the cramped bridge, the heat rose rapidly as the thermal sinks struggled against the atmospheric friction. Aoki checked the hull integrity displays, watching the green status indicators for the forward shear plates.

"Atmospheric friction is holding at forty percent," the tactical officer reported, wiping sweat from his eyes. "Pact optical tracking is blind! They have lost our trajectory!"

Outside the thick quartz viewports, the orange re-entry plasma began to thin as the corvette climbed out of the upper stratosphere into the dark vacuum of low orbit. Below them, the vast, blue expanse of the Pacific Ocean gleamed through swirling cloud cover, completely oblivious to the high-G void battle raging seventy kilometers above.

Aoki wiped a trickle of sweat from his chin, his eyes locked on the thermal sink indicator. The timer had stopped its dangerous descent, hovering at three minutes of emergency capacity. The atmospheric re-entry dive had successfully bled off seventy percent of their accumulated reactor heat without exposing their position to distant Pact optical tracking.

"Thermal sinks returning to safe threshold," the tactical officer reported, his breath coming in ragged gasps as the high-G pressure eased off his chest. "Reactor temperature is dropping. We have cleared the primary flak zone."

Nakamura-san exhaled deeply, his hands slowly relaxing their white-knuckled grip on the flight yoke as the secondary attitude thrusters fired brief stabilization pulses. He looked over at Aoki, his eyes showing a mixture of relief and deep respect. "We made it, Aoki-san. The frigate and the other three corvettes are matching our climb vector. Thermal sinks are back in the green."

Aoki held his harness buckle tightly, his teeth clenched as the last residual G-forces eased off his body. He straightened his flight tunic and nodded once toward the young helm officer.

"Good trim, Nakamura-kun," Aoki gasped through the pressure, checking the primary sensor slate one last time. "Hold vector... break away now!"

The wolfpack lanced out of the upper cloud layer, breaking clear of the kill-zone into high orbit as the distant Chinese nuclear flak flashes faded into dark, harmless telemetry points on their rear radar displays. 

**A2.5. Cmdr. Chen Yu-ting**   *2132 — Taiwan Strait, High Orbit*
In the humid, sweat-stained air of the Taiwanese Destroyer *ROCS Tang*, Commander Chen Yu-ting stood behind the primary navigation console, her dark eyes fixed on the tactical display screen. Taped directly beside the glass monitor was her hand-drawn paper grid map from A1.8, its pencil lines smudged by months of sea-spray and thumb marks. The bridge smelled of ozone and hot electrical insulation, vibrating under the constant thrum of their Helios-2 reactor core.

Outside the armored bridge, the narrow waters of the Taiwan Strait were torn by high-altitude missile contrails and artillery splashes. A squadron of six Chinese People's Fleet landing craft had broken through the outer picket line and were diving toward the northern beaches of Formosa.

"The landing craft are accelerating!" her tactical chief reported, his voice high with tension. "They are clearing our surface missile envelope!"

Chen looked at her hand-drawn grid. According to the Imperial Navy liaison's rules, her ship was mandated to hold fixed picket coordinates to preserve mutual defense coverage. But as she watched the enemy landing craft dive toward the coast, her discipline snapped.

"Break formation!" Chen commanded, her voice ringing sharp across the bridge. "All engines full ahead! We intercept those transports before they hit the beach!"

The destroyer surged forward, leaving its assigned sector and diving into the narrow coastal channel.

Immediately, a series of red warning beacons flashed across her secondary display.

"Enemy coastal railgun array at Fuzhou has locked on our hull!" the tactical chief screamed. "We have entered a pre-targeted kill-zone!"

Before Chen could order an evasive turn, three hyper-velocity kinetic slugs lanced across the strait from the continental shore at Mach thirty. The heavy 500-millimeter depleted-uranium penetrators tore through the ocean mist, aimed directly at the *Tang's* unarmored port flank.

Chen froze, her hands gripping the console edge as she realized her fatal tactical error. She had abandoned the grid line, walking her crew directly into a pre-sighted trap.

Suddenly, a massive dark shadow lanced down from high orbit.

A 750-meter Imperial Interceptor Cruiser (*IJN-DNK-SP*) executed a high-G inclination dive, dropping directly between the Taiwanese destroyer and the incoming railgun salvo.

"Faceted barrier active!" the Imperial warship's VI chime echoed over the open channel.

Five meters off the Imperial cruiser's port hull, a massive golden-amber External Shear Barrier lanced into existence. The three heavy railgun slugs slammed into the energy plane. The kinetic impactors dissolved into blinding bursts of white-hot plasma, their relativistic energy sliding off the sloped barrier plane.

The secondary shockwave slammed into the *Tang*, throwing Chen against the steel bulkhead. Sparks rained down from the ceiling conduits, and the bridge lights flickered before switching to dim battery backups.

When Chen hauled herself back to her feet, she looked out the starboard viewport. The 750-meter Imperial cruiser hung thirty meters off her beam. The massive kinetic impact had scorched the cruiser's forward hull plates, tearing away two secondary turret housings and leaving deep, glowing red scars along its high-tensile steel armor.

The Imperial warship had taken heavy structural damage to save her crippled ship.

Around her, the bridge crew began clearing shattered glass and unseating jammed terminal keys. The tactical chief stumbled back to his seat, wiping a line of blood from a cut above his brow where a flying console panel had struck him during the impact.

"Starboard barrier generator is offline," the chief reported, his voice hoarse over the dying electrical hum. "Main engine power is down to forty percent. But the hull integrity is holding... we didn't take a direct hit."

Chen looked out the viewport at the massive 750-meter Imperial interceptor cruiser floating alongside them. The *Haruna's* forward hull plates were glowing dull cherry-red where the heavy railgun impactors had hit the barrier plane. Plumes of vented coolant gas hissed from two damaged secondary turret mounts, freezing instantly into white ice crystals against the black backdrop of space.

Her eyes drifted down to her smudged paper map taped beside the dead monitor. The pencil lines she had drawn so carefully under her desk lamp in Taipei felt like a bitter indictment. She had understood the theoretical risk of coastal railgun overlap, yet in the heat of tactical pressure, she had allowed her instincts to overrule her training.

The bridge comms console chimed, its amber indicator flashing. The deep, calm voice of the Imperial cruiser captain came through the speaker, unhurried and firm despite the alarms ringing in his own background.

"ROCS Tang, this is IJN Haruna," the Imperial captain said. "Your hull is clear of the kill-zone. Why did your vessel break formation from the assigned defense grid, Chen-san?"

Chen stood on her shattered bridge, her hand resting flat against the smudged paper map on the console. She drew a long, deep breath to answer as the scene cut.
