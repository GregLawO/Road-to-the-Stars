### The Arcturus Threshold

Behind them in the dark, flying without mass relays and invisible to element-zero sensors, the Imperial Starfleet settled sovereign worlds off the relay grid, hardened its outer-system fortresses, and prepared quietly for the inevitable day humanity met whatever else lived in the galaxy."*

A5.1

**Emperor Kenji Himura**  
**Amaterasu Palace Private Study, Kyoto — Summer 2149**

Warm summer rain pattered softly against the heavy cedar eaves outside the open lattice doors of Emperor Kenji Himura's private study, carrying the clean, damp scent of wet moss and mountain pine needles into the quiet, wood-paneled room. Kenji sat comfortably on a cushioned stool at his workbench in a plain dark cotton shirt with the sleeves rolled neatly to his forearms, using a fine brass vernier caliper to check the keel alignment on a carved wooden scale model of the newly commissioned heavy carrier *Shōkaku*.

Across the low keyaki-wood tea table, Empress Kaguya sat calmly pouring steaming roasted green tea into two stoneware bowls while High Censor Renji Kurosawa stood near the sliding shoji screen, holding a slim encrypted photonic slate flat against his dark wool coat. Beside the outer garden gate, two newly upgraded Ashigaru-9 palace automata held silent watch beneath the eaves, their dark-titanium armor plates gleaming faintly in the gray afternoon light and their horizontal amber optical slits dimmed to a steady ember.

"The Systems Alliance Parliament in Geneva formally ratified its Colonial Expansion Charter at noon today, Your Majesty," Kurosawa reported, his voice even, polite, and unhurried in the peaceful room. "Backed by joint UNAS and Eurasian Pact capital, they have consolidated their deep-space survey fleets under a unified Arcturus Command, and their pathfinding frigates are already racing down the Arcturus Stream to claim every habitable world and dormant secondary relay they can find."

Kenji set his brass caliper down neatly beside a small jar of wood glue, dusted fine cedar shavings off his calloused palms, and walked over to take the warm cup of tea Kaguya offered him. He took a slow, appreciative sip, resting one hip comfortably against the edge of the low table while looking out at the rain-slicked stones of the inner palace garden.

"They're sprinting down an unlit hallway with both eyes shut," Kenji said easily, his tone practical and completely free of alarm. "Washington and Moscow spent seventeen years fighting over asteroid scraps, and the second someone hands them an alien highway, they act like the entire galaxy belongs to them."

Kaguya smiled faintly, folding her hands neatly over her lap as a stray gust of warm rain misted the wooden veranda rail. "Did Admiral Togo's vanguard find any sign of living civilizations on the far side of Arcturus?"

"Nothing alive yet, just empty relay junctions, derelict Prothean navigation buoys, and a lot of unclaimed terrestrial worlds," Kenji replied, glancing over at Kurosawa. "Which means whoever actually patrols those relay lanes hasn't stumbled across humanity's survey beacons yet. Knowing how aggressive the Alliance's scout captains are, though, it's only a matter of years before someone tries to crack open a locked relay without reading the warning signs."

"Shall I instruct Fleet Admiral Togo to deploy First Battle Squadron to escort the Systems Alliance scouts and regulate which relays they activate?" Kurosawa asked calmly, his ageless face completely composed.

"No," Kenji said, shaking his head once with firm, grounded certainty. "If we step in front of them and play nanny every time they make a reckless move, Geneva will never learn caution, and worse, whoever built or patrols those relays will spot our hulls before we're ready to set the terms. Let the Alliance chart the relay network and take their own risks. Meanwhile, keep our colonization and shipyard convoys strictly off the relay grid. Every Imperial world we settle gets reached by Sequential Spatial-Fold drive alone, with zero relay footprint, zero element-zero trail, and complete invisibility to anyone riding the alien highways."

Kurosawa bowed slightly, recording the directive onto his photonic slate with a quiet tap. "Understood, Your Majesty. Survey Corps captains under Captain Kenji Mori have already verified atmospheric habitability and orbital defense perimeters on our three primary off-relay worlds: Shin-Yamato at eighteen-point-four light-years, Mizuho at twenty-four-point-one light-years, and Kōya at thirty-one-point-six light-years. None of those three systems lies within forty light-years of an active Prothean mass relay."

"And our Tripartite partners?" Kenji asked, leaning back comfortably against the table edge. "Have Prime Ministers Lin and Han finalized their own off-relay settlement manifests?"

"They confirmed this morning, Your Majesty," Kurosawa answered promptly. "Taiwanese survey transports are preparing to depart Kaohsiung orbital anchor for Penglai, and South Korean colony ships out of Busan are clearing final inspection for Shin-Baekje. Both governments have pledged absolute adherence to the non-relay transit protocol."

"Good," Kenji said, setting his empty teacup down on the lacquer tray and stretching his shoulders with a relaxed grin. "How are the foundry shifts at Titan and Enceladus holding up with the new Solaris-9 assembly schedule?"

"Ahead of quota by four days, Your Majesty," Kurosawa replied. "Lady Shizuka personally inspected the helium-3 skimmer rings over Saturn last week and reported zero seal failures across all twelve atmospheric harvesters. Drydock crews have already completed thirty-eight Solaris-9A battlecruiser conversions and eighty Solaris-9C Shadow cruiser refits."

"Remind me to send Shizuka a crate of Kyoto peaches and fresh Uji matcha on the next courier," Kenji said, turning back toward Kaguya with a warm smile. "When she stays in the outer yards too long, she forgets to eat anything except shipyard rations."

"I already packed two crates this morning before the courier shuttle lifted from Wakayama," Kaguya said softly, her eyes crinkling with quiet amusement. "Along with three new drafting pencils so she stops threatening her welders with a stub."

Kenji laughed quietly, looking back toward Kurosawa near the doorway. "Tell Admiral Togo to send Rear Admiral Ryunosuke and *Yamato* out to Shin-Yamato next month to oversee the first heavy orbital foundry drop. Once those subterranean refineries are humming, our outer colonies won't need a single bolt shipped from Earth."

"I will transmit the deployment orders tonight, Your Majesty," Kurosawa said, bowing respectfully before sliding the shoji screen aside and stepping out into the rain-cooled corridor. **[D]**

A5.2

**Rear Admiral Ryunosuke Himura**  
**IJN-BB-01 *Yamato* (1,000m Refitted Flagship Battleship) — Off-Relay System *Shin-Yamato* (18.4 LY from Sol), Autumn 2149**

Eighteen-point-four light-years from Sol, in a quiet K-type star system completely unlinked to any Prothean mass relay, the one-thousand-meter Imperial flagship battleship *Yamato* hung motionless in high orbit above the emerald-and-sapphire continents of *Shin-Yamato*. Sweeping bands of white cumulus clouds drifted slowly over deep temperate oceans and broad, fertile river valleys, untouched by industrial smoke or foreign transponder beacons.

Inside *Yamato*'s vast, red-lit tactical citadel, Rear Admiral Ryunosuke Himura stood behind the golden-amber quantum-optical globe, watching forty automated orbital drop barges descend smoothly through the upper stratosphere toward a broad coastal plateau below. Around the sunken command pit, tiered watch officers monitored descent telemetry in crisp, disciplined silence. The calm, unhurried order on *Yamato*'s bridge stood in stark contrast to the chaotic, unencrypted radio chatter currently echoing across the Systems Alliance's crowded Arcturus corridor forty light-years away.

"Atmospheric entry complete across all forty landing barges, Admiral," Flag Captain Shinji Takeda reported from the sensor rail, columns of golden-amber kanji cascading cleanly down his angled photonic slate. "Zero thermal hull loss during aerobraking. Subterranean foundation crews and two thousand Ashigaru-9 engineering automata are already offloading heavy Helios-8 industrial reactors and prefabricated foundry domes onto the primary plateau."

Ryunosuke rested both hands on the brass guard rail, his dark eyes reflecting the warm amber glow of the tactical projection. Unlike the frantic, supply-starved frontier outposts the Systems Alliance was throwing together along the Arcturus Stream, which depended on vulnerable element-zero freighters forced to bottleneck through fixed mass relays, *Shin-Yamato* would be completely self-sufficient within eighteen months. Shielded by orbital defense platforms and accessible only via sovereign Solaris-9 Sequential Spatial-Fold drives, the colony existed outside the galactic highway map entirely.

"What is the atmospheric and soil survey readout from the forward agricultural teams?" Ryunosuke asked quietly, keeping his voice low in the hushed bridge pit.

"Nitrogen-oxygen atmosphere at zero-point-nine-eight Earth standard with clean trace argon, Admiral," Takeda replied promptly, checking a secondary status column on his slate. "Native microbial life is completely inert to terrestrial crops, and the river valley ten kilometers east of the foundry site holds enough rich alluvial loam to feed two million colonists within three harvests. First-wave civilian agricultural teams from Hokkaido are already setting up modular greenhouses along the southern ridge."

"And the orbital smelter ring assembly?" Ryunosuke inquired, shifting his gaze upward on the amber globe where six heavy construction tenders floated in synchronous orbit above the equator.

"Skeletal keel trusses for *Shin-Yamato Orbital Yard One* locked into alignment twenty minutes ago, sir," Takeda answered. "Once the asteroid tugs haul the first hundred thousand tons of nickel-iron ore inward from the outer belt next month, our local slips will be able to lay down *Kurokage-class* interceptor hulls without drawing a single ingot from Titan or Luna."

"And the passive gravimetric sweep of the surrounding three-light-year volume?"

"Completely clear, sir," Takeda confirmed, tapping his slate to display an unbroken, featureless perimeter scan. "No element-zero drive signatures, no tachyon carrier waves, and no mass-relay resonance within forty-five light-years. As far as any relay-bound civilization in the galaxy is concerned, this star is just another unremarkable orange dwarf on an empty astronomical chart."

Ryunosuke nodded once, feeling a deep, quiet pride in the sheer scale of what his cousin Kenji had set in motion. Nineteen years earlier, when Ryunosuke had been an impatient young prince eager to flex Japan's muscles in Earth orbit, he had never imagined how far or how quietly the Chrysanthemum Throne would reach. While the other nations of Earth raced blindly down alien corridors without knowing who built the roads or who guarded the gates, Kyoto was planting an unassailable, self-sustaining civilization in the deep dark between the relays, where no outside fleet could ever find them unless Emperor Kenji chose to reveal himself.

"Signal Lord Commander Haruka Tachibana's Gin-Musha-9 surface security detachment to establish automated perimeter beacons around the subterranean reactor vaults," Ryunosuke ordered, turning toward his command chair as the deterministic quantum-optical VI chimed a single, resonant bronze temple-bell tone. "Maintain full Eclipse thermal-sink concealment on all orbital watch destroyers, and keep our twin Solaris-9A cores on standby fold readiness. Once the surface defense grids pass live-fire calibration at dawn, we rotate back to Arcturus shadow to keep an eye on the Alliance's frontier scouts."

"Aye, Admiral," Takeda replied crisply, relaying the encrypted optical burst across the bridge circuit. "Perimeter protocols active and return fold vector to Arcturus dark sector plotted."

Ryunosuke took his seat and looked up through the thick armored quartz viewport at the bright, clean curve of *Shin-Yamato* gleaming below. For the first time in human history, a sovereign world stood completely free of Earth's old rivalries and alien chains alike. **[P]**

A5.3

**Captain Hana Sato (Systems Alliance Survey Corps)**  
**SSV *Normandy-Zero* (Alliance Pathfinding Frigate) — Arcturus Stream Outer Sector, Winter 2149**

Cold blue instrument light washed over the cramped, bustling bridge of the Systems Alliance pathfinding frigate *SSV Normandy-Zero* as Captain Hana Sato gripped the edge of her forward console. Through the reinforced quartz viewport ahead, a massive, dormant secondary mass relay tumbled slowly against the glittering starfield. Designated Relay 218 on the newly compiled Arcturus navigation charts, its twin metallic prongs sat dark and unpowered, surrounded by swirling clouds of interstellar dust and frozen cometary grit.

Behind Sato, two Alliance astrophysics specialists were frantically feeding power-routing algorithms into the frigate's element-zero drive core. They were preparing to broadcast the high-energy activation pulse that had successfully awakened three previous local relays along the Arcturus Stream over the past six months. Around the compact deck, junior officers monitored thermal gauges, magnetic containment coils, and radiation scrubbers with tight, nervous focus.

"Capacitor banks charged to ninety-two percent, Captain," Lieutenant Jenkins reported from the engineering pit. He wiped sweat from his forehead with the back of his sleeve as static sparks snapped faintly along the bulkhead grounding strips. That persistent electrical crackle was a constant, annoying side effect of running early human-built element-zero cores without sufficient planetary magnetic discharge time. "Navigation lock on Relay 218's central gyroscope is solid, and the mass-effect field emitters are aligned."

"Keep an eye on the starboard bus temperature, Jenkins," Sato ordered briskly, glancing briefly over her shoulder toward the engineering hatch. "We burned out two primary relays off Arcturus last month pushing the discharge pulse too fast. I do not want to limp back to Charon on secondary fusion thrusters."

"Watching it like a hawk, ma'am," Jenkins answered, adjusting a coolant valve on his board. "Thermal load is holding well below the red line, and our static bleed capacitors have plenty of margin."

Sato frowned slightly, glancing down at her passive optical and gravimetric sensor slate. Two hours earlier, as *Normandy-Zero* had decelerated out of the primary Arcturus corridor, her long-range optical camera had caught a fleeting, razor-thin silhouette. It had looked like a dark, wedge-shaped hull hanging completely silent in the shadow of a nearby gas giant before vanishing without a single thruster flare, chemical plume, or mass-effect field spike.

"Any repeat of that optical ghost we logged at zero-four-hundred, Sensors?" Sato asked, keeping her voice brisk and professional so the junior watch officers would not hear her unease.

"Negative, ma'am," the sensor officer replied, tapping his headset and checking his swept-band radar screen. "Must have been a metallic asteroid tumbling across the starlight. No element-zero drive signature anywhere within twenty light-years, and zero tachyon carrier waves. We are completely alone out here."

Sato exhaled slowly and nodded once, pushing the lingering suspicion out of her mind. Seven months after *SSV Galileo* had opened the Charon Relay, humanity had found dozens of empty worlds and zero living aliens. Half the Admiralty back at Arcturus Station had already begun joking over drinks that the Protheans had left an empty galaxy behind for human settlers to claim. Yet Sato had grown up in Kobe before emigrating to join the UNAS orbital corps in her youth, and she knew better than most Western officers how silently Emperor Kenji's black warships moved through the dark.

"Do you think the Japanese Survey Corps has already charted this sector, ma'am?" Lieutenant Ramirez asked quietly from the navigation console, lowering his voice so the audio logs would not pick up idle speculation. "Geneva says Kyoto signed the Mars scientific exchange pact, yet we haven't seen a single Japanese transponder past Pluto since April."

"Keep your eyes on your own scope, Ramirez," Sato said evenly, though she appreciated the young navigator's sharp instincts. "What Kyoto does off the relay grid is Geneva's headache. Our job is to map the Prothean network before anyone else finds it."

"Capacitors at one hundred percent, Captain!" the astrophysics lead announced, his voice rising with excitement across the cramped bridge. "Gyroscopic resonance lock confirmed. Ready to transmit activation carrier wave on your mark."

"All stations, stand by for relay activation pulse," Sato ordered, keying the ship-wide intercom with steady fingers. "Brace for magnetic static surge. Let's open the next junction and map the cluster."

A brilliant beam of cerulean mass-effect energy lanced from *Normandy-Zero*'s bow into the dormant Prothean relay.

Inside the frigate, overhead cabin lights flickered wildly for three seconds as static electricity discharged across the hull grounding strips with a loud, sharp crack. A faint smell of ozone drifted up from the deck vents before the air scrubbers cleared the cabin.

Outside the forward viewport, ancient blue-gray rings spun slowly to life around a blinding core of pure cerulean light. A deep, sub-harmonic hum vibrated through the deck plates beneath Sato's boots as Relay 218 aligned its massive prongs toward a distant star cluster.

"Corridor established!" Ramirez called out triumphantly, leaning over his holographic star plot. "Transit link locked onto an uncharted G-type star system forty-two light-years deeper into the stream. Long-range passive probes are through the gate and beaming back initial telemetry now!"

Sato stepped over to the navigation rail, watching high-resolution optical and spectral scans cascade across the glass screen. Four terrestrial worlds orbited a bright yellow sun, flanked by two dense asteroid belts rich in platinum, titanium, and water ice. Not a single radio signal, orbital beacon, or artificial heat source registered anywhere in the new system.

"Atmospheric telemetry on the second terrestrial planet shows breathable nitrogen-oxygen levels at one-point-zero-two Earth standard," the science officer reported excitedly, adjusting his spectrometer readout. "Wide temperate oceans, stable axial tilt, and zero native pathogens detected by the probe's bio-scanner. Arcturus Command is going to name a colonial charter fleet after us before dinner."

"Log the coordinates for Arcturus Command and mark the system for heavy mineral and habitability survey," Sato said, a genuine smile finally breaking across her tired face as her bridge crew cheered softly at their consoles. "Another clean door, and another rich frontier waiting for human settlers."

Neither Sato nor anyone aboard *Normandy-Zero* noticed that ten thousand kilometers away in the black shadow of the gas giant's inner ice rings, the seven-hundred-fifty-meter Imperial Shadow cruiser *Kurokage* held completely motionless under Eclipse stealth.

Inside *Kurokage*'s dim, red-lit bridge pit, Captain Hiroshi Mori sat calmly in the command chair he had inherited after Flag Captain Shinji Takeda transferred to the flagship *Yamato*. Across the golden-amber quantum-optical globe, columns of crisp kanji recorded every harmonic frequency, power surge, and directional vector generated by Relay 218's awakening.

"Full harmonic profile of Relay 218 logged into the sovereign optical crystal, Captain," Lieutenant Hara reported quietly from the sensor rail. "The Nara mass-effect sensor patch captured the entire transit wave without emitting a single active pulse."

"Good," Mori said softly, resting one hand on the cold brass rail. Powered by her Era III Solaris-9C gravitic core and diamond micro-channel cooling sinks, *Kurokage* vented zero thermal wake and zero element-zero static, allowing Kyoto to map every corridor of the Prothean relay network in total silence without ever setting foot inside the alien gates. **[W]**

A5.4

**Commander Jon Grissom**  
**Arcturus Construction Flotilla Flagship *SSV Pioneer* — Arcturus System, Spring 2150**

Inside the brightly lit orbital construction command module overlooking the massive, half-finished skeletal rings of Arcturus Station, Commander Jon Grissom stood beside the main holographic assembly table with a steaming mug of black coffee in hand. Forty-five years old now, newly promoted to oversee the Systems Alliance's primary deep-space logistics hub after commanding the historic Charon relay expedition aboard *SSV Galileo* the previous spring, Grissom watched the vast station take shape against the glittering starfield. Outside the wide observation windows, dozens of UNAS, European, and Eurasian Pact heavy tugs were welding prefabricated titanium hull modules onto the station's central spindle, bathed in the harsh white glare of arc welders and work lamps.

Across the holographic table, Vice Admiral Steven Hackett, head of the newly organized Alliance Fleet Operations Directorate, studied a glowing interstellar transit chart showing the growing web of mapped relays radiating outward from Charon and Arcturus. Beside him stood two senior logistics officers checking docking manifests, element-zero fuel quotas, and colonial transport schedules for the coming summer.

"The first heavy ore haulers from the newly opened 218 cluster will arrive at Arcturus by June, Jon," Hackett said, his voice gruff and practical as he tapped a finger against the station's docking schedule. "Once our orbital refineries and drydock bays come online, the Systems Alliance won't have to beg Earth's national governments for every ton of refined titanium or helium-3 propellant."

Grissom nodded slowly, taking a sip of bitter black coffee as he watched a heavy cargo tug maneuver a ninety-ton pressure bulkhead into alignment along the station's outer docking ring. "Assuming our element-zero freighters hold up under continuous relay transits, Admiral. Chief Engineer Adams tells me three of our bulk haulers arrived from the 218 run yesterday with hairline static fractures along their forward hull plating."

"We're installing heavier magnetic discharge strips on every hull passing through the Arcturus drydocks," Hackett replied grimly. "Until our metallurgists solve the static buildup problem from the Mars schematics, our captains will just have to schedule mandatory planetary magnetosphere dips every three jumps."

Grissom's gaze drifted away from the station trusses toward the far, dark edge of the holographic star chart. Beyond the bright blue lines connecting the active Prothean mass relays stretched a vast, unmapped expanse of black space where zero Alliance transponders or survey beacons registered.

"That isn't the only headache keeping my watch officers awake, Admiral," Grissom said quietly, lowering his voice so the junior logistics officers at the far console would not overhear. "Have you reviewed the latest passive telescope logs from our outer picket frigates?"

Hackett's jaw tightened slightly, a muscle flexing beneath his weathered cheek. "The phantom drive wakes."

"Five separate Alliance survey ships caught brief optical glimpses of matte-black, wedge-shaped hulls operating light-years away from any active mass relay over the past four months," Grissom said evenly, tapping a console key to pull up a grainy long-range optical frame captured by *SSV Normandy-Zero* near Relay 218. "No element-zero core signature, no static discharge, and zero relay transit logs. Emperor Kenji's fleet isn't using the Prothean highways at all. They're cutting straight across empty space on their own reactionless drives and settling worlds we can't even reach."

"And Geneva is furious because Kyoto refuses to submit Imperial colony coordinates to the Alliance Colonial Registry," Hackett said grimly, crossing his arms over his navy tunic. "The Parliament wants to declare all extra-solar human settlements subject to Systems Alliance maritime law. They're sending a formal diplomatic delegation to Kyoto next month to demand full telemetry sharing and joint customs inspection rights."

Grissom let out a short, dry breath and looked back out at the welding flashes illuminating the massive ribs of Arcturus Station. "I stood on the bridge of *Galileo* at Charon last year, Admiral, and I saw seven Japanese warships holding station behind the moon's dark limb without radiating a single watt of infrared heat. After what happened in October 2132, I don't think telling Emperor Kenji what laws apply to his ships is going to go over very well."

"Geneva doesn't ask my opinion on diplomacy, Jon," Hackett said dryly, though his sharp eyes reflected the exact same caution. "My job is to build a fleet that can defend human colonies without relying on Kyoto's goodwill. How fast can your construction crews finish Berth Four and Berth Five on the outer ring?"

"If the ore barges from 218 arrive on schedule in June, we can lay down the keels for four new *Agincourt-class* element-zero cruisers before autumn," Grissom answered promptly, shifting back into practical engineering mode. "We've already pre-fabricated the main spine trusses in zero gravity, and our EVA welding teams are running three shifts around the clock."

"Good," Hackett said, nodding once with crisp approval as he rolled up his hard-copy transit chart. "Keep those slips moving, Commander. Whether we ever run into whoever built those Prothean relays or just have to prove to Kyoto that the Systems Alliance can stand on its own two feet, Arcturus Station has to be an iron fortress before the year is out."

"We'll have the armor plating bolted down before the first autumn frost hits Geneva, sir," Grissom assured him.

Hackett shook Grissom's hand firmly and walked briskly toward the airlock hatch to catch his inspection shuttle back to the fleet anchorage. Left alone beside the observation window, Grissom stood quietly for a long moment with his cooling coffee mug in hand, watching tiny sparks from dozens of orbital arc welders flare like fireflies against the vast, silent dark of the Arcturus stream. Somewhere out there in the black beyond the relay beams, Emperor Kenji's silent black dreadnoughts were already watching, waiting to see what humanity would make of its hard-won reach into the stars. **[P]**

A5.5

**Director General Evelyn Vance**  
**Systems Alliance Intelligence Directorate, Geneva — Summer 2150**

Cold fluorescent light hummed over the subterranean conference table in Geneva as Director General Evelyn Vance laid a thin folder of deep-space optical intercepts onto the polished oak surface. Around the room sat the senior members of the newly formed Systems Alliance Executive Council: American, Russian, European, and Chinese ministers who had pooled their nations' resources after the 2149 Charon activation to build a unified human interstellar government.

At the far end of the table, completely calm and unruffled in his dark wool diplomatic coat, sat Ambassador Kenjiro Matsuda of the Empire of Japan, flanked by his Taiwanese and South Korean counterparts. Behind Matsuda's chair stood two silent diplomatic security officers in plain dark suits, their hands folded neatly at their waists.

"Our long-range astronomical arrays at Lagrange One and Arcturus have confirmed at least eleven separate off-axis transit wakes departing Sol over the past fourteen months," Evelyn said, keeping her voice measured and diplomatic yet pointed as she looked straight down the table toward the Japanese delegation. "Under the 2149 Geneva Spaceflight Charter, all human vessels operating beyond the Kuiper Belt are required to file flight plans and share habitable-world survey data with the Systems Alliance Registry to prevent territorial disputes and overlapping colonial claims."

Ambassador Matsuda did not flinch or raise his voice. Seventy-one years old, though Stage Four Chōju renewal had left his posture razor-straight and his dark eyes sharp as polished obsidian, he folded his hands neatly over his blotter before answering.

"The Empire of Japan, alongside the sovereign Republics of Taiwan and Korea, signed the 2148 Mars Scientific Exchange Protocol, Director Vance, not the 2149 Geneva Spaceflight Charter," Matsuda said evenly. "Our vessels do not utilize Prothean mass relays, they do not consume element zero, and they do not enter any star system claimed or traversed by Systems Alliance survey ships. Consequently, Geneva holds zero legal or practical jurisdiction over sovereign Tripartite Accord expeditions."

"If humanity fractures into rival, secret empires before we even finish mapping the Arcturus Stream, we invite chaos if we ever encounter the alien threat described in the Mars archives," UNAS Secretary of State Elena Vance argued from the front row, leaning forward on her elbows. "What does Emperor Kenji want in exchange for integrating your star charts and colony coordinates with Arcturus Command?"

Matsuda permitted himself a faint, polite smile that never reached his eyes. "His Majesty has no desire to rule your colonies or dictate your relay expeditions, Secretary Vance. Your survey frigates are welcome to map every mass relay in the galaxy without interference from Kyoto. However, if the Systems Alliance wishes to maintain peaceful orbital trade and shared early-warning telemetry with our outer-system buoys, Geneva will formally recognize Tripartite sovereignty over all off-relay star systems settled by our citizens, and immediately halt all covert Alliance recruitment and technical espionage inside Japanese, Taiwanese, and Korean borders."

Silence hung heavy over the council chamber for several seconds. Across the table, Marshal Volkov of the Eurasian Pact exchanged a tight, frustrated glance with the UNAS representatives. Both blocs were acutely aware that while their element-zero frigates were still tethered to fixed Prothean gates and forced to discharge static into planetary magnetospheres every few jumps, Kyoto's Solaris-9 battlecruisers could cross fifty light-years in under three days without asking anyone's permission.

"You speak of covert recruitment and espionage as though Geneva were running hostile operations against Kyoto, Ambassador," Marshal Volkov rumbled, his heavy Russian accent echoing off the concrete walls. "Thousands of young engineers in Yokohama, Taipei, and Busan have voluntarily applied to join the Alliance Frontier Corps because they want to explore the new relay worlds."

"Voluntary applicants are free to emigrate through standard customs channels, Marshal Volkov," Matsuda replied calmly, sliding a single blue dossier across the polished oak table toward Director General Vance. "However, our Censorate port inspectors at Yokohama and Kaohsiung intercepted three forged diplomatic courier manifests over the past six weeks attempting to smuggle former Kinki-Kyoku optical technicians and restricted crystal schematics aboard Alliance charter shuttles. His Majesty views such actions as a direct violation of the 2132 Armistice."

Evelyn Vance opened the blue folder, scanning the high-resolution surveillance photographs and forged passport numbers stamped with the imperial chrysanthemum seal. Her jaw tightened slightly as she closed the folder and looked back up at Matsuda.

"We will investigate those unauthorized courier manifests immediately and recall any diplomatic attaché who exceeded their mandate, Ambassador," Evelyn said evenly, keeping her expression composed despite the sharp glance she shot toward the European intelligence minister across the table. "In return, we expect Imperial vessels operating near the Arcturus Stream to maintain standard navigational transponders so our survey frigates do not mistake them for unknown alien contacts."

"Imperial warships do not enter active Prothean relay corridors unless invited by treaty, Director Vance," Matsuda answered smoothly, rising from his chair with a polite, unhurried bow as his Taiwanese and Korean colleagues stood beside him. "Draft the parliamentary ratification articles recognizing our off-relay sovereignty, and deliver them to our embassy before the end of the month. As long as Alliance ships respect our borders, the stars are more than wide enough for both of us."

As the Tripartite delegation filed quietly out of the subterranean chamber, Secretary Elena Vance leaned back in her chair and exhaled a long, tired breath. Across the table, Marshal Volkov stared grimly at the blue dossier lying on the oak tabletop. Eighteen years after October 2132, humanity had finally reached the stars, yet Geneva was learning the exact same lesson Washington and Moscow had learned in Earth orbit: Emperor Kenji Himura shared the sky freely with good neighbors, and never yielded a single inch to anyone who tried to crowd his gate. **[D]**
