A3.1

**Emperor Kenji Himura**  
**Kii-Yamato Subterranean Drydock Seven, Wakayama — Spring 2133**

Warm, dry air smelling faintly of machined titanium and cedar oil drifted across the steel observation platform overlooking Drydock Seven. Emperor Kenji Himura leaned both forearms comfortably on the safety rail, watching the automated gantry winches retract from the upper hull below. Forty meters beneath his boots, bright halogen work lamps gleamed off the freshly rolled neutronium-carbide armor plates of the refitted interceptor cruiser *Kurokage*. Two dozen Kinki-Kyoku technicians in dark navy coveralls were sealing the armored access hatch over her new Solaris-7C gravitic core, checking every torque bolt with optical calipers.

Beside Kenji on the catwalk, Lady Shizuka Himura held a thick wooden clipboard tucked under one arm. She checked weld tolerances against a printed schematic, a stubby wooden pencil resting behind her right ear and her wire-rimmed spectacles perched low on her nose. Behind them at the stairwell landing, Lord Commander Haruka Tachibana stood in her updated Gin-Musha-7 silver-plated exosuit. Her helmet rested neatly under her left arm while her dark eyes swept the upper gantry cranes and ventilation shafts with quiet, unbroken vigilance.

"Zero structural stress on the main keel ribs during the sixty-five percent mass-shedding bench test," Shizuka said briskly. She tapped her pencil twice against the clipboard. "The upgraded solid-state lithium-sulfur nano-capacitor matrix absorbs the spatial-fold surge without heating the diamond sinks past forty degrees. Honestly, Kenji, this core makes the old Solaris-4 look like a coal-fired steam boiler."

"It ought to," Kenji said with an easy, genuine smile, rolling his shoulders back in his dark wool workshop jacket. "Your Nara crystal team spent three full years cutting the lattice impurities down to parts per billion. How fast can we swap the cores on Captain Takeda's remaining three Shadow cruisers?"

"Six weeks per hull if the drydock crews keep their current rotation," Shizuka replied, pushing her glasses up the bridge of her nose. "Foreman Sato is already complaining that your tactical micro-jump spec gives his welders half the tolerances they had on the pre-war frames. He says a two-millimeter seating gap on the capacitor bus will rattle a crewman's teeth out during a fold."

"Tell Sato he's right about the seating gap, and tell him I'm sending down two crates of Kobe beef and a case of good plum wine for his shift tonight," Kenji said, turning toward the iron stairs with a relaxed nod. "Good precision deserves good dinner."

"I will tell him myself before the night whistle blows," Shizuka said, tucking her pencil behind her ear as she turned back toward the inspection lift. "Keep your hands out of the coolant manifolds until the welders clear the bay, Kenji. Kaguya will have my hide if you come back to Kyoto smelling like gear oil again."

"No promises," Kenji replied with a quiet laugh.

Half an hour later, Kenji stepped out of the high-speed subterranean lift into the sunlit upper courtyard of the Kii-Yamato mountain compound. A crisp spring breeze carried the clean scent of pine needles and distant sea salt up from the Pacific coast. High Censor Renji Kurosawa was waiting beside the covered cedar walkway, holding a slim encrypted photonic slate flat against his dark wool coat while two Ashigaru-7 automata stood motionless in recessed stone alcoves ten paces behind him.

"Morning briefing from Geneva and Lagrange One, Your Majesty," Kurosawa said, falling into step beside the Emperor as they walked toward the garden pavilion. "Both UNAS and the Eurasian Pact have formally unveiled their first prototype gravitic test hulls, the *USS Constitution* and the *RSFS Gagarin*. According to our passive optical telemetry from Luna, neither hull can sustain more than twelve percent apparent mass reduction without overheating its magnetic containment coils."

Kenji let out a quiet, dry chuckle, tucking his calloused hands into his jacket pockets. "Twelve percent. That's barely half of what we welded into the export Helios-2 cores we gave Taipei and Seoul three years ago."

"Correct, Your Majesty," Kurosawa replied, a faint hint of satisfaction touching his ageless, unlined face. "Desperate to close the gap before their next orbital budget cycle, both Washington and Moscow have tripled their deep-space mining claims in the main asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter. Four heavily armed Pact and UNAS prospectors have already clashed over metallic-rich asteroids near Vesta and Ceres."

Kenji stopped beside a carved granite water basin where clear mountain water trickled steadily from a bamboo spout, watching the concentric ripples spread across the dark surface. He thought briefly of the quiet nights in his prior life spent poring over orbital charts of the solar system. He knew even then that whoever controlled the raw metals, volatile gases, and water ice of the outer belt controlled the next five hundred years of human history.

"Let them fight over loose rocks near Vesta," Kenji said calmly, his voice practical and completely unhurried. "Tell Fleet Admiral Togo that as soon as *Amaterasu-no-Yaiba* finishes her Solaris-7A core refit next month, First Battle Squadron and the heavy mining tenders are moving out to the Jovian Trojans and the Saturnian rings. By the time Washington and Moscow figure out how to fly past Mars without burning up their drive bells, we'll already own the outer system."

Kurosawa bowed slightly, recording the directive onto his photonic slate with a quiet tap. "Understood, Your Majesty. Prime Ministers Lin and Han also confirmed this morning that their Helios-5 coastal defense grids are fully operational, leaving us free to shift our heavy cruisers outward without weakening the home perimeter."

"Good," Kenji said, turning back toward the courtyard gate. "Let's get back to Kyoto before Kaguya starts dinner without me." **[D]**

**Captain Shinji Takeda**  
**IJN-KGE-SP *Kurokage* (750m Refitted Interceptor Cruiser) — Lunar Far-Side Proving Ground, Summer 2133**

Inside the sunken command pit of the newly refitted interceptor cruiser *Kurokage*, the golden-amber quantum-optical globe hummed with a deeper, richer resonance than it had during the autumn battle over Honshu. Captain Shinji Takeda sat strapped into his magnetic crash chair, his calloused palms resting firmly on the padded armrests. Takeda leaned back slightly against his cushioned headrest, feeling the steady, sub-audible thrum of the upgraded Solaris-7C octahedron core vibrating through the deck plates beneath his boots. "All stations, brace for tactical fold," Takeda commanded. "Mori, execute."

Space outside the armored quartz viewports did not streak with blurred starlight. It simply folded inward like dark silk pulled smoothly through a brass ring.

A faint, metallic taste touched the back of Takeda's tongue as the spatial-fold wave rippled cleanly from bow to stern.

Zero crushing G-force slammed his chest against the crash webbing. The upgraded ninety-nine-point-nine percent inertial dampeners swallowed the transition whole.

In less than half a second, the distant gray curve of the lunar crater wall snapped into sharp, towering focus directly off *Kurokage*'s starboard quarter.

"Emergence complete!" Mori reported, his voice completely steady. "Range to target hulk is six hundred meters. Drift error is one-point-four meters."

"Apertures open. Twin Energy Lances firing!" Hara shouted.

Two millisecond firing apertures tore open across *Kurokage*'s dorsal faceted barrier.

Invisible, coherent lightspeed fire lanced across the six-hundred-meter gap before a human eye could have blinked.

The forty-thousand-ton booster stage split cleanly along its central titanium spine in a silent, sparkless flash of white-hot metal. Its two severed halves drifted lazily apart above the gray lunar dust, completely devoid of high-velocity shrapnel clouds.

"Apertures closed! Core temperature holding at thirty-eight degrees!" Kobayashi called up from engineering. "Diamond thermal sinks at twelve percent capacity."

Takeda unclipped his magnetic chest harness and stood up on the command dais, looking across the silent bridge pit at his young helmsman and gunnery officer. Every battleship admiral in Washington, Moscow, and Beijing was still drawing orbital defense perimeters based on light-speed radar sweeps and continuous ballistic flight trajectories. Against a fleet that could vanish at three thousand kilometers and emerge six hundred meters off their unshielded bellies with lightspeed weapons already firing, every naval battle doctrine on Earth had just become obsolete overnight.

"Log the test telemetry into the sovereign optical crystal, Mori," Takeda said quietly, resting one hand on the brass rail. "Then set course for Earth-Luna Lagrange Four. First Battle Squadron departs for the asteroid belt at dawn." **[P]**

A3.3

**Lord Commander Haruka Tachibana**  
**Kōsan-Ceres Deep-Core Mining Complex (`Ceres-01`) — Main Asteroid Belt, Autumn 2134**

Low gravity, barely three percent of Earth's pull, gave every footstep inside the excavated basalt cavern of *Kōsan-Ceres* a smooth, measured glide. Lord Commander Haruka Tachibana walked the perimeter steel catwalk high above the primary smelting bay, her magnetic boot soles clicking softly against the grated deck. Beneath her updated Gin-Musha-7 silver-plated exosuit, the passive BMUS-7 biomechanical under-suit mapped her stride cleanly onto her pelvis and spine through tensioned carbon-fiber tendons without draining a single watt of reserve power. Between her shoulder blades, the compact Solaris-7M Ember core hummed with quiet, effortless strength.

Sixty meters below the catwalk, massive magnetic induction furnaces powered by a blackboxed Helios-8 industrial reactor were refining thousands of tons of raw nickel-iron and platinum-group ore. Silent teams of Ashigaru-7 engineering automata carved the ore from deep subterranean veins inside Ceres, loading heavy hover-skips in rhythmic, unbroken shifts. Unlike the noisy, surface-scarred strip mines operated by UNAS and Eurasian Pact corporations on Eros and Vesta, the Imperial extraction complex sat two kilometers beneath Ceres's frozen crust. It vented zero thermal plume into space and shipped refined alloy ingots back to the lunar yards aboard stealth-shrouded bulk tenders.

"Perimeter seismic sensors along Shaft Four are clean, Lord Commander," Lieutenant Kenzo reported over the encrypted Hatamoto suit channel. His silver exosuit gleamed faintly under the cavern's amber work lamps as he checked the heavy titanium vault airlock seals. "All forty Ashigaru-7 security units are holding their alcove posts in passive watch mode."

Haruka nodded once inside her helmet, her dark eyes scanning the reinforced blast doors leading toward the surface ice-mining shaft. Three weeks earlier, a UNAS long-range survey drone launched from a Prospectors' Guild cruiser near Pallas had drifted within fifty kilometers of Ceres's northern pole. A Shadow destroyer had quietly netted it in deep shadow without leaving a trace of debris. Emperor Kenji's orders were clear: the outer belt was to remain an invisible industrial engine until the fleet's Solaris-7 expansion was complete.

Suddenly, a sharp, two-tone amber warning chime echoed inside Haruka's helmet visor. The station's deterministic Quantum-Optical VI had detected an unauthorized seismic breach in the upper rock strata.

"Seismic anomaly in Ice Shaft Seven, two hundred meters above the smelting gallery," the VI's calm, synthesized voice announced over the command circuit. "Thermal plasma cutting torches detected breaching Bulkhead Door Nine."

"Hatamoto First Squad, on me," Haruka said, her voice flat and completely steady. "Keep the smelting bay sealed and cut power to the upper lift cables. Capture at least two intact for High Censor Kurosawa's interrogation team."

She drew her high-frequency mono-blade katana from its magnetic hip scabbard.

Haruka engaged her Solaris-7M Ember core at sixty-five percent mass-shedding.

She kicked off the steel catwalk, gliding upward through the low-gravity shaft like a silver hawk.

At the top of Ice Shaft Seven, the heavy titanium bulkhead door swung inward with a dull metallic groan. Clouds of white vapor poured into the corridor as four operatives in dark, unmarked Pact Spetsnaz pressure suits stepped through, carrying portable plasma lances and magnetic demolition packs.

"Contact!" the lead Pact operative shouted over unencrypted local radio, raising a heavy-caliber caseless assault rifle.

Four bursts of armor-piercing tungsten slugs sparked furiously off Haruka's faceted directional barrier. The rounds deflected wide into the basalt cavern walls without scratching her silver chest plate.

Haruka closed the thirty-meter corridor in less than a second.

She swept her mono-blade in a tight, controlled horizontal arc.

The vibrating edge sheared cleanly through the barrels of the two forward rifles, leaving the operatives staring at smoking stubs of steel.

Before the third man could draw a sidearm, Haruka drove an armored palm straight into his chest plate. The blow launched him backward across the low-gravity chamber into the ice wall with enough force to crack his visor and knock the wind out of his lungs.

The fourth operative dropped his demolition pack and lunged for his suit's self-destruct collar switch.

Haruka blurred forward with a micro-burst of directional gravitic thrust, caught his wrist in her silver gauntlet, and snapped the bone cleanly above the cuff before his thumb could touch the stud.

Behind her, six Hatamoto guards landed silently on the cavern deck, leveling compact electromagnetic flechette carbines at the stunned survivors. Two Ashigaru-7 automata stepped out of the wall alcoves in lockstep to apply heavy polymer restraints.

Haruka sheathed her mono-blade with a crisp metallic click and looked down at the groaning Spetsnaz commander pinned against the ice wall.

"How did your ship track our ore tender through the belt?" Haruka asked calmly over external proximity speakers.

The Russian officer spat blood inside his helmet and glared up at her silver faceplate. "Your ships don't leave drive plumes, Samurai. Your ice drills shake the rock. Moscow knows you're digging out here."

Haruka activated her suit's long-range encrypted burst transmitter to alert the orbiting Shadow destroyer *Oboro*. "Lord Commander Tachibana to *Oboro*. Four Pact infiltrators secured in Ice Shaft Seven. Sweep the horizon within ten thousand kilometers. Their insertion stealth boat is sitting somewhere in the dark." **[W]**

A3.4

**Director General Pavel Orlov**  
**Eurasian Pact Deep-Space Survey Cruiser *RSFS Gagarin* — Jovian Trojan Belt, Spring 2136**

Red emergency strobes flashed rhythmically down the narrow, condensation-streaked central corridor of the *RSFS Gagarin*. Director General Pavel Orlov gripped the bolted hand-rung beside the main tactical plotting table, steadying himself as the deck plates vibrated under uneven engine thrust. Thirty-eight million kilometers behind their stern, the pale yellow disk of Jupiter hung like a bruised pearl against the pitch-black void, surrounded by the faint, glittering pinpricks of its four Galilean moons.

Inside the cramped, overheated command deck, the air smelled sharply of burnt electrical insulation, stale sweat, and recirculated lubricant. Across the illuminated plotting display, two escorting Pact nuclear-thermal frigates, *Voronezh* and *Irkutsk*, were bleeding bright white plumes of superheated hydrogen plasma as they struggled to brake from their high-speed Hohmann transfer orbit into the L4 Trojan asteroid cluster.

"Core containment temperature on our prototype gravitic drive has crossed ninety-four percent, Director," Chief Engineer Volodin reported. He wiped grease and sweat from his forehead with the back of his sleeve as warning chimes pinged steadily from his console. "At twelve percent apparent mass reduction, the magnetic field coils are warping under harmonic stress. If we push the governor to fifteen percent to match the escort frigates' deceleration burn, the containment ring will quench inside ninety seconds and melt the primary manifold."

Orlov scowled, leaning over the thick glass tactical screen where three years of painstaking intelligence work had finally led him. After losing twelve elite operatives at Kōsan-Ceres in 2134 and watching Kyoto's diplomatic walls remain completely impenetrable, Moscow and Beijing had poured four hundred billion rubles into reverse-engineering a shattered fragment of a Helios-2 optical manifold salvaged from a sunken Taiwanese cutter during the October war. The result was *Gagarin*, the Eurasian Pact's crown jewel, a three-hundred-meter experimental cruiser carrying the first mainland-built reactionless drive into the outer solar system.

"Hold the gravitic coil at ten percent and light the secondary nuclear-thermal bells to bleed off excess velocity," Orlov ordered, his voice rasping in the dry cabin air. "We came all the way out to the Trojans to verify the deep seismic anomalies our long-range telescopes detected around Patroclus. I am not turning back to Baikonur with empty hard drives and excuses for the Politburo."

"Thermal plumes flaring on *Voronezh* and *Irkutsk*," the sensor officer called out, adjusting his headset as passive optical telescopes swept the dark, tumbling mountain-sized asteroids ahead. "Range to asteroid Patroclus is four thousand kilometers. No radio emissions, no transponder beacons, and zero infrared signatures on the surface."

Orlov allowed himself a thin, triumphant smile. For two years, Kyoto's representatives in Geneva had claimed that Imperial Japan was confining its space activities strictly to Earth-Luna orbit and peaceful orbital habitats. Yet here, nearly eight hundred million kilometers from the Sun, deep-space gravitational lensing surveys had detected massive, unexplained mass shifts inside the largest Trojan asteroids.

"Deploy the autonomous lidar mapping probes and bring our active phased-array radar online," Orlov commanded. "Sweep every crater, ridge, and shadow pocket on Patroclus. Once we transmit hard proof of an illegal Japanese military shipyard outside the Armistice exclusion zone to the UNAS Security Council, Washington will have no choice but to join us in freezing Kyoto's Pacific trade lanes."

"Activating phased-array sweep," the radar operator confirmed, flipping the heavy toggle switch on his panel.

For three full seconds, the sweeping green radar beam painted the jagged, carbonaceous surface of Patroclus, revealing nothing but ancient impact craters and frozen dust. Then, directly off *Gagarin*'s port beam, less than nine hundred meters away in open space where the radar sweep had shown absolute emptiness half a second earlier, three massive, matte-black hulls materialized out of folded space without a sound.

Orlov's breath caught in his throat.

The lead warship was one thousand meters long, shaped like an elongated, razor-edged arrowhead of dark neutronium-carbide, flanked by two sleek interceptor cruisers. No drive nozzles glowed at their sterns, no radiators bled heat into the dark, and no warning beacons flashed along their hulls.

"Tactical micro-jump!" Volodin gasped, his face draining of color as his passive gravimetric sensors spiked off the scale. "Three vessels, zero approach vector! They just folded space right on top of us!"

"All ships, emergency evasive burn!" Orlov shouted, grabbing the edge of the plotting table as proximity alarms shrieked across the bridge. "Launch decoy flares and bring railguns to bear!"

Before the gunnery officer's fingers even touched the firing key, invisible lightspeed fire lanced across the nine-hundred-meter gap.

Silent, surgical beams sliced through *Gagarin*'s dorsal radar mast, severed the power conduits feeding her experimental gravitic drive ring, and melted the railgun turrets off both escorting frigates in less than two seconds. Not a single hull was breached, and not a single life-support compartment lost pressure. Within three heartbeats, the pride of the Eurasian Pact deep-space fleet was drifting blind, deaf, and powerless in the dark shadow of Jupiter.

Over the main bridge speaker, a calm, unhurried Japanese voice spoke in fluent, unaccented Russian: *"Unidentified vessels in sovereign Imperial survey sector L4. Your weapons and propulsion systems have been disabled to prevent navigational hazards. Prepare to receive boarding shuttles for immediate escort back to inner-system shipping lanes."*

Orlov stood frozen beside the dead tactical screen, staring out the viewport at the silent black warships hanging effortlessly in the void. Moscow had spent four years and half a trillion rubles trying to catch up to October 2132, and Emperor Kenji's fleet was already fighting a war from another century. **[P]**
