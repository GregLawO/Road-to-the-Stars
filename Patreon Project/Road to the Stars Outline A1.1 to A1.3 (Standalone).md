# Road to the Stars: Outline A1.1 to A1.3 (Standalone, from the Revised Outline)

**Conventions for every segment (not repeated below)**

- Each segment runs 1,000 to 2,000 words.
- About 80% is slow gear. Fast gear starts at the physical trigger and carries one body detail.
- The segment opens on a concrete detail that raises a question.
- Dialogue tags are "said" and "asked", with action beats instead.
- Past-life hints for Kenji stay under three sentences per chapter.
- Each A1 scene carries a word target. If a draft runs long, trim the last scene's tail, never the ending beat.
- The "Write it" notes say how to draft each scene. Names, ages and ranks marked (decision) are open to overrule.
- Every scene entry carries three notes after its beats: *How to write it*, *What it does for the segment*, and *What it does for the story*. A scene is not complete in the outline without all three. (author ruling)

## ERA I: THE EVE OF THE TEMPEST (2130 to 2132)

### A1. THE QUIET EDGE (2130 to 2131) [Intrigue-weighted, 6 Intrigue + 4 Action]

**Batch 1.** Opens with the excerpt "On the Quiet Decade," *Annals of the Chrysanthemum Restoration*.

**Closing feeling:** calm on the surface, with one small thing wrong in every room.

**Echo:** a seal, a log and a badge that don't match (A1.2, A1.4, A1.5).

**A1.1. Emperor Kenji Himura (38, 2130, Kyoto-Heian Imperial Citadel). Intrigue. Ending E. (~1,700 words)**

1. *Design bay, morning (~600).* The Quantum-Optical VI chimes its bronze bell tone over the design table, and the table projects a Solaris-4B core model with one phase conduit glowing a different amber. A minute ago Kenji reshaped that conduit so the core holds its 45% mass-shedding at a lower load. The VI finishes its run and the model holds, as he expected. He approves the revised core on the table's console, and the file goes to the Kinki-Kyoku foundry, where Kōgyō-4 automata will build the test unit and the engineers will run it. Ayame, 4, sits on a cushion by the table watching the model turn. She points at the next conduit. Kenji says that one will not change anything, and rotates it anyway. Shizuka comes in with a bowl and a one-line report: the third dock launched its hull ahead of the ledger, and she wants the core spec frozen for the next batch. Kenji says tomorrow, once the test unit's numbers come back. He eats standing at the table while the next model loads.

*How to write it:*

- Slow gear throughout.
- Open on the bronze bell tone and the one conduit glowing a different amber.
- Kenji designs and improves his own technology. Automata and Kinki-Kyoku engineers build and test what he draws. He never handles tools or materials.
- He does not hesitate over the model. The VI run confirms what he already knew.
- The design table is open to family. Ayame sits at it and no one sends her out. Her pointing at the next conduit and his rotating it anyway is the whole exchange, with no lesson attached (decision).
- Shizuka's report is one line, plain and brisk. She addresses him as Heika, since she is reporting dock business to the Emperor (decision).
- Kenji's enjoyment of the model holding as predicted is the first half of the chapter's single past-life hint. The sea bream in scene 3 is the second half. Together they stay under three sentences.
- End on Kenji eating standing at the table as the next model loads (decision).

*What it does for the segment:* It is the anchor scene and sets the calm baseline. It opens the segment on a concrete detail that raises a question (the one conduit in a different amber), shows Kenji's full mastery of his own machines, and establishes the warm, unhurried tone that scenes 2 and 3 continue.

*What it does for the story:* It shows who Kenji is before anything else happens: a sovereign who designs his own technology, trusts it, and enjoys it. It quietly seeds the hidden buildup (a core spec going to the Kinki-Kyoku foundry, the third dock ahead of schedule) without explaining it, and it shows the household as a working, affectionate unit rather than a court.

2. *Council anteroom, midday (~500).* Before the afternoon session, Kenji walks from the design bay to the council anteroom, where Minister Kitagawa Sōichirō waits outside the closed session doors with his papers. The envoys from Seoul and Taipei are already seated inside (decision). Kenji hands Kitagawa the terms in a few sentences: Imperial hulls that come to an ally's aid leave when the fight ends, no Imperial flag stays on their soil, and the field command is theirs. Kitagawa asks how firmly to hold the point. Kenji says to put it in the text. Kitagawa bows and goes in to sign, and Kenji does not attend the session.

*How to write it:*

- Slow gear throughout.
- Open on the session papers squared against Kitagawa's chest, outside the closed session doors (decision).
- Kenji comes to him from the design bay and arrives with a plain, unhurried walk (decision). The scene happens in the anteroom only.
- The treaty terms are two or three plain sentences in dialogue, not a speech.
- Kenji states the cost first, the way he does with everything he commits to. He is relaxed and plain-spoken, with a comparison from engines, welds or food.
- Kitagawa says *Heika*. His question about how firmly to hold the point is a real question and gets a short answer.
- Do not show the envoys. They are only mentioned (decision).
- This is the promise that returns in A3.3. Keep the three terms exactly as stated: they leave when the fight ends, no Imperial flag stays, the field command is theirs.
- End on Kitagawa bowing and going in to sign.

*What it does for the segment:* It shifts the segment from work to policy and carries the batch's main echo, the three treaty terms. It is the segment's only dialogue-driven scene with a second speaker outside the family, and it gives the middle of the segment a quieter, more formal register.

*What it does for the story:* It states the Empire's foreign policy as a personal promise, with the cost named before anyone commits. This is the moral spine of the series, and A3.3 returns to it when Seoul formally requests help. It also shows how Kenji governs (he decides cleanly, delegates the signing, skips the session) and introduces Kitagawa, a recurring Tripartite POV, through his role rather than a speech.

3. *Evening (~600).* Kiyoshi, 16, walks into the design bay at the end of the day, as he has since he was small, and tells his father he wants to join the cadet company. Kenji asks which branch he wants. Kiyoshi says the Legions, leading a fireteam with automata. Kenji says Tachibana's instructors will test him hard on it, and that he would have picked the same. Kaguya arrives to collect them both for dinner. She tells Kiyoshi the cadet commandant already expects him on the next intake list, since she saw this coming a month ago. At the family table with Ayame, Kenji laughs and steals half Kaguya's sea bream, and she lets him.

*How to write it:*

- Slow gear throughout.
- Open on Kiyoshi walking in without knocking, which no one remarks on (decision).
- Kiyoshi comes to his father easily and is answered easily. The family is warm and has no tension.
- Kiyoshi says *Chichi-sama*. Kenji's branch question is the practical question he asks about any job, and it is the same question he asks in A2.6.
- Kaguya runs the household and is a step ahead of everyone in it. She never asks about sleep and never mentions dreams.
- Keep the family table short and physical: the bowls, Ayame, the bream, Kenji laughing (decision).
- The bream is the second half of the chapter's single past-life hint. Kenji's pleasure in it counts against the same three-sentence limit as scene 1.
- Close on warmth with no hook. End on Kaguya letting him take the fish.

*What it does for the segment:* It closes the segment on the E ending the plan calls for, a moment of warmth with no hook. It returns the setting to the design bay where scene 1 began, brings Kaguya into the segment, and completes the shift from work, to policy, to home.

*What it does for the story:* It sets up Kiyoshi's arc as a ground soldier, including his posting request in A2.6, and establishes the warm family baseline that the later succession tension is measured against. It also shows Kaguya's role as the household's steady hand, and it closes on the thing the Empire is ultimately protecting: an ordinary family dinner.

****A1.2. Audit-Marshal Sano Mitsuko (~29, 2131, Hakata port, Kyushu) (decision). Action. Ending W. (~1,500)**

1. *Warehouse before dawn (~550).* Sano stands in the dark of the Hakata customs warehouse beside a door post where an Ashigaru-4 stands with its slits dimmed. Three days ago she flagged this shipment, when the freight log and the declared cargo stopped matching. She let the cargo in, and forty minutes ago five crewmen came through the side loading door to collect one crate. She shut that door behind them and sealed it with a Directorate key, and the sentinels took their posts at every door. The crew has not yet worked out that the building is closed. Kurosawa arrives after the doors are held and stops off-angle from the entrance, out of the light. Sano gives him the crate's location, the crew count and the weapons count in three sentences, and he nods once. She has already marked the armed man: the stocky one on the left, whose coat hangs heavier on the right side (decision). Kurosawa steps into the open and offers the crew one chance to set the crate on the floor and walk away from it. While he speaks, Sano moves along the wall in the dark until she is four paces from the armed man (decision). Kurosawa asks the crew nothing else. The crew leader asks by what authority. Kurosawa names the Kansatsu-In and the 2119 Edict, and the relay cores in the crate. He repeats the offer once. The armed man's right hand goes into his coat.

*How to write it:*

- Slow gear through the whole scene.
- Open on the sealed side door and the sentinel beside it, slits dimmed, with Sano's key still in her hand.
- Sano set the trap and knows it.
- Sano's report is three plain sentences: the crate's location, five crewmen, one weapon (decision). Kurosawa nods once and says nothing to her.
- Sano has marked which man is armed and keeps her eyes on his right hand. She closes the distance in the dark while Kurosawa talks, so she is already in reach when the trigger comes.
- Kurosawa stays off-angle from the light until he steps out to make the offer. His voice stays even, and he never raises it.
- The offer is a few plain sentences, not a speech. He asks the crew nothing, and answers the crew leader's question about authority with the bureau's name, the Edict and the cargo.
- The sentinels hold their posts in complete silence.
- End on the armed man's hand going into his coat.

*What it does for the segment:* It is the first Action segment of the batch, and it opens on a physical detail (the sealed door, the key in Sano's hand). It builds the trap slowly, so the armed man's hand going into his coat in scene 2 lands as the trigger for fast gear. It also plants the first "small thing wrong" in a room: a cargo that did not match its log.

*What it does for the story:* It introduces the Compliance Directorate in action. It shows Kurosawa's method (he arrives after the trap is shut and offers one clean chance), the Ashigaru-4 holding posts in silence, and the 2119 Edict being enforced. It establishes Sano as a competent junior Audit-Marshal, a recurring POV, who sets traps rather than chasing and who marks the armed man and positions herself against him before anything happens.

2. *The fight (~320, fast gear).* The armed man's right hand is barely inside his coat when Sano is already moving. She has watched that hand since she counted the crew, and she starts on the first inch of his shoulder. Kurosawa, who watches hands, raises his own before the pistol is out, and the sentinels move for the first time. The ones nearest the crew pin the other four, who are unarmed, and the rest stay at the doors. Sano reaches the armed man as the pistol's grip clears the lining. She does not draw, because a shot here could hit the crate and she wants him alive (decision). She takes his wrist, turns it until the pistol drops, and cuffs him, with the cuffs in her grip as the body detail. The pistol never fires, and no one on the Directorate's side is hurt. The sentinels then stand the four up on their knees in a row facing the crate, within earshot of Kurosawa (decision), and step back to their posts.

*How to write it:*

- Fast gear starts at the hand entering the coat. Short sentences, one action each: the hand, her step, the wrist, the cuffs.
- Neither officer is surprised and neither is late. Sano moves on the first inch of the shoulder, before the pistol is clear, and Kurosawa signals before it is out.
- Sano's reason for not drawing is one plain thought about the crate.
- The cuffs are in her grip from the moment she moves. The body detail is the cold steel against her palm.
- Kurosawa does not move or speak. His only action is the raised hand.
- The sentinels stay silent until Kurosawa signals, then move with the clack of clamps and amber slits. They pin and do not strike.
- The pistol never fires. There are five crewmen and one pistol: Sano cuffs the armed man, and the sentinels take the other four.
- No taunts and no speeches.
- End on the four kneeling in a row and the quiet hall, with Kurosawa walking to the crate.

*What it does for the segment:* It is the segment's only fast-gear scene, and it is over in seconds because both officers saw the threat before it moved. It gives the batch its one physical burst early, so the rest of Batch 1 can stay quiet. It keeps the plan's Action weighting without a P ending, since the segment ends on W.

*What it does for the story:* It shows how Imperial force works: the sentinels do not move until Kurosawa signals, and when they move, they do so fluidly and with such assured restraint that they cause no injury unless told otherwise. Both Directorate officers watch the room, so Kurosawa signals before the pistol is clear. Sano's choice not to draw, to protect the crate and keep the man alive, is her own judgment.

3. *The manifest (~600).* Kurosawa lifts the manifest from the crate lid and reads it in silence, standing off the light. Sano, who flagged this cargo three days ago, stands close enough to see the page. The seal line carries a Sasebo customs mark, though the route lists no stop at Sasebo, and the registry timestamp sits four hours behind the dock log she holds (decision). The crew leader, kneeling in the row, asks where the Directorate takes what it seizes. Kurosawa does not look up. He reads to the last line, closes the manifest, and puts it inside his coat. He tells Sano to take the five into custody, and she answers that she will. He leaves through the nearest door, and the sentinel there steps aside and resumes its post. The door closes. The crew leader asks Sano the same question, and she does not answer him.

*How to write it:*

- Slow gear.
- Kurosawa reads in silence and explains nothing to Sano. She is the junior who flagged the cargo, so she reads the page herself.
- The seal and the timestamp are two plain facts on the page: a Sasebo mark with no Sasebo stop on the route, and a registry stamp four hours behind the dock log. State them plainly in Sano's observation.
- The crew leader, who asked about authority in scene 1, asks where seized goods go. Kurosawa's silence is shown only by his not looking up. No jaw lock, no tell.
- The order is one sentence: "Take the five into custody, Sano-san." Sano answers "Yes, Kurosawa-sama."
- He takes the manifest with him and says nothing else. The Directorate leaves no paper behind.
- End plainly on Sano not answering the crew leader's repeated question.

*What it does for the segment:* It is the quiet scene after the fight, and it carries the batch's echo: a seal and a log that don't match. It ends on the W ending the plan calls for. The crew leader's question goes unanswered by Kurosawa and by Sano.

*What it does for the story:* It shows how Kurosawa works. He reads the evidence alone, takes the manifest, hands the prisoners to a subordinate, and leaves. It shows Sano as a junior who already knows her case, so neither character has to explain anything to the other. It also plants two facts, a Sasebo seal on a route with no Sasebo stop and a registry stamp four hours late, which point toward a Japanese port office.

**A1.3. Capt. Han Jae-won (South Korea, ~33, 2131, DMZ ridge) (decision). Intrigue. Ending E. (~1,500)**

1. *Ridge (~600).* Han's thumb sinks into a soft pour under a skin of frost, on the stretch of the line farthest from the road. He tells the Seoul staff colonel the cold stopped it setting. The colonel, standing on finished concrete, says the stretch should be held by the treaty-supplied Helios-2 automata, since they are expendable and concrete will not cure before spring. Han answers that bunkers hold ground and machines in a gap hold only until the first breach. The colonel does not touch the pour. He ends the argument by telling Han that an Imperial Legion advisor runs a night drill and Han will attend. Han says he will.

*How to write it:*

- Slow gear throughout.
- Open on the thumb sinking into the pour.
- Name the left flank plainly as the unfinished stretch (decision).
- Each speaker gets two or three sentences per turn, with a reason in each.
- The colonel stays on finished concrete and never touches the pour. Han crouches, keeps his voice level, and disagrees anyway.
- Two Helios-2 automata stand still at the stretch, slits dim (decision).
- The colonel's contempt is aimed at the machines as cheap fill. The staff's "cowardice" mutter belongs to scene 2.
- End plainly on Han saying he will attend the drill.

*What it does for the segment:* It opens the segment on a physical detail that raises a question (why is the pour soft?) and answers it in the scene. It places the left flank before scene 3 needs it, and it states both doctrines as two people's reasons. Scene 2's drill arrives as an order, so the segment moves from argument to demonstration to decision.

*What it does for the story:* It introduces Han as a stubborn, practical officer who disagrees with a superior without defiance. It shows the treaty-supplied Helios-2 automata as an unresolved doctrinal question inside an allied army, and it keeps Imperial involvement at the level of a requested advisor. It also gives the batch its "small thing wrong in the room": a soft pour on the one stretch that most needs to hold.

2. *Night drill (~600).* On the practice ground below the ridge, a marker shell bursts on the lead Helios-2 automaton and paints its chest red, and the automaton keeps walking. The four-automaton fireteam crosses the pre-sighted lane with the barrage falling around it. Han stands beside the Imperial Legion advisor, a lieutenant in a plain field coat who says only what the drill requires. The barrage lifts when the machines reach the far berm. Forty meters behind them, the Korean NCOs cross the same lane and come out with no dye on any helmet. Two Seoul staff officers behind Han mutter that sending machines first is cowardice. Han asks the advisor how far behind the machines the NCOs must stay. The advisor says forty meters, and that the machines go on to the next berm only when a person is behind them. Han asks him to put the interval on the drill sheet. The advisor writes it and hands it over, and Han folds it into his coat.

*How to write it:*

- Slow gear throughout. The marker bursts are loud and bright but nothing in the scene is a danger to anyone.
- Open on the red dye landing on the lead automaton, which keeps walking.
- The result is countable: dye on the machines, none on the NCOs' helmets (decision).
- The advisor is a lieutenant in a plain field coat with no name given (decision). He answers in one or two plain sentences and never mentions Imperial secrets or Imperial equipment beyond the drill.
- The automata stay silent and still until the fireteam leader orders them forward. They carry no Imperial markings (decision).
- The two staff officers are Seoul staff, not the colonel from scene 1. Their mutter is one low exchange that Han hears and does not answer.
- Han asks one real question. His thumb, still grey from the pour, rests on the rail while he asks it (decision).
- Han is respectful and not awed. He addresses the advisor by rank, nods once, and takes the sheet without comment.
- End plainly on Han folding the drill sheet into his coat.

*What it does for the segment:* It is the demonstration between the argument in scene 1 and the order in scene 3. It turns both doctrines into something countable: machines absorb the dye, and the NCOs come out clean. It also gives Han the one fact scene 3 uses, the forty-meter interval, and it stages the staff's "cowardice" mutter that scene 1 left for it.

*What it does for the story:* It shows the Imperial role as an advisor running a drill at an ally's request, with no secret shown and no flag on the field. It states the half of the doctrine the Seoul staff reject, machines forward with a living NCO behind them, which answers the colonel's idea of leaving machines alone in a gap. It also keeps the Korean army's pride and its pragmatism in the same room.

3. *Order (~300).* The drill sheet lies unfolded on the order form, with the forty-meter interval written on it in the advisor's hand. Han has the pen. The staff colonel stands on finished concrete beside the table. He asks why Han is putting a single fireteam on the left flank instead of leaving the machines there alone, as he said they should be. Han answers that the machines hold the gap only while a person stands behind them. The left flank is the unfinished stretch. One sergeant from Han's own company will stand forty meters behind the four Helios-2 automata, and the bunkers on the finished stretch stay manned (decision). The colonel looks at the form and does not touch it. He says nothing more. Han signs the order. The form has one signature line, and it is his (decision). He blows on the ink until it dries (decision).

*How to write it:*

- Slow gear throughout.
- Open on the drill sheet unfolded on the order form, with the advisor's handwriting (decision).
- The advisor is absent (decision). Han uses the sheet alone.
- Say plainly that the left flank is the unfinished stretch.
- The colonel is the same Seoul staff colonel from scene 1. He stays on finished concrete and never touches the pour or the form.
- The exchange runs two or three sentences per turn, with a reason in each. Both men use rank titles. The doctrinal argument sits in the dialogue, with no narrator explaining it.
- Han is stubborn, practical and respectful without being awed. He disagrees without defiance, as in scene 1.
- The colonel's silence after Han's answer is his only reply (decision).
- Han signs his own line, the only line on the form (decision).
- End on the ink drying, with no stated feeling and no hook.

*What it does for the segment:* It completes the sequence of argument, demonstration and decision. The colonel's idea (machines hold the gap) and Han's idea (people hold ground) both appear in the order. The scene closes on a quiet physical moment, as the E ending calls for.

*What it does for the story:* It shows Han as an officer who decides on his own authority and signs his own name. It places one trial fireteam on the unfinished left flank, with a living sergeant behind four treaty-supplied automata. It also shows the colonel disagreeing and saying nothing more.
