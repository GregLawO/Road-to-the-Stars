# Specific Rules (trimmed for A2 and A3)

Rules specific to this story's chapters, dates, tiers, and secrets. Character rules live in the character profiles. Trimmed: later-chapter casting, 2125 count, patron-seat details, Reaper-era items. Style rules live in General_Writing_Rules.md.

**Status labels.** An entry the author states or approves is fact. (inference) is a consequence of settled facts. (open) is unsettled. (author ruling) supersedes earlier text. (decision) is my choice, open to overrule.

---

## 1. Process and format

- **Order.** The story is written chapter by chapter. No fixed word count.
- **Labels.** Chapters are A1, A2, and so on. Micro-chapters are single-POV pieces coded A1.1, A1.2. "Excerpt" means only the quoted epigraph that opens a chapter.
- **Published canon.** A1 is being rewritten to plant the A2 changes; Kenji stays unnamed and without POV in A1. A2.1 to A2.15 are drafted and canon (7 November 2026): A2a Final, A2b v6, A2c v6. A2.16 onward and A3 are not drafted. The first release is the first batch of A2 (A2.1 to A2.5).
- **Chapter sizes.** A2 has 20 micro-chapters in four batches of 5. A3 has 10 in two batches.
- **Date-order exceptions.** A3.1 (2062) leads A3 as a step back to A1.5. A2.7 is Vance's ledger (2051 to summer 2053), ordered by its start date. A2.9 (April 2055 to November 2056) is ordered by its start date and runs past A2.10 and A2.11.
- **Micro-chapter length (author ruling, 9 November 2026).** At most 1,500 words. May hold two or three dated scenes in one POV.
- **Chapter excerpt header format (author ruling).** Each excerpt comes from Excerpt_Bank.md and sits at the very top, above any title or byline:
  1. The excerpt's title only, as a heading, no code and no "Excerpt" label.
  2. The excerpt text in italics inside quotation marks.
  3. An attribution line: a dash, then *The Road to the Stars*. No author named.
- **Saving a draft.** When a micro-chapter is added to a chapter that already has a draft, write the whole chapter file (existing micro-chapters unchanged, new one appended). Put drafts in `/mnt/user-data/outputs/`, call `present_files`, and tell the author the draft is not yet in the Project.
- **Revision commentary** does not belong in the outline or other story files.

## 12. Secrecy and reveal discipline

- **Reaper secrecy.** Only Kenji, Hannah, Brooks, and Alexandra (from her twenty-first birthday) know of the Reapers, and they learn it from him much later. No chapter, document, or character outside the inner circle may refer to an unnamed enemy that the drills are for.
- **The conspiracy (Cerberus).** Cerberus hears of the technology in 2055 and wants the technologies he lets spread. It cannot find or take a Level 2 pod. The incidents are the physician's offer (Nov 2055), three offers to buy Oyama's patents (Jan, Apr, Oct 2058), and the forged letter that closes Galveston (Oct 2061). The raids (Dec 2056, Aug 2057, early 2058) and the assault (spring 2059) are a rival elite's, not Cerberus's. There is no 2056 shakedown (A2.8): hospital chains and others only send inspection requests and complaints. The network finds one organization but not who leads it, and Brooks takes the file in 2062. No chapter shows the network knowing who leads it before the outline says so.
- **How the name is learned (Act_I_Outline, A2.9 onward).** An operative who traced the elite patients' remembered contact to Whitcombe and Marchetti calls his group Cerberus in A2.18 (2060) and tries to turn them. It is not learned from money trails, documents, or a captured raider. The story calls it "Cerberus" or "they," never "it."
- **Patient memory (decision, 5 November 2026).** Every Level 2 patient except recruits, managers, and the found family remembers nothing of who, how, where, when, or why, other than one contact person. A patient remembers being treated and may talk about anything else. No treated patient shows knowledge of the five details even under pressure. The seal is named only where a POV who knows of it is present.
- **In-universe documents** never contain the conspiracy, the true cause of the 2065 withdrawals, or the truth of the 2125 killing. POVs may show the truth where they could know it.
- **No human meets an alien, and the Citadel appears nowhere.**

## 13. Network, robots, and technology

- **Security (author ruling, revised 3 November 2026).** The network begins in summer 2051 with Vance, who brings in Ashby. Vance's platoon wears Pellam's AEMS suits (the powered suits come later; date open). From autumn 2052 private sites are also guarded by Level 2 security droids built by Oyama, run by Level 2 Dumb AI on Kenji's data crystals, and they guard the pods. From 2056 Level 3 droids guard every Level 2 pod around the clock, no pod moves without Kenji knowing, and a pod taken from its guards destroys its own core (author ruling, 5 November 2026; the 2056 date is a decision, 9 November 2026). Droids stand inside private sites only. Droids do not patrol (author ruling, 9 November 2026). At night they move to assigned spots (walls, entrances, key points) and stand still, observing their sectors. They move only to take a post or to answer something, and they make noise only when they move. Two Level 5 droids are with Kenji at all times from summer 2053. They look and move like men. They are told to act low-key in public. Oyama sells Level 1 to Level 3 robots to the public.
- **The network** is founded in summer 2051 with Vance (A2.1 hook, A2.7). Recruitment order: Vance, Ashby, the lawyers (October 2051), a larger team, Byron (July 2052). It exists before Kenji meets the Shepards (2053), and the three clinics (Galveston, Marseille, Singapore) open in summer 2053.
- **Operatives carry no POV**, apart from Vance and, in his fight chapter, Danny.
- **The Foundation runs on Dumb AI (author ruling, 9 November 2026).** From A2.6 (December 2053) almost everything the Foundation uses has a Dumb AI assistant, and human members mostly validate, by signature. Clinics run administrative AI, yards run design and production AI, and ships carry an onboard AI for navigation and ship status that can also fly the ship if needed. Med-Pod AIs are exempt. A failure in a record or a run is a failure of validation by a person, never of the AI. Each AI keeps to its one task (A2.6) and does not act outside it.
- **Tier examples for robots (author ruling):**
  - **Primitive (Level 1):** immobile, or needs a human controller.
  - **Level 2:** acts on its own, but moves clankily and loudly. Most advanced AI it supports is Level 2.
  - **Level 3:** moves more smoothly, still very loud. Most advanced AI it supports is Level 3.
- **Tiers.** Kenji starts the tier system in A2.1 (he builds no Primitive pod; Oyama does after July 2052): a Primitive grade for what may leave the room, a higher level for what stays. The Primitive grade must always be one the world could have invented unaided.
- **Warp.** Flights before the first crewed crossing are unmanned and private. The first crewed crossing beyond the Solar System is set in 2064 (no chapter in the current outline).
- **Unconfirmed tier tables.** The Med-Pod and Anti-Gravity levels (public L1, private L2) are decisions pending the author's definitions.

## 20. Names for the platoon and its ranks

- **No Foundation member uses "fighter" to address a fellow member (author ruling, 9 November 2026).** The word is out of members' speech and, in the drafts, out of the narration as well. Rank and file are "operators." The unit stays "the platoon," Vance's own word. Use the labels below for the corporate side.
- The labels below are the author's reference list (9 November 2026). They are the register the story's corporate world uses for combat personnel.

### 1. Corporate euphemisms (the polite labels)
Megacorporations rarely call their combatants "soldiers." They use clinical, bureaucratic language to keep a veneer of legality and professionalism.
- **Risk Mitigation Specialists.** Sounds like an insurance team; actually heavily armed tactical intervention squads.
- **Asset Protection Officers.** Implies they guard buildings, but functionally acts as a private army or heavy infantry.
- **Corporate Liaison / Field Enforcers.** Individuals deployed to "settle disputes" or to force client and competitor compliance.
- **Human Capital Security.** A sterile, chilling way to classify the department responsible for kinetic operations.

### 2. Tactical and operational labels
If the corporation is openly militaristic, the titles mimic elite military units but keep corporate operational phrasing.
- **Operators.** Inherited from real-world special forces. Fits corporate settings to denote highly skilled, contract-based combatants.
- **Tactical Specialists / Consultants.** Individuals hired for specific, high-risk missions (for example corporate espionage extraction, wetwork).
- **Response Teams (CRT / ERT).** Crisis Response Teams or Emergency Response Teams, the corporate equivalents of SWAT or rapid-deployment forces.
- **Kinetic Solutions Division.** The department name itself says its job is physical, violent intervention.

### 3. Hierarchical structures and ranks
Instead of traditional military ranks (Sergeant, Captain), corporate militaries adapt corporate hierarchies to command structures.

| Traditional military role | Corporate combat equivalent |
|---|---|
| Squad Leader / NCO | Team Lead / Project Supervisor |
| Platoon / Company Commander | Operations Manager / Director of Security |
| General / Commander | Vice President of Global Security / Chief Security Officer (CSO) |
| Private / Frontline Soldier | Associate / Field Agent / Operator |

### 4. Slang and informal corporate labels
Within the fiction, employees in regular corporate branches (HR, Accounting, Marketing) would likely have distinct shorthand for the combat personnel.
- **"Suits with Guns" / "The Janitors."** Clean-up crews sent to erase corporate mistakes or eliminate whistleblowers.
- **"The Expense Report."** Cynical slang for mercenaries or high-casualty frontline units whose losses are factored into the quarterly budget.
- **"Auditors."** A double entendre for a combat team sent to aggressively "audit" a rival facility.
