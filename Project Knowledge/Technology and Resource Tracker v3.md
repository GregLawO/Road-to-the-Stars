# TECHNOLOGY AND RESOURCE TRACKER — ROAD TO THE STARS 

**Document Status:** Revised Working Canon  
**Purpose:** Track the development, testing, production, and deployment of Akatsuki's technologies alongside the industrial resources required to support them.

This document prevents technological progress from becoming instantaneous or detached from practical constraints. It complements the Master Story Bible and Technology Reference by tracking when capabilities become available and what is required to support them.

---

# 1. Development Principles

- Knowing a technology's underlying principles does not mean having a finished design.
- A successful prototype is not automatically a reliable production system.
- Production requires materials, components, facilities, trained personnel, quality control, maintenance, and supply chains.
- Technologies must be tested under realistic operating conditions before widespread deployment.
- Research, prototypes, commercial products, and operational military systems must be distinguished.
- Automation increases productivity but does not eliminate energy, material, maintenance, or infrastructure requirements.
- New capabilities should emerge through credible development rather than appearing whenever the plot needs them.
- Akatsuki's development must reflect the combined contributions of its researchers, engineers, employees, AI systems, and industrial workforce.

# 2. Technology Development Priorities

The broad development sequence is:
1. Medical technology and Med-Pods.
2. Specialized non-sapient AI.
3. Advanced robotics and droids.
4. Gravitic technology.
5. Spacecraft and independent FTL.
6. Advanced weapons and military systems.

These areas can overlap as Akatsuki's research and industrial capabilities expand.

Development priorities do not imply that one field must be completely finished before work begins in another.

# 3. Medical Technology

## 3.1 Med-Pods

- Derived from Kenji's knowledge of advanced medical technology.
- Initial development focuses on medical imaging, treatment systems, and increasingly sophisticated automated medical procedures.
- Development must account for biological variation, patient safety, diagnostic reliability, and clinical validation.
- Medical systems require qualified human oversight where clinical judgment is necessary.
- Med-Pod technology later provides the foundation for advanced tissue engineering and biological components.

## 3.2 Early Medical Development

In 2137, Kenji develops an early medical scanner and a bench-scale treatment system.

Established limitations from Chapters 1–5:
- Ultrasound penetration is approximately 3% shallow relative to the intended target.
- Lateral resolution is approximately 2.5 mm against a 1 mm clinical target.
- The system cannot reliably resolve 2–4 mm targets.
- Image fusion remains unproven.
- Shadowing has not been adequately tested.
- The carriage is not yet suitable for hospital-grade disinfection.
- Electrical isolation and leakage-current issues require correction.
- The scanner can support screening but cannot independently make clinical diagnoses.
- The closure workflow requires clinician examination, irrigation, and attestation.

The early system must not be portrayed as a fully operational Med-Pod.

## 3.3 Development Restrictions

- Early prototypes must retain their established limitations.
- Improvements require testing and validation.
- Medical technology must not become a universal solution to every injury or disease.
- Advanced tissue engineering must develop progressively from the underlying medical technology.

# 4. Specialized AI

## 4.1 Architecture

Akatsuki develops highly capable, specialized AI systems.

- Each AI is optimized for a defined role.
- Fundamental core architecture, specialization, and core operating instructions are immutable after commissioning.
- Operational objectives, permissions, parameters, and external reference databases can be adjusted through authorized controls.
- New capabilities requiring changes to fundamental architecture require a newly designed core.
- AI systems coordinate through authenticated interfaces.
- Specialized systems can disagree because of different objectives, assumptions, available data, and risk tolerances.
- AI systems are not omniscient general-purpose intelligences.

## 4.2 Corporate Deployment

By 2148:
- AI and droids perform the overwhelming majority of routine corporate and industrial work.
- AI systems exercise delegated authority within defined limits.
- High-consequence, exceptional, strategic, legal, and political decisions remain subject to human authority and accountability.
- Human employees remain important in research, leadership, judgment, external relationships, negotiations, and selected service roles.

Automation does not eliminate the need for capable human employees.

## 4.3 Security

Akatsuki's AI security architecture includes:
- Compartmentalization.
- Cryptographic authentication.
- Hardware-enforced restrictions.
- Network isolation.
- Independent verification of high-consequence commands.
- Continuous anomaly monitoring.
- Controlled recovery and isolation procedures.
- Restricted access between AI specializations.

These protections are substantially ahead of contemporary human industry but are not infallible.

Security must account for both unauthorized access and legitimate systems making incorrect decisions based on flawed information.

# 5. Robotics and Droid Architecture

## 5.1 Modular Architecture

A droid consists of two principal elements.

**Chassis**

- Body structure.
- Locomotion.
- Manipulators.
- Sensors.
- Power systems.
- Task-specific equipment.

**AI Core**

- Specialized intelligence.
- Role-specific operating knowledge.
- Authorized functions and permissions.

Compatible droids can change roles by receiving different AI cores, provided the chassis has the physical capabilities and equipment required.

Core transfers require authentication and integrity checks.

A new AI core cannot give a chassis capabilities its hardware lacks.

## 5.2 Industrial Deployment

By 2148, droids perform most routine manufacturing, logistics, maintenance, and other operational tasks.

Their deployment still requires:
- Manufacturing capacity.
- Replacement parts.
- Energy.
- Maintenance facilities.
- Diagnostics.
- Quality assurance.
- Secure core management.

Droids must remain part of the industrial resource and maintenance calculations.

## 5.3 Personal Battle Droids

Kenji's secret personal battle droids are a distinct, specialized class.

Established concepts:
- Human-sized humanoid mechanical combat platforms.
- Human-looking external appearance.
- Genuine organic components developed using Med-Pod-derived technology.
- Mechanical internal construction.
- Specialized immutable combat AI cores.
- Accumulated combat experience from veteran contributors.
- Neural command interface through which Kenji issues orders.
- Tactical autonomy in executing assigned protective and combat objectives.

They are not ordinary industrial droids or substitutes for a professional military.

Human security personnel remain part of Kenji's protection arrangements.

Their number, individual identities, development schedule, and first deployment remain unresolved.

# 6. Memory and Experience Transfer

Kenji intends to develop technology capable of recording, preserving, and transferring selected memories and practical experiences.

The concept includes:
1. Recording selected experiences from a consenting contributor.
2. Preserving those experiences in a structured information format.
3. Integrating validated experience packages into compatible AI systems through a separate protected memory subsystem.

This technology does not automatically transfer consciousness, personality, or identity.

Veteran contributors provide the combat experiences that can later be incorporated into specialized battle droids. Healthcare is part of the intended exchange.

## 6.1 Limitations

- Human memories may be incomplete or distorted.
- Contributors may disagree about the same events.
- Historical tactics may become obsolete.
- Experience is not equivalent to omniscience.
- Integrated information must be validated and tested.
- The receiving AI retains its original immutable core and specialization.

The technology's development milestones and operational date remain unresolved.

# 7. Gravitic Technology

Gravitic technology is a major long-term technological advantage and the foundation of Akatsuki's advanced spacecraft architecture.

## 7.1 Capabilities

- Inertial manipulation.
- Gravitational field shaping.
- Artificial gravity.
- Gravitic shielding.
- Reactionless sublight propulsion.
- Faster-than-light travel.
- Advanced weapons.

## 7.2 Established Constraints

- Maximum conventional inertial reduction is 90%, producing an effective inertial mass of 0.1m.
- Inertial reduction does not erase momentum or energy.
- It does not automatically guarantee structural integrity.
- Gravitic shields can interact with kinetic and energy attacks but do not automatically block every form of attack.
- Reactionless sublight propulsion depends on generator capacity, stability, navigation, power, and structural limits.
- Gravitic systems require reliable control, maintenance, and integration with other spacecraft systems.

Gravitic technology must not be treated as a universal solution to engineering problems.

## 7.3 Spacecraft Integration

Akatsuki spacecraft are built around gravitic technology rather than conventional Mass Effect propulsion and defensive systems with gravitic additions.

Established principles:
- Gravitic propulsion replaces conventional reaction-based sublight propulsion.
- Gravitic shields replace conventional Mass Effect kinetic barriers.
- Artificial gravity is provided through gravitic systems.
- Inertial manipulation protects crews and structures during acceleration and maneuvering.
- The Gravitic Spacetime Drive provides independent FTL.

Other technologies, including electromagnetic weapons and directed-energy weapons, remain compatible with this architecture.

# 8. Gravitic Spacetime Drive

Akatsuki's Gravitic Spacetime Drive (GSD) provides independent FTL without element zero or mass relays.

## 8.1 Performance Targets

|Generation|Target cruise speed|
|---|---|
|GSD-0|10c|
|GSD-1|50c|
|GSD-2|100c|
|GSD-3|250c|
|GSD-4|500c|
|GSD-5|1,000c|

These are performance targets, not guarantees that every vessel can achieve them under all conditions.

Actual performance depends on drive generation, vessel integration, operating conditions, and other limitations.

## 8.2 Development Timeline

- **2148–2150:** Experimental gravitic FTL frigate development and initial testing.
- **2151–2152:** Integration into additional spacecraft designs.
- **2153–2157:** Operational deployment and wider military-industrial production.
- **By 2157:** Akatsuki-developed FTL technology has reached the point where at least one or two complete Systems Alliance fleets can be equipped with it.

The precise ownership and operational relationship between these fleets and Akatsuki's own fleet remain unresolved.

## 8.3 Testing Requirements

- Controlled FTL activation.
- Short-range trials.
- Navigation and emergence accuracy.
- Extended drive operation.
- Emergency shutdown and recovery.
- Systems integration.
- Reliability and maintenance validation.
- Personnel training and operational doctrine.

Independent FTL does not automatically provide independent FTL communications.

# 9. Spacecraft Development and Fleet Composition

## 9.1 Established Ship Classes

Akatsuki's principal combat spacecraft classes are:

|Class|Length|General role|
|---|---|---|
|Corvette|250 m|Patrol, reconnaissance, escort, and light combat|
|Frigate|500 m|General combat, escort, and independent operations|
|Fast Battlecruiser|750 m|Heavy combat, rapid response, and interception|
|Line Cruiser|1,000 m|Major fleet combatant, command, and sustained operations|

These four classes form the foundation of Akatsuki's naval development.

## 9.2 2148 Fleet Status

Established position:
- Six operational 250-meter corvettes.
- One 500-meter frigate undergoing trials.
- The frigate is a technology demonstrator and future military vessel, not yet commissioned or combat-ready.
- The corvettes have real anti-piracy operational experience.
- Human military personnel participate in the frigate trials.

The larger spacecraft classes are still preliminary development ambitions at this stage.

Kenji discusses plans for the Fast Battlecruiser and Line Cruiser with Commander Kastanie Drescher during the frigate trials in Chapter 8.

These discussions establish long-term ambitions rather than completed designs or vessels already under construction.

## 9.3 2157 Fleet Baseline

Working fleet composition:

|Class|Number|
|---|---|
|Corvettes|8|
|Frigates|4|
|Fast Battlecruisers|2|
|Line Cruisers|1|
|**Total combat vessels**|**15**|

This total excludes auxiliary and support vessels.

**Important readiness distinction:**

The 1,000-meter Line Cruiser is undergoing advanced operational trials in Shanxi in 2157.

It must not automatically be described as fully commissioned or combat-ready.

The working baseline therefore represents:
- 14 operational combat vessels.
- One additional Line Cruiser undergoing trials.

The exact commissioning date of the Line Cruiser remains dependent on the results of its trials.

## 9.4 Support Fleet

The combat fleet requires additional vessels and infrastructure.

Supporting assets include:
- Replenishment ships.
- Repair and maintenance vessels.
- Transport ships.
- Medical support vessels.
- Survey and reconnaissance craft.
- Logistics and industrial support vessels.

These do not count toward the 15 combat vessels.

## 9.5 Naval Development Constraints

Spacecraft development requires:
- Structural fabrication.
- Precision components.
- Propulsion systems.
- Power generation and distribution.
- Thermal management.
- Sensors and communications.
- Navigation systems.
- Life support.
- Maintenance facilities.
- Trained human personnel.
- Supply chains and spare parts.

Akatsuki's industrial growth must support both civilian and military production.

# 10. Naval Armament and Combat Doctrine

## 10.1 Primary Offensive Weapons

Advanced torpedoes are the principal offensive weapons of Akatsuki's combat spacecraft.

Drones support:
- Reconnaissance.
- Target identification.
- Fire-control and targeting support.
- Independent combat operations.
- Torpedo delivery.

Torpedoes and drones are complementary systems rather than competing weapon categories.
## 10.2 Secondary Weapons

Secondary and defensive armament includes:
- Coil-gun turrets.
- Laser turrets.
- Point-defense systems.
- Other defensive weapons appropriate to the vessel.

The 750-meter Fast Battlecruiser and 1,000-meter Line Cruiser may also carry small spinal mass accelerators.

Corvettes and frigates do not require spinal accelerators.
## 10.3 Fleet Doctrine

Akatsuki favors coordinated long-range attacks using torpedoes and drones.

General principles:
- Larger combatants generally operate from the rear of the formation.
- Torpedo attacks are coordinated rather than relying exclusively on individual ship engagements.
- Drones extend reconnaissance and targeting capabilities.
- Smaller vessels provide escort, screening, patrol, and other forward operational functions.
- Larger ships provide heavier offensive capability and fleet coordination.

The doctrine draws selectively from historical naval concepts, especially coordinated torpedo attacks, without reproducing the assumptions and weaknesses of historical fleets.

Akatsuki's fleet has a distinct defensive and independent operational role. It is not simply another frontline formation of the Systems Alliance Navy.

Further weapons development may expand the fleet's capabilities as Akatsuki's military-industrial position grows.

# 11. Military Technology

By 2148, Akatsuki is an established defense contractor rather than a prototype laboratory.

## 11.1 Established Product Categories

Its products include:
- Sensors and surveillance systems.
- Electronic warfare.
- Targeting and fire control.
- Secure communications.
- Protective equipment and armor.
- Defensive robotics.
- Autonomous drones.
- Ship protection and early gravitic shielding.
- Propulsion and spacecraft subsystems.
- Directed-energy and kinetic weapons.
- Point-defense systems.

## 11.2 Commercial Maturity

Systems must have credible testing, manufacturing, maintenance, and customer histories before being treated as mature products.

By 2148, Akatsuki should have:
- Actual customers and contracts.
- Deployed equipment.
- Documented test records.
- Established production capacity.
- Maintenance and support arrangements.
- Credible supply chains.

Several products are commercially available and deployed rather than remaining prototypes.

Akatsuki's military capabilities must grow alongside its industrial base. It should not instantly possess a mature interstellar navy merely because it can build advanced spacecraft.

# 12. Shanxi Orbital Defense Systems

Akatsuki's Shanxi settlement receives a restricted orbital defense capability.

## 12.1 Established Deployment Limit

- Exactly two ODPS are authorized.
- Both are positioned directly above Akatsuki's settlement.
- No additional ODPS are authorized elsewhere in the Shanxi system.
- No third platform is included in the established plan.

The platforms protect Akatsuki's own settlement rather than constituting a general orbital defense network for the entire planet.

## 12.2 Development and Construction

The order to construct the ODPS is issued immediately after Kenji's first failed attempt to prevent or delay Relay 314 activation in Chapter 14.

The decision reflects his recognition that Akatsuki cannot control decisions made by independent human authorities and must improve its ability to protect its own people and assets.

## 12.3 Unresolved Details

The following remain open:
- Exact platform specifications.
- Weapon configuration.
- Construction duration.
- Commissioning dates.
- Operational staffing and maintenance arrangements.

Do not invent additional platforms or expand their deployment beyond the established restriction.

# 13. Industrial and Resource Constraints

Technology development and production depend on practical resources.

## 13.1 Essential Requirements

- Raw materials and refined feedstock.
- Precision manufacturing equipment.
- Semiconductor and computing infrastructure.
- Power generation and distribution.
- Specialized research facilities.
- Skilled engineers and scientists.
- Manufacturing and maintenance droids.
- Transport and logistics.
- Quality assurance.
- Capital and commercial relationships.

## 13.2 Automation Costs

AI and droid-heavy operations still require:
- Droid production and replacement.
- Component inventories.
- Maintenance and repair.
- Energy generation.
- Computing hardware.
- Secure communications.
- Manufacturing capacity.

Automation improves scale and efficiency; it does not create resources from nothing.

## 13.3 Industrial Growth

Akatsuki's industrial capacity must expand alongside its technological ambitions.

New facilities require:
- Construction materials.
- Industrial machinery.
- Power infrastructure.
- Logistics.
- Skilled personnel.
- Maintenance and replacement capacity.

The company cannot simultaneously expand every research field, manufacturing program, settlement, and military project without resource allocation and trade-offs.

# 14. Off-World Industrial Infrastructure

Established before 2148 as Akatsuki's major corporate settlement beyond Earth.

Its facilities include:
- Civilian habitats.
- Research laboratories.
- Manufacturing.
- Mining and resource processing.
- Spacecraft maintenance and construction.
- Power infrastructure.
- Transport and logistics.

Callisto serves as a major industrial and technological center during the earlier stages of Akatsuki's expansion.

## 14.2 Shanxi

Developed between 2150 and 2153 as a legitimate commercial and industrial settlement.

Its activities include:
- Resource extraction.
- Resource processing.
- Manufacturing.
- Spacecraft maintenance.
- Commercial shipping.
- Logistics.
- Research and development.
- Exploration support.

Akatsuki's settlement is geographically separate from the principal human settlement on Shanxi.

It is a genuine commercial and industrial presence, not a disguised military installation.

The two authorized ODPS are restricted to the immediate orbital area above Akatsuki's settlement.

## 14.3 Amatsu

Kenji names the planet Amatsu (天園), meaning "Heavenly Garden."

Its development proceeds progressively:
- **2151–2152:** Initial settlement and infrastructure preparation.
- **2151–2155:** Formal colonization, permanent habitation, and expansion of industrial and civilian infrastructure.
- **2155–2156:** Development into a substantial settlement with civilian communities, research, industry, and corporate administration.
- **By 2157:** A major, functioning colony with substantial infrastructure and a growing defensive capability.

Its established and developing facilities include:
- Civilian residential districts.
- Industrial and manufacturing facilities.
- Research and development centers.
- Spaceports and orbital infrastructure.
- Transport and logistics.
- Medical facilities.
- Administrative and corporate headquarters.
- Defensive and military installations.
- Shipbuilding, maintenance, and repair infrastructure.

Amatsu eventually becomes Akatsuki's principal headquarters, industrial center, and political heart.

It should not be portrayed as a fully developed planetary city or an overwhelmingly populated world immediately after settlement begins.

Its development must remain consistent with the time available.

# 17. Updating This Tracker

For each major technology or industrial program, record:
- Development stage.
- First successful demonstration.
- Important limitations.
- Production readiness.
- Deployment date.
- Industrial requirements.
- Maintenance and logistical requirements.
- Known vulnerabilities.
- Any changes to established performance assumptions.

Use consistent distinctions:
**Concept:** A proposed design or technical principle.
**Research:** Active investigation and development.
**Prototype:** An experimental physical implementation.
**Tested system:** A prototype or system that has passed specified tests.
**Production system:** A design manufactured to a repeatable standard.
**Operational system:** A deployed system with trained operators, maintenance, and support.
Never silently promote a concept or prototype into an operational capability.

# 18. Authoritative Continuity Rules

1. The Master Story Bible establishes the overall technological and industrial direction.
2. The Technology Reference establishes the accepted technical principles and capabilities.
3. This tracker records development stages, industrial requirements, and deployment chronology.
4. The Continuity Tracker records what has actually happened in the narrative.
5. New capabilities require credible development, testing, and production.
6. Industrial automation does not remove resource, maintenance, or logistical constraints.
7. Ship classes, fleet composition, and established military doctrine must remain consistent.
8. The two Shanxi ODPS restriction must be preserved.
9. Unresolved development dates and specifications must remain unresolved until established.
10. New technical details should be added only when they support the story or prevent a genuine continuity problem.

**Central Principle:** Akatsuki's technological advantage comes from sustained research, disciplined engineering, industrial expansion, and careful deployment—not from treating advanced technology as something that can be produced instantly or without cost.
