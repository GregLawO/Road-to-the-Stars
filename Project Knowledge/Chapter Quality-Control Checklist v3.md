# ROAD TO THE STARS

# CHAPTER QUALITY-CONTROL CHECKLIST

### 1. Purpose

This checklist provides a consistent standard for reviewing completed chapter drafts.

It evaluates narrative quality, characterization, pacing, prose, continuity, and compliance with the established project references.

A chapter does not need to be perfect in every category, but significant problems must be corrected before approval.

Every completed chapter must also comply with the project's manuscript terminology restrictions and include Author's Notes.

### 2. Narrative Structure

- Does the chapter accomplish its intended narrative objectives?
    
- Does it follow the approved chapter outline?
    
- Does it begin with an action, conversation, problem, decision, or significant event?
    
- Does the story progress meaningfully?
    
- Are scenes relevant to plot, characterization, relationships, or worldbuilding?
    
- Are transitions between scenes clear?
    
- Does the chapter end at an appropriate narrative point?
    
- Are major developments sufficiently developed rather than rushed?
    

### 3. Writing Style and Prose

- Is the chapter written in past tense?
    
- Is Kenji's narration consistently first person?
    
- Are other POV characters written in third person?
    
- Is POV switching clear and controlled?
    
- Does the prose follow the established simple mixed tone?
    
- Are descriptions concise and relevant?
    
- Is scenery introduced naturally rather than through lengthy introductory passages?
    
- Are internal monologues and introspection kept under control?
    
- Are emotions conveyed naturally through actions, dialogue, and reactions?
    
- Is the prose readable and free of unnecessary repetition?
    
- Does the chapter avoid padding and excessive exposition?
    
- Does the narrative read like a novel rather than a technical report or corporate presentation?
    

### 4. Characterization

- Does Kenji behave consistently with his established personality?
    
- Is he capable, confident, pragmatic, and proactive?
    
- Are his mistakes and limitations credible?
    
- Does he retain appropriate agency over his decisions?
    
- Are supporting characters given independent motivations?
    
- Do characters behave consistently with their backgrounds and experiences?
    
- Are reactions and decisions believable?
    
- Does the chapter avoid making supporting characters unnecessarily deferential to Kenji?
    
- Are character introductions and appearances relevant?
    
- Do supporting characters retain meaningful agency rather than functioning solely as instruments of Kenji's ambitions?
    

### 5. Dialogue

- Does dialogue sound natural?
    
- Does each character have a recognizable voice?
    
- Does dialogue serve a purpose?
    
- Are conversations appropriately paced?
    
- Is exposition conveyed through dialogue naturally?
    
- Are characters avoiding unnecessary repetition of information?
    
- Do conversations reflect the participants' knowledge, personalities, and relationships?
    
- Are disagreements and negotiations believable?
    
- Does dialogue avoid unnecessary technical jargon and artificial exposition?
    
- Do characters sound like people rather than information-delivery mechanisms?
    

### 6. Worldbuilding and Exposition

- Does the chapter follow established Mass Effect and alternate-universe continuity?
    
- Are new details consistent with the Story Bible?
    
- Is exposition relevant to the current narrative?
    
- Are established facts explained only when necessary?
    
- Does the chapter avoid unnecessary lore dumps?
    
- Are organizations and institutions portrayed with appropriate capabilities and limitations?
    
- Are political, commercial, and social reactions credible?
    
- Are historical events and character chronologies respected?
    
- Does the setting feel appropriately futuristic for its established timeframe?
    
- Are background developments conveyed efficiently without unnecessary dedicated scenes or chapters?
    

### 7. Technology and Resources

- Does every demonstrated technological capability have an established basis?
    
- Is technical knowledge distinguished from practical manufacturing capability?
    
- Are materials, facilities, personnel, and equipment sufficient for the demonstrated work?
    
- Are prototypes distinguished from validated products and mass production?
    
- Are development timelines respected?
    
- Are AI and robotics portrayed consistently with the Technology Reference?
    
- Are gravitic capabilities consistent with established principles?
    
- Are financial investments, acquisitions, and commercial developments plausible?
    
- Are maintenance, logistics, and operational requirements considered where relevant?
    
- Does the chapter avoid arbitrarily weakening established technology?
    
- Are AI systems portrayed as specialized, non-sapient intelligences?
    
- Are immutable AI cores distinguished from external information and experience packages?
    
- Are robotic capabilities consistent with the physical limitations of their chassis?
    
- Are technological advantages balanced by credible limitations and operational requirements?
    

### 8. Knowledge and Information Continuity

- Does each character know only what they have learned or reasonably established?
    
- Is Kenji's Mass Effect knowledge consistent with his casual-gamer baseline?
    
- Is organizational knowledge distinguished from Kenji's personal knowledge?
    
- Are confidential and classified information handled consistently?
    
- Are discoveries and intelligence transmitted through plausible channels?
    
- Are characters' assumptions distinguished from confirmed facts?
    
- Does the chapter avoid giving characters information they have no reason to possess?
    
- Are remembered events distinguished from verified information where appropriate?
    
- Does the chapter avoid treating Kenji's memories as infallible?
    

### 9. Relationships and Plot Threads

- Are established relationships reflected accurately?
    
- Are changes in trust, hostility, respect, or cooperation supported by events?
    
- Do characters retain independent interests?
    
- Are important promises and obligations preserved?
    
- Are active plot threads advanced when appropriate?
    
- Are unresolved issues preserved rather than forgotten?
    
- Are mysteries and future developments protected from premature resolution?
    
- Are new plot threads meaningful and manageable?
    
- Does the chapter preserve the balance between Kenji's professional ambitions and his personal relationships?
    

### 10. Alternate-Universe Continuity

- Does the chapter respect established AU divergences?
    
- Does it avoid reproducing canon events that no longer occur?
    
- Are canon characters adapted to their changed circumstances?
    
- Are character ages, careers, and timelines consistent?
    
- Is the Arca Monolith storyline correctly excluded?
    
- Is Jack Harper's avoidance of indoctrination through the Monolith incident preserved?
    
- Are First Contact War developments consistent with the established AU direction?
    
- Are future milestones treated as planned developments rather than events that have already happened?
    
- Are Amatsu's naming restrictions respected?
    
- Are pre-First Contact restrictions on direct alien interaction preserved?
    

### 11. Manuscript Terminology and Intellectual-Property Naming

- Does the chapter avoid non-Mass Effect franchise-specific names and terminology?
    
- Are franchise-specific classifications, designations, and technical names from unrelated properties excluded?
    
- Are inspired technologies described using generic, functional, or independently established terminology?
    
- Are underlying technological functions, capabilities, architecture, and limitations preserved where appropriate?
    
- Are private inspiration references kept out of narration, dialogue, character thoughts, and in-universe documents?
    
- Are established Mass Effect terms used appropriately?
    
- Are specific naming restrictions in the Master Story Bible respected?
    
- Are chapter titles and other published chapter material free of prohibited terminology?
    

Private planning references may retain source-franchise names as inspiration aids. Their presence in internal reference documents does not authorize their use in the published manuscript.

Any prohibited franchise-specific terminology appearing in chapter content must be corrected before approval.

### 12. Pacing and Reader Engagement

- Does the chapter maintain reader interest?
    
- Are important developments given sufficient attention?
    
- Are slower passages justified by characterization or necessary development?
    
- Are action scenes understandable and consequential?
    
- Are technical and business scenes engaging rather than report-like?
    
- Does the chapter avoid unnecessary delays before reaching its main events?
    
- Is the balance between action, dialogue, exposition, and reflection appropriate?
    
- Does the chapter avoid repetitive problem-diagnosis-solution sequences?
    
- Are major background developments handled efficiently?
    
- Does the chapter contribute to the larger narrative rather than feeling isolated?
    

### 13. Author's Notes

Every completed chapter must include an Author's Notes section after the narrative.

Check the following:

- Are Author's Notes present?
    
- Are they clearly separated from the fictional narrative?
    
- Do they explain the author's intention?
    
- Do they discuss the creative approach behind significant narrative choices?
    
- Do they communicate the intended reader takeaway?
    
- Are they written in a personal, conversational voice?
    
- Are they ideally two to three concise paragraphs?
    
- Do they offer insight rather than simply retelling the chapter?
    
- Do they avoid unnecessary future spoilers?
    
- Do they complement the narrative without overexplaining it?
    

Author's Notes are a permanent part of the chapter presentation, not an optional appendix.

They do not independently establish fictional canon. Continuity updates must be based on events and facts established in the approved narrative.

### 14. Final Editorial Review

Before approval, check:

- No major continuity contradictions.
    
- No unexplained character behavior.
    
- No unnecessary scenes or redundant passages.
    
- No unresolved drafting notes or placeholders.
    
- No accidental POV or tense changes.
    
- No important outline objectives omitted without authorization.
    
- No unauthorized changes to established canon.
    
- No prohibited non-Mass Effect franchise terminology in chapter content.
    
- No violations of established Mass Effect naming restrictions.
    
- Author's Notes are present and satisfy the required elements.
    
- No editorial commentary has been inserted into the fictional narrative.
    
- No obvious grammar, spelling, or punctuation problems.
    
- The chapter reads as a continuous part of the novel.
    

### 15. Review Classification

Use the following classifications when evaluating a draft.

|Classification|Meaning|
|---|---|
|Approved|Meets the project's requirements and can enter the manuscript.|
|Approved with minor corrections|Only small editorial changes remain.|
|Revision required|Significant issues must be corrected.|
|Major revision required|Structural, continuity, or characterization problems require substantial work.|
|Rejected|Fundamentally unsuitable and should be rewritten.|

A chapter should not be rejected merely because it contains an imperfect sentence or minor stylistic issue.

A subjective stylistic preference should not automatically be treated as a continuity or compliance failure.

Mandatory requirements, including manuscript terminology restrictions and Author's Notes, must be satisfied before final approval.

### 16. Review Report Format

When reviewing a chapter, use this format:

**Chapter:** Number and title.

**Overall assessment:** Brief evaluation of the chapter.

**Classification:** One of the five review classifications.

**Strengths:** What works particularly well.

**Issues requiring correction:** Significant problems, prioritized by importance.

**Minor editorial issues:** Optional refinements that do not prevent approval.

**Terminology and presentation compliance:** Confirm compliance with manuscript naming restrictions and Author's Notes requirements.

**Continuity updates:** Relevant changes to project trackers.

**Recommended action:** The next concrete step.

Avoid producing excessively long editorial reports for chapters that need only minor corrections.

Do not manufacture criticism merely to demonstrate that a review was performed. Distinguish genuine problems from optional stylistic improvements.

### 17. Governing Principle

The checklist exists to protect the quality and continuity of the novel, not to impose mechanical perfection or make every chapter conform to an identical structure.

**Prioritize compelling storytelling, credible characterization, coherent continuity, and readable prose over checklist completion for its own sake. Ensure that mandatory project requirements are respected without allowing editorial rigidity to undermine the novel.**
