# Chapter 8 — The Fleet We've Built

**Narrative Year:** 2148  
**Working Title:** The Fleet We've Built  
**POV:** First-person, Himura Kenji  
**Continuity Status:** Revised Working Brief

## Chapter Purpose

Show how far Akatsuki Technologies has progressed beyond its origins as a small technology business. The company now has a credible presence in the defense and aerospace industries, including an emerging spacecraft program.

The chapter should demonstrate that Akatsuki's military capabilities are the result of years of investment, engineering, testing, and institutional development—not simply Kenji introducing advanced technology.

It should also introduce Kastanie Drescher as a professional acquaintance whose relationship with Kenji begins through the frigate trials.

## Scene 1 — The Fleet We've Built

Establish Akatsuki's current spacecraft program and military-industrial position.

**Established fleet status in 2148:**

- Six operational 250-meter corvettes.
    
- One 500-meter frigate undergoing trials.
    
- The frigate is not yet commissioned or combat-ready.
    
- The corvettes have accumulated operational experience, including anti-piracy assignments.
    
- Akatsuki has established shipbuilding, maintenance, and support capabilities.
    

Show the practical infrastructure behind the ships: personnel, maintenance facilities, supply chains, testing procedures, and operational experience.

Avoid presenting the fleet as a fully mature navy. Akatsuki is a growing private military-industrial organization with credible capabilities, but it still has considerable room to develop.

## Scene 2 — The Frigate Trials

The 500-meter frigate is undergoing trials at Callisto.

Alliance Navy personnel are present as observers. Introduce **Kastanie Drescher** as one of the military observers.

Drescher should be a competent, professional officer with an independent perspective. She is not there to admire Akatsuki's technology or provide Kenji with automatic validation.

Her questions should address practical military concerns:

- Propulsion reliability and performance.
    
- Gravitic shield operation and limitations.
    
- Damage control and crew safety.
    
- Maintenance requirements and logistical support.
    
- Combat suitability.
    
- How the vessel would perform under realistic operational conditions.
    

Kenji answers honestly. He distinguishes between capabilities demonstrated during trials, capabilities still being evaluated, and future improvements that remain theoretical.

The trials should include both successful demonstrations and limitations or issues requiring further work. Avoid making the frigate appear flawless.

## Scene 3 — A Professional Acquaintance

Allow Kenji and Drescher to interact beyond formal introductions.

Their relationship should develop through professional discussion and mutual respect. Drescher recognizes that Akatsuki takes military reliability seriously; Kenji recognizes that she understands the difference between impressive technology and a useful warship.

They may disagree over design choices, operational assumptions, or the practical demands of military service.

Do not turn this into instant friendship, personal loyalty, or a promise of future Alliance support. Drescher has her own responsibilities and institutional obligations.

## Scene 4 — What Comes After the Frigate?

During a conversation about the trials and Akatsuki's future shipbuilding ambitions, Drescher asks whether the company intends to stop at frigates.

Kenji explains that the frigate is only one stage of a longer development plan.

Akatsuki is considering:

- Continued production and improvement of corvettes.
    
- A larger frigate program.
    
- A **750-meter Fast Battlecruiser** intended for heavier combat duties and fleet operations.
    
- A **1,000-meter Line Cruiser** intended to serve as a larger fleet-capable vessel.
    

These larger classes are preliminary ambitions, not completed designs or ships already under construction.

Drescher questions whether Akatsuki has the industrial capacity, workforce, financing, and supply chain to pursue such an ambitious program.

Kenji acknowledges the difficulties. He can explain that the company is planning ahead and building the industrial foundations required, but he should not pretend that the ships are guaranteed or easy to produce.

Keep this discussion relatively brief and conversational. Do not turn it into a technical specification presentation.

The future classes should be introduced as plans that will become relevant in later years. The Line Cruiser will eventually undergo trials in Shanxi in 2157, with Drescher—by then an Admiral—and elements of her fleet present.

## Scene 5 — Beyond the Trials

Conclude with the broader implications of Akatsuki's progress.

The trials represent more than a successful engineering milestone. They demonstrate that Akatsuki is becoming a serious participant in humanity's emerging space and defense industries.

Kenji can reflect on the distance between the small repair business he inherited and the organization he has built.

However, avoid making the ending excessively triumphant. The company has achieved a great deal, but its ambitions now exceed its existing industrial and organizational capabilities.

End with a sense of forward momentum and the understanding that the fleet being built today is only the beginning.

## Character and Continuity Requirements

### Himura Kenji

- Remains the company's strategic and technological leader.
    
- Does not personally command the fleet or conduct every aspect of its development.
    
- Relies on engineers, shipbuilders, military professionals, and corporate leadership.
    
- Demonstrates technical knowledge without becoming an infallible authority.
    

### Kastanie Drescher

- Introduced as an Alliance Navy military observer during the 2148 frigate trials.
    
- Is not yet an Admiral.
    
- Her exact 2148 rank should not be stated unless already established elsewhere in the manuscript or confirmed by the author.
    
- Is professional, observant, and capable of challenging Kenji.
    
- Develops familiarity with Kenji through professional interactions.
    
- Does not promise future Alliance support or make commitments on behalf of the Navy.
    

### Technology and Military Continuity

- Akatsuki spacecraft are built around gravitic technology as their technological foundation.
    
- Gravitic shields replace conventional Mass Effect kinetic barriers in Akatsuki designs.
    
- Independent FTL remains experimental in this period.
    
- Do not portray the frigate as operationally commissioned or combat-ready.
    
- Do not invent new performance figures or specifications without established support.
    

## Narrative Restrictions

- Preserve the first-person perspective.
    
- Maintain the established narrative voice and characterization.
    
- Avoid excessive technical exposition.
    
- Do not introduce future events prematurely.
    
- Do not imply that Akatsuki already possesses its later battlecruisers or line cruiser.
    
- Do not make Drescher a close personal friend or committed ally immediately.
    
- No non-Mass Effect franchise-specific names, terminology, designations, or references may appear in chapter prose, dialogue, narration, or in-universe documents.
