# Road to the Stars — Chapter 1 Drafting Brief

## Chapter 1: Getting Started

**Arc:** I — Foundations  
**Period:** 2137  
**Primary POV:** Himura Kenji, first person  
**Tense:** Past  
**Primary Setting:** Kenji's inherited house and mechanic shop  
**Estimated Structure:** 5 scenes  
**Chapter Function:** Introduce Kenji, establish his starting resources and limitations, select Med-Pods as his first major project, and begin the process of developing specialized AI.

---

## 1. Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, the reader should understand:

- Who Kenji is through his behavior and decisions.

- That he is twenty years old and lives independently.

- That he inherited his house and mechanic shop from his deceased mother.

- That he possesses considerable technical knowledge but has limited resources.

- That he intends to turn his knowledge into practical technology.

- Why he chooses Med-Pods as his first major project.

- Why he needs specialized AI to accelerate his development work.

The chapter should establish Kenji as intelligent, capable, practical, and proactive.

It should not portray him as someone who spends the entire day thinking about what he might accomplish. He should already be taking concrete steps.

---

## 2. Scene One — Another Day at the Shop

**Purpose:** Introduce Kenji through action and establish his practical circumstances.

**POV:** Kenji, first person.

**Setting:** His mechanic shop.

### Opening Situation

Open with Kenji already working on a vehicle.

He is diagnosing or repairing a mechanical problem that has resisted an initial attempt at resolution.

The opening should immediately establish activity. Avoid beginning with a description of the neighborhood, the building, the weather, or Kenji waking up.

### Events and Actions

- Kenji examines the problem.

- He identifies the actual cause rather than simply replacing components.

- He carries out the necessary repair or adjustment.

- The work demonstrates his mechanical competence and familiarity with the shop.

- If a customer is present, their interaction can establish that this is an operating business rather than an abandoned workshop.

The repair should be straightforward enough to establish competence without consuming the entire scene.

### Characterization

Show that Kenji:

- Understands mechanical systems.

- Approaches problems methodically.

- Is comfortable working with his hands.

- Is practical about earning money.

- Can interact normally with other people.

He should not be unnecessarily antisocial or dismissive toward customers.

### Background Information to Introduce Naturally

Through the scene, establish that:

- The shop belonged to his mother.

- She was an automotive mechanic.

- Kenji inherited it after her death.

- He currently operates it himself.

Do not interrupt the scene with a lengthy explanation of his family history.

### Conflict

The immediate mechanical problem provides a small practical challenge.

The larger underlying limitation is that the shop earns enough to maintain his independence, but its current business cannot support the much larger projects he intends to pursue.

### Scene Ending

Kenji completes the immediate job and turns his attention toward his own work.

The customer-facing business and his personal engineering ambitions coexist in the same small workshop.

**Transition:** With the immediate work finished, Kenji evaluates what he can realistically accomplish with his current resources.

---

## 3. Scene Two — Taking Stock

**Purpose:** Establish Kenji's financial and material starting position.

**POV:** Kenji, first person.

**Setting:** The workshop, followed by his work area.

### Opening Situation

Kenji reviews his available finances, equipment, and materials.

He is not conducting an elaborate business audit. He is determining what he can afford to do next.

### Events and Actions

- He reviews the shop's current financial position.

- He considers his personal expenses and available savings.

- He inventories useful equipment and materials.

- He identifies the workshop's strengths and weaknesses.

- He distinguishes between equipment he can adapt and equipment he must acquire.

- He considers how much time he can devote to research without abandoning the shop's existing income.

### Important Details

Kenji has:

- A house and workshop inherited from his mother.

- Basic mechanical and fabrication equipment.

- Some income from operating the shop.

- Limited capital for advanced research.

- No established research team or industrial manufacturing facilities.

His starting position is modest but not destitute.

He does not need to borrow money immediately or behave as though he has no means of supporting himself.

### Characterization

Demonstrate his pragmatism.

He understands that having knowledge of advanced technology does not automatically give him the equipment, materials, or infrastructure needed to reproduce it.

He is willing to start small and build progressively.

### Conflict

His ambitions exceed his current resources.

He must select a project that offers a realistic path toward greater financial and technological independence.

### Scene Ending

Kenji narrows his options and settles on the first technology he intends to develop.

**Transition:** The next scene establishes why Med-Pods are the logical starting point.

---

## 4. Scene Three — The First Project

**Purpose:** Establish Med-Pods as Kenji's first major technological undertaking.

**POV:** Kenji, first person.

**Setting:** His personal work area in the workshop.

### Opening Situation

Kenji examines the technologies he could potentially develop and considers which one should receive his immediate attention.

The decision should emerge from practical reasoning, not a lengthy internal monologue.

### Events and Actions

**1. Evaluate the available opportunities.**

Kenji considers the advantages and difficulties of beginning with different technological fields.

He recognizes that some technologies would require industrial facilities, specialized materials, or resources far beyond what he currently possesses.

**2. Select Med-Pods.**

He settles on medical technology.

Reasons include:

- Clear and substantial practical benefits.

- A large potential market.

- The possibility of developing a commercially valuable product.

- Opportunities to establish a foundation for further medical research.

- A plausible path toward generating the capital needed for more ambitious projects.

**3. Establish the initial scope.**

Kenji decides to focus on developing a functional Med-Pod rather than attempting to reproduce an entire advanced medical infrastructure at once.

He identifies the major systems that need to be developed and integrated.

**4. Organize the work.**

He begins creating technical notes, organizing the available information, and breaking the project into manageable tasks.

The details should be understandable to the reader without becoming an engineering textbook.

### Characterization

Kenji should display confidence in his chosen direction while remaining realistic about the work involved.

He knows what he wants to build, but he does not assume that the process will be effortless.

### Conflict

The technology is far beyond what his existing workshop was designed to produce.

He needs a way to accelerate research, calculations, design organization, and technical analysis.

### Scene Ending

Kenji identifies a major bottleneck: the amount of research and computational work required.

He decides that developing a specialized AI system should become one of his first supporting projects.

**Transition:** The next scene introduces his approach to building the tools needed for his larger undertaking.

---

## 5. Scene Four — A Tool for the Job

**Purpose:** Establish the decision to develop specialized non-sapient AI.

**POV:** Kenji, first person.

**Setting:** His work area.

### Opening Situation

Kenji begins examining the computational resources available to him.

He considers how to organize and accelerate the research process.

### Events and Actions

- He assesses his existing computer hardware and software.

- He identifies the computational limitations of his current setup.

- He outlines the functions he needs from a specialized AI system.

- He prioritizes engineering calculations, technical documentation, design assistance, data organization, and simulation support.

- He considers how to build the system incrementally using available hardware.

- He starts organizing the initial software architecture.

### Important Continuity Rules

The AI is specialized and non-sapient.

It is not:

- A conscious personality.

- A digital friend.

- An independent decision-maker with human-like motivations.

- An omniscient system capable of solving every problem.

It is an advanced tool that Kenji can develop and improve over time.

Do not introduce a lengthy philosophical discussion about AI consciousness.

### Characterization

Show Kenji's preference for automation through practical decisions.

He would rather build a system that can handle repetitive analytical work than spend unnecessary hours doing everything manually.

This does not mean he dislikes working with people. It means he values efficiency.

### Conflict

His current hardware is insufficient for everything he eventually wants the AI to do.

He must prioritize the initial capabilities and accept that more advanced functionality will require additional resources.

### Scene Ending

Kenji establishes the initial development plan for the AI and begins work on its foundation.

**Transition:** The chapter concludes with the first concrete step toward creating the tools that will support Akatsuki's future technological development.

---

## 6. Scene Five — The First Step

**Purpose:** End the chapter with forward momentum and establish the beginning of Kenji's new enterprise.

**POV:** Kenji, first person.

**Setting:** The workshop.

### Opening Situation

Kenji is already working on the initial AI framework.

The chapter should not repeat the planning already covered in the previous scene. It should show him beginning implementation.

### Events and Actions

- He organizes the first development tasks.

- He starts configuring the available hardware.

- He establishes a basic structure for the system.

- He records the work that remains to be done.

- He returns briefly to the Med-Pod project and considers how the AI will support it.

The AI does not need to become operational in this chapter. Its initial development is enough.

### Characterization

Kenji should end the chapter with a sense of purpose rather than grandiose declarations.

He has selected his first project, identified its requirements, and begun building the tools necessary to accomplish it.

### Conflict

There is no need for an additional dramatic crisis.

The central obstacle remains the gap between his ambitions and his available resources.

### Scene Ending

End with Kenji actively beginning the work.

The closing should reinforce that his ambitions will be pursued through practical development, incremental progress, and the creation of useful capabilities.

**Ending Function:** Establish the story's initial direction and lead directly into Chapter 2, where the specialized AI and workshop improvements receive greater attention.

---

## 7. Chapter-Wide Characterization Requirements

### Kenji

Portray him as:

- Intelligent.

- Confident.

- Practical.

- Technically capable.

- Independent.

- Proactive.

- Resourceful.

- Comfortable making decisions.

He should not:

- Behave timidly or incompetently.

- Spend excessive time second-guessing obvious decisions.

- Constantly remind himself that he has memories from another life.

- Become obsessed with explaining the implications of his reincarnation.

- Treat ordinary social interaction as an inconvenience.

- Possess unlimited money, equipment, or resources.

### His Mother

Her presence should be established through the inherited house and shop.

Her influence can be reflected in Kenji's familiarity with mechanical work and the practical skills he possesses.

Do not introduce unnecessary flashbacks or make grief the focus of the opening chapter.

### Other Characters

Any customer or minor supporting character should serve a clear purpose.

Do not introduce a large supporting cast simply to make the opening feel populated.

---

## 8. Style and Pacing Requirements

1. Write in first-person past tense throughout this chapter.

2. Start with activity, not scenery or exposition.

3. Keep descriptions concise and relevant.

4. Let background information emerge naturally.

5. Use dialogue where it contributes to characterization or scene progression.

6. Avoid excessive internal monologues.

7. Keep technical explanations understandable.

8. Do not turn the chapter into a series of engineering reports.

9. Avoid unnecessary dramatic conflicts.

10. Give each scene a clear purpose and progression.

11. Do not rush through the entire Med-Pod development process.

12. Do not make the AI fully operational before Chapter 2.

13. Maintain a believable balance between operating the shop and pursuing research.

14. Do not establish Akatsuki before its formal founding in Chapter 4.

15. Do not introduce ASID or major security threats at this stage.

---

## 9. Continuity Checklist

Before completing the chapter, verify:

- The story takes place in 2137.

- Kenji is twenty years old.

- He is an American of Japanese descent.

- He lives in the house inherited from his deceased mother.

- His mother was an automotive mechanic.

- He inherited her mechanic shop.

- His financial and technical resources remain limited.

- His memories are already integrated; no merger drama is introduced.

- His Mass Effect knowledge remains at casual-gamer level.

- Med-Pods are selected as his first major project.

- Specialized non-sapient AI is identified as a necessary supporting project.

- The AI has not yet become a fully developed system.

- Akatsuki has not yet been formally established.

- No premature ASID storyline or major security crisis is introduced.

---

## 10. Final Drafting Direction

Write this as the opening chapter of an actual science-fiction novel, not as an expanded version of this outline.

The scenes and events above establish the intended progression, but the prose should remain natural.

Claude has freedom to:

- Write natural dialogue.

- Develop small interactions.

- Choose appropriate transitions.

- Add incidental details that enrich the scenes.

- Adjust the exact sequence of minor actions where doing so improves the prose.

Claude must not:

- Change established story decisions.

- Invent major developments that contradict the outline.

- Add unnecessary exposition.

- Pad the chapter with repetitive technical work.

- Turn Kenji into a passive observer of his own story.

**The chapter's governing principle: Introduce Kenji by showing him doing something useful, then let the reader discover what he intends to build and why.**
