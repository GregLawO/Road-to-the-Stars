# Chapter 6 — The Company We've Built

**Scene-by-Scene Drafting Brief**

**Year:** 2148  
**Kenji's age:** Approximately 31  
**POV:** First-person, Himura Kenji  
**Purpose:** Reintroduce Kenji after the eleven-year time skip and show, through ordinary operations and interactions, how much Akatsuki Technologies has grown since 2137.

## Central Approach

Do not write a chronological summary of the eleven years.

The chapter should follow Kenji through a relatively ordinary working day. Readers discover Akatsuki's scale through the people he interacts with, the facilities he visits, the decisions he makes, and the systems that operate around him.

The company should feel like a functioning organization rather than an exhibition of technology.

Kenji has not yet recognized the Mass Effect universe. Nothing in this chapter should suggest that he knows what is coming.

## Scene 1 — An Ordinary Morning

Open with Kenji beginning his day.

Establish the passage of time through his surroundings, routines, and capabilities rather than a lengthy explanation.

Show:

- His comfortable but practical living arrangements.
    
- His improved physical condition and subtle enhancements.
    
- His personal AI assistants supporting his routine.
    
- His human personal staff handling arrangements and interpersonal matters.
    
- His Chief of Staff demonstrating independent judgment and the willingness to challenge him.
    

The interaction should establish that Kenji is successful but not isolated. He has people around him who know him well enough to disagree.

Avoid a long inventory of enhancements or AI capabilities.

## Scene 2 — The Company at Work

Move into Akatsuki's headquarters or primary business facilities.

Show the company's advanced automation naturally:

- Specialized AI systems coordinate routine corporate operations.
    
- Droids handle manufacturing, logistics, maintenance, and other repetitive work.
    
- AI systems make delegated operational decisions within authorized limits.
    
- Human employees retain responsibility for leadership, strategic decisions, external relationships, research, and exceptional situations.
    

Include a small operational issue or disagreement that demonstrates the system's limitations and the need for human judgment.

Do not make the AI malfunction merely to create drama. A difference in risk assessment, conflicting priorities, or an unusual situation is sufficient.

## Scene 3 — People Behind the Organization

Kenji interacts with a small number of established or newly introduced human colleagues.

Show that Akatsuki's success depends on people with their own expertise and priorities.

Possible elements:

- An engineering discussion.
    
- A research or manufacturing decision.
    
- A disagreement about resource allocation or project priorities.
    
- A colleague offering an alternative that Kenji accepts or seriously considers.
    

Do not introduce a parade of executives or create an extensive organizational chart.

Use only the people necessary to make the company feel populated and credible.

## Scene 4 — Research and Development

Show the current state of Akatsuki's medical and technological work.

The company's capabilities should have advanced substantially since the early prototype in 2137.

Possible areas:

- Mature medical technology and Med-Pod development.
    
- Specialized AI and robotics.
    
- Advanced manufacturing.
    
- Gravitic research.
    
- Spacecraft-related engineering.
    

Distinguish mature commercial products from ongoing research.

Kenji should still be personally involved in selected technical work, but he is no longer the only person capable of solving problems.

His private Engineering Assistant and Knowledge Management AI remain separate from Akatsuki's corporate systems. He shares validated research selectively.

Do not introduce complete memory-transfer technology or operational personal battle droids as established capabilities.

## Scene 5 — Security and ASID

Show ASID as an established organization rather than an improvised security team.

Its responsibilities include:

- Executive protection.
    
- Corporate counterintelligence.
    
- Threat assessment.
    
- Facility security.
    
- Protection of sensitive research and intellectual property.
    

A short briefing or security-related interaction can demonstrate that Akatsuki's success has attracted unwanted attention.

ASID should appear professional, competent, and appropriately discreet.

Do not introduce Reaper-related intelligence, alien artifacts, or explicit Mass Effect preparations. Kenji has not yet recognized the setting.

## Scene 6 — The Scale of What Was Built

Give the reader a broader impression of Akatsuki's operations.

This may include:

- Manufacturing facilities.
    
- Medical technology operations.
    
- Research centers.
    
- Commercial and industrial partnerships.
    
- Defense contracts.
    
- Callisto as an established off-world corporate settlement.
    

Use a natural transition, conversation, report, or decision to introduce these developments.

Do not turn this scene into a company history lecture.

The important impression is that Akatsuki has become a substantial organization with operations beyond Earth.

## Scene 7 — Closing

End with a character-driven moment rather than a checklist or grand declaration.

Possible approaches:

- Kenji reflects briefly on how much has changed since he started in his mother's shop.
    
- A human colleague reminds him that the company is no longer something he can build alone.
    
- A personal interaction provides a contrast to the pressures of running Akatsuki.
    
- An ordinary decision reveals the scale of responsibility he now carries.
    

The ending should feel earned and understated.

Do not foreshadow the Charon Relay or reveal that Kenji recognizes Mass Effect.

## Writing Requirements

- First-person POV throughout.
    
- Approximately 3,500–4,500 words, unless the material naturally warrants less.
    
- Begin with an active scene, not a long exposition paragraph.
    
- Maintain the grounded, technically credible style established in Chapters 1–5.
    
- Show technological advancement through use rather than catalogues.
    
- Avoid repetitive diagnose-fix-test sequences.
    
- Include natural interpersonal friction.
    
- Keep dialogue purposeful and character-specific.
    
- AI systems are specialized, non-sapient, and role-appropriate.
    
- Human staff must remain relevant.
    
- Do not over-explain the company's structure.
    
- Do not introduce alien characters or direct alien contact.
    
- Do not mention Eden Prime or suggest Kenji has recognized the Mass Effect universe.
    

**Success criterion:** Readers should finish the chapter understanding that eleven years have passed, Akatsuki has become a major corporation, and Kenji has grown into a leader who relies on capable people and advanced systems without losing his personal identity.
