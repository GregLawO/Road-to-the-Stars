# Chapter 10 — The Universe Just Got Bigger

**Scene-by-Scene Drafting Brief**

**Year:** 2148  
**POV:** First-person, Himura Kenji  
**Purpose:** Introduce the Charon Relay discovery, trigger Kenji's realization that he is living in the Mass Effect universe, and mark the transition from his previous ambitions to a much larger understanding of what lies ahead.

## Central Approach

This is a major turning point, but it should not become an abrupt genre shift or an exposition-heavy revelation.

The discovery should emerge through an ordinary scientific or corporate development. Kenji should initially approach it as another important astronomical discovery.

His recognition comes only after the evidence establishes what the object is.

The emotional and strategic consequences should follow naturally from that realization.

## Scene 1 — An Unexpected Discovery

Open with Kenji engaged in ordinary work when an unusual astronomical discovery reaches his attention.

The discovery concerns an object in the Pluto system, later identified as the Charon Relay.

Possible circumstances:

- An astronomical survey detects an anomalous structure.
    
- A research team identifies unusual properties inconsistent with a natural object.
    
- Further observations reveal geometric or material characteristics suggesting artificial construction.
    
- Akatsuki's researchers request additional analysis or Kenji's attention.
    

Do not begin with Kenji already knowing what the object is.

The initial discovery should be presented through credible scientific observation and cautious interpretation.

## Scene 2 — Verification

Show the process of investigating the object.

Researchers should distinguish observations from hypotheses.

The investigation may establish:

- The object is artificial.
    
- Its scale and construction are inconsistent with known human engineering.
    
- Its location and characteristics make it unlike any ordinary human installation.
    
- It appears to be some form of advanced transportation infrastructure.
    

Avoid having the researchers immediately identify the Protheans or understand the relay network.

The discovery should remain scientifically extraordinary before it becomes personally significant to Kenji.

## Scene 3 — The Realization

Once the evidence becomes sufficiently clear, Kenji recognizes the Charon Relay.

This is when he realizes that his memories of Mass Effect are not merely memories of a fictional setting.

He should experience a brief but powerful internal reaction.

Important points:

- His memories provide context that the researchers do not possess.
    
- He recognizes the implications of the relay's existence.
    
- He understands that humanity is not alone.
    
- He realizes that the galaxy is much larger and more dangerous than he had previously believed.
    

Do not make him immediately recall every character, event, faction, or plot detail from the games.

His memories are useful but incomplete, and the actual timeline has already diverged in ways he cannot yet understand.

## Scene 4 — Keeping Control

Kenji must decide how to respond.

He should not immediately announce everything he knows.

He has no reason to believe that a private revelation about his memories would be useful or credible to anyone else.

Instead, he should:

- Ensure the discovery is handled securely.
    
- Authorize further scientific investigation.
    
- Restrict access appropriately without causing unnecessary panic.
    
- Begin evaluating the implications for Akatsuki and humanity.
    
- Avoid revealing knowledge he cannot substantiate.
    

His decisions should reflect his experience as a corporate leader and his awareness that premature conclusions can cause damage.

## Scene 5 — A Different Strategic Picture

Allow Kenji to begin reassessing his priorities.

He now has reason to consider:

- The possibility of extraterrestrial civilizations.
    
- The existence of advanced technology beyond humanity's current understanding.
    
- The likelihood that humanity's discovery of the relay will eventually bring it into contact with other civilizations.
    
- The strategic importance of independent FTL research.
    
- The need for stronger intelligence and security capabilities.
    
- The vulnerability of a humanity that is technologically inexperienced on a galactic scale.
    

Do not turn this into a comprehensive Reaper War preparation plan.

Kenji has only just recognized the setting. He needs to verify what he remembers and understand how much has changed.

His immediate response should be measured rather than impulsive.

## Scene 6 — A Conversation

Include a meaningful interaction with another human character.

This could be a trusted colleague, senior researcher, or member of Kenji's personal staff.

The person should notice that the discovery has affected him more than he expected.

Kenji does not need to reveal the truth about his memories.

The conversation should demonstrate that he is not handling the situation in complete isolation, even if he must keep his most important knowledge private.

## Scene 7 — Closing

End with Kenji contemplating the consequences of the discovery.

His previous goals have not become irrelevant:

- Building Akatsuki.
    
- Expanding humanity's industrial and technological capabilities.
    
- Developing independent spaceflight.
    
- Protecting the people who depend on him.
    

But the context has changed.

Humanity is no longer merely expanding into its own solar system. It is approaching a much larger galactic reality.

The chapter should close with a restrained sense of urgency.

Do not introduce Amatsu or Shanxi yet. Those developments belong to the subsequent chapters.

## Writing Requirements

- First-person POV throughout.
    
- Approximately 3,500–4,500 words, unless the material naturally warrants less.
    
- The discovery must unfold through observation and verification.
    
- Kenji must not recognize the relay before the evidence supports it.
    
- Researchers should not immediately know more than their evidence establishes.
    
- Kenji's realization should be emotionally significant but restrained.
    
- His Mass Effect memories are incomplete and potentially unreliable.
    
- Avoid a long exposition dump about the entire Mass Effect setting.
    
- Do not make Kenji omniscient about future events.
    
- Preserve the established corporate, technological, and personal continuity.
    
- No direct alien contact in this chapter.
    
- Do not name the planet Amatsu or establish the Shanxi settlement.
    
- Never use the name Eden Prime in narrative prose, dialogue, in-universe documents, or character narration.
    

**Success criterion:** Readers should feel that the story's scope has fundamentally changed. Kenji has spent eleven years building a powerful organization without consciously recognizing the setting. Now he finally understands what universe he is in, and the implications are enormous.
