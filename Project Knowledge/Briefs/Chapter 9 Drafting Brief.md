# Chapter 9 — Among Our Own

**Scene-by-Scene Drafting Brief**

**Year:** 2148  
**POV:** First-person, Himura Kenji  
**Purpose:** Give Kenji a chapter focused on his personal relationships and the human side of his life, establishing that despite Akatsuki's enormous growth and automation, he has not become an isolated corporate technocrat.

## Central Approach

This chapter is about people, not technological milestones.

After Chapters 6–8 establish Akatsuki's corporate scale, Callisto settlement, and spacecraft operations, give readers time to see Kenji outside the immediate demands of running his company.

Follow him through a social occasion and the interactions surrounding it. The chapter should reveal relationships through natural conversation, disagreements, familiarity, humor, and shared history.

Avoid making every character's dialogue about Akatsuki.

## Scene 1 — A Change of Pace

Open with Kenji finishing or stepping away from a demanding workday.

Someone in his personal or professional circle has arranged, invited him to, or persuaded him to attend a social gathering.

Establish that:

- Kenji has a life beyond the company.
    
- His human staff help him maintain that balance.
    
- His Chief of Staff can tell him when he needs to stop working.
    
- His personal AI assistants support his schedule without replacing human relationships.
    

The opening should lead naturally into the social event rather than becoming another executive briefing.

## Scene 2 — The Gathering

Introduce the setting and attendees.

It could be a private dinner, informal gathering, celebration, or social event involving people Kenji has known through his personal and professional life.

Use a manageable cast rather than introducing many characters at once.

The gathering should feel comfortable and lived-in, with people who have enough familiarity to speak candidly.

Include:

- Ordinary conversation.
    
- Humor and personal observations.
    
- Differences in opinion.
    
- References to shared experiences or events from the preceding years.
    

Do not turn the gathering into a meeting disguised as a dinner.

## Scene 3 — Relationships and Friction

Give Kenji meaningful conversations with two or three people.

These interactions should reveal different sides of his life:

- Someone who knows him well enough to challenge his priorities.
    
- Someone with whom he can relax and speak without being the company founder.
    
- Someone whose ambitions or concerns differ from his own.
    

A conversation may touch on the demands of Akatsuki's growth, but it should not become a corporate status report.

Kenji should be able to listen, joke, disagree, and occasionally be caught off guard.

Do not make everyone admire him or treat him as a visionary.

## Scene 4 — The Cost of Success

Include a more personal conversation about what Kenji's life has become.

Possible subjects:

- How much his responsibilities have changed since 2137.
    
- Whether he has enough time for himself.
    
- The difficulty of maintaining friendships while running a large corporation.
    
- The difference between being surrounded by people and actually spending time with them.
    
- What he wants his future to look like beyond continued expansion.
    

Do not force an emotional confession or an identity crisis.

The scene should be honest but understated.

## Scene 5 — A Moment of Ordinary Life

Give Kenji an opportunity to enjoy himself without a strategic purpose.

This could be:

- A shared meal.
    
- A game or recreational activity.
    
- A conversation about something unrelated to work.
    
- A personal interest or hobby.
    
- A spontaneous outing or small shared experience.
    

The point is to show Kenji as a person with preferences, humor, and ordinary human needs.

Do not invent an elaborate romance or a major personal subplot without prior development.

## Scene 6 — Closing

End with a quiet but meaningful interaction.

Kenji should leave the gathering with a renewed appreciation for the people in his life, not a sudden revelation or grand philosophical conclusion.

The chapter should reinforce that the people around him are not merely employees, resources, or supporting cast members in his personal ambitions.

They have their own lives, and their relationships with him matter independently of Akatsuki.

## Writing Requirements

- First-person POV throughout.
    
- Approximately 2,500–3,500 words, unless the material naturally warrants less.
    
- Focus on dialogue and character interaction.
    
- Use a small, manageable cast.
    
- Give supporting characters independent personalities and opinions.
    
- Include humor, warmth, and at least one meaningful disagreement.
    
- Avoid exposition-heavy conversations.
    
- Avoid turning the social event into a corporate meeting.
    
- Do not make every character admire or defer to Kenji.
    
- Maintain continuity with Chapters 1–8.
    
- No alien characters or direct alien contact.
    
- Do not introduce Amatsu or Shanxi.
    
- Do not reveal Kenji's recognition of the Mass Effect universe.
    

**Success criterion:** Readers should see that Kenji has built more than a corporation. He has relationships and a personal life worth protecting, and the people around him matter for reasons beyond their usefulness to his ambitions.
