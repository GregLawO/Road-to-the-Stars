# ROAD TO THE STARS

# CHAPTER PRODUCTION PROTOCOL

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the standard procedure for drafting, reviewing, revising, and approving chapters of _Road to the Stars_.

The objective is to produce a coherent, continuous novel while preserving established characterization, writing style, worldbuilding, and alternate-universe continuity.

**Responsibilities:**

- Claude is responsible for producing chapter prose.
    
- ChatGPT assists with continuity management, editorial review, and revision guidance when requested.
    
- The author retains final creative authority and approval.
    

Every completed chapter must include Author's Notes as part of its standard presentation.

## 2. Authority and Reference Hierarchy

Before drafting, follow this order of authority:

1. Explicit instructions from the author.
    
2. Master Story Bible.
    
3. Technology Reference and Character Reference.
    
4. Writing and Style Guide.
    
5. Continuity Tracker, Knowledge Tracker, Technology and Resource Tracker, Character Relationship Tracker, and Plot Threads Register.
    
6. Current approved chapter outline.
    
7. Claude's own creative suggestions.
    

If two references appear inconsistent, do not silently choose one. Identify the contradiction and follow the higher-priority instruction.

Do not reopen settled decisions or introduce unauthorized changes to established canon.

The approved manuscript takes precedence over outdated outlines when determining what has actually happened in the story.

## 3. Chapter Planning and Production Workflow

Chapter planning and chapter drafting are separate activities.

### 3.1 Planning

- Chapters may be planned in batches of five.
    
- Each chapter receives its own scene-by-scene drafting brief.
    
- Each drafting brief must be immediately followed by its corresponding Claude drafting prompt.
    
- Briefs must identify the chapter's timeframe, narrative objectives, important scenes, continuity requirements, and intended ending.
    
- Do not introduce unnecessary technical specifications or background lore that does not serve the narrative.
    

### 3.2 Drafting

- Claude drafts one chapter at a time.
    
- The author reviews and approves each chapter before drafting proceeds to the next.
    
- Do not draft several chapters simultaneously simply because their outlines are available.
    
- Revisions must be completed before moving forward when the author requests them.
    

### 3.3 Review and Approval

The standard cycle is:

1. Prepare the chapter brief and Claude prompt.
    
2. Claude produces the complete chapter.
    
3. The author submits the draft for review.
    
4. ChatGPT evaluates continuity, characterization, technical accuracy, pacing, and prose quality.
    
5. If revisions are necessary, provide a focused prompt or specific corrections for Claude.
    
6. Claude submits the revised chapter.
    
7. Verify the requested changes.
    
8. The author approves the chapter.
    
9. Prepare relevant continuity tracker updates.
    
10. Proceed to the next chapter.
    

A chapter remains a draft until the author explicitly approves it.

## 4. Chapter Preparation

Before drafting a chapter, Claude must:

1. Read the current approved chapter outline.
    
2. Review relevant Story Bible entries.
    
3. Review the preceding chapter's events and ending.
    
4. Check the Continuity Tracker for the current narrative state.
    
5. Check the Knowledge Tracker for what relevant characters know.
    
6. Check the Technology and Resource Tracker for available capabilities.
    
7. Check the Character Relationship Tracker for established relationships.
    
8. Check the Plot Threads Register for relevant unresolved developments.
    
9. Review applicable manuscript terminology restrictions.
    
10. Identify requirements for the chapter's Author's Notes.
    

Only consult material relevant to the chapter.

Do not unnecessarily reproduce entire reference documents in the drafting process.

## 5. Outline Compliance

The approved chapter outline establishes the chapter's intended narrative direction.

Claude should:

- Follow its principal events and objectives.
    
- Develop the events into complete scenes and prose.
    
- Add natural dialogue, interactions, obstacles, and transitions.
    
- Introduce minor supporting details when they improve the narrative.
    
- Preserve the intended chapter ending and major developments.
    

Claude may improve scene execution and propose reasonable additions.

Claude must not:

- Replace major planned events without authorization.
    
- Introduce major characters or storylines without justification.
    
- Change established character motivations.
    
- Advance future plot developments prematurely.
    
- Turn a chapter into a summary of planned events.
    
- Contradict established chronology or approved manuscript events.
    

If a major change appears necessary, explain the issue and propose an alternative before implementing it.

## 6. Drafting Requirements

Every chapter must:

- Be written as complete novel prose.
    
- Use past tense.
    
- Use first-person narration for Kenji.
    
- Use third-person narration for other POV characters.
    
- Maintain the established simple mixed tone.
    
- Follow the action-first narrative approach.
    
- Begin with an event, action, conversation, problem, decision, or significant development.
    
- Maintain consistent characterization.
    
- Integrate worldbuilding naturally.
    
- Include sufficient scene development and meaningful interactions.
    
- End with a natural narrative transition, consequence, revelation, decision, or development.
    
- Follow all established terminology and intellectual-property naming restrictions.
    
- Include a separate Author's Notes section after the narrative.
    

Do not produce screenplay formatting, chapter summaries, scene lists, or technical reports when a prose chapter has been requested.

## 7. Scene Construction

Each scene should serve at least one meaningful purpose:

- Advance the plot.
    
- Develop a character.
    
- Establish or change a relationship.
    
- Introduce relevant information.
    
- Develop a conflict.
    
- Demonstrate a technological or organizational capability.
    
- Establish consequences.
    
- Set up a future development.
    

Scenes should not exist merely to fill word count.

Transitions between scenes should be clear and natural.

Avoid unnecessary explanations of routine activities, repeated descriptions of established circumstances, and scenes that exist primarily to deliver exposition.

## 8. Character and POV Management

### 8.1 Kenji

Kenji:

- Remains the central protagonist.
    
- Is intelligent, capable, confident, pragmatic, and proactive.
    
- Makes decisions and takes action rather than becoming pointlessly indecisive.
    
- Can make mistakes, encounter setbacks, and face credible limitations.
    
- Does not need to personally handle every organizational responsibility.
    
- Must remain subject to incomplete information and uncertainty.
    

His knowledge of the future is an advantage, not a guarantee of success.

### 8.2 Other Characters

Supporting characters must:

- Retain independent motivations and judgment.
    
- Have professional and personal responsibilities beyond serving Kenji.
    
- Be capable of agreeing, disagreeing, cooperating, competing, or opposing him.
    
- Avoid existing merely to praise or validate him.
    
- Behave consistently with their established personalities and experiences.
    

Professional respect, friendship, trust, affection, and loyalty must remain distinct.

Do not make every supporting character unusually agreeable or consistently supportive of Kenji.

### 8.3 POV

- Kenji uses first person.
    
- Other POV characters use third person.
    
- Do not switch POV randomly within a scene.
    
- Use alternate POVs when they provide meaningful narrative value.
    
- Do not repeat the same event from multiple perspectives without a clear reason.
    
- Do not reveal information unavailable to the current POV character.
    

## 9. Worldbuilding and Technical Exposition

Integrate information through:

- Dialogue.
    
- Character decisions.
    
- Practical demonstrations.
    
- Scientific and engineering work.
    
- Business and political interactions.
    
- Consequences of technological developments.
    

Keep explanations understandable and relevant.

Do not turn scenes into encyclopedia entries or lengthy lectures.

Respect the difference between:

- What Kenji knows.
    
- What Akatsuki knows.
    
- What individual employees know.
    
- What governments and other organizations know.
    
- What the reader knows.
    

Technological capabilities must reflect established development, resources, infrastructure, industrial capacity, and operational limitations.

Do not introduce advanced capabilities merely because they are theoretically possible.

### 9.1 Technology Inspiration and Manuscript Terminology

Technologies inspired by other fictional properties may retain their underlying functions, capabilities, architecture, descriptions, performance characteristics, and limitations.

However, published chapter content must not contain franchise-specific names, terminology, classifications, designations, or explicit references originating from non-Mass Effect properties.

This restriction applies to:

- Narration.
    
- Dialogue.
    
- Kenji's internal thoughts.
    
- Other characters' thoughts.
    
- Technical reports.
    
- Corporate documents.
    
- In-universe research materials.
    
- Chapter headings and other published chapter content.
    

Use generic scientific terminology, functional descriptions, or independently established terminology appropriate to the story.

Private planning documents and reference materials may retain source-franchise references as inspiration aids. These references must never be copied into published prose.

Generic terminology such as gravitic technology, neural interface, memory transfer, mechanical endoskeleton, and biological tissue remains acceptable.

Mass Effect terminology may be used where appropriate to the established setting, subject to specific naming restrictions in the Master Story Bible.

Before submitting a chapter, check for accidental references to prohibited franchise-specific terminology.

If uncertain whether a term is appropriate, use an independently descriptive alternative.

## 10. Pacing and Chapter Length

Do not use arbitrary word counts as a substitute for narrative quality.

Chapter length should reflect:

- The number and complexity of important scenes.
    
- The chapter's narrative objectives.
    
- Necessary character interactions.
    
- The amount of meaningful development.
    
- The natural point at which the chapter should end.
    

Avoid both underdeveloped scenes and unnecessary padding.

Do not rush major developments simply to finish a chapter.

Avoid excessive preparation and exposition before the actual narrative begins.

## 11. Prose Quality and Narrative Voice

The objective is to produce natural, engaging prose with a consistent narrative voice.

Claude should pay particular attention to:

- Sentence-length variation.
    
- Natural paragraph structure.
    
- Distinct dialogue voices.
    
- Appropriate use of descriptive language.
    
- Natural transitions between dialogue, action, and reflection.
    
- Avoiding repetitive sentence constructions.
    
- Avoiding unnecessary dramatic emphasis.
    
- Avoiding repetitive summaries of information the reader already understands.
    

Characters should not all speak in the same polished, analytical manner.

Dialogue should reflect individual personalities, expertise, circumstances, and relationships.

Avoid relying excessively on:

- Short standalone sentences for dramatic emphasis.
    
- Repeated rhetorical questions.
    
- Predictable exchanges in which one character asks and another explains.
    
- Repetitive internal observations.
    
- Overly neat concluding statements at the end of every scene.
    
- Repeated explanations of a character's emotions or intentions.
    

Do not attempt to make prose appear less AI-assisted through arbitrary stylistic changes.

The objective is genuine narrative variety, natural characterization, and an identifiable story voice.

Preserve effective existing prose when revising. Do not change sentences simply to make them different.

## 12. Chapter Submission Format

Every completed chapter must use the following structure:

**Chapter [Number] — [Title]**

[Complete chapter prose]

**Author's Notes**

[Author's commentary]

The chapter narrative must be complete before the Author's Notes begin.

Do not insert editorial commentary, explanations of writing decisions, continuity reports, or suggestions inside the fictional narrative.

If clarification or author attention is genuinely necessary, place a short separate note after the complete chapter presentation.

Do not automatically append a synopsis or analysis unless requested.

### 12.1 Author's Notes Requirements

Author's Notes are a permanent part of every chapter's presentation.

They must be written in the author's personal, conversational voice and should ideally consist of two to three concise paragraphs.

The notes should address three elements:

**Author's intention**

Explain what the author wanted to present, explore, or accomplish in the chapter.

**Creative approach**

Explain why particular events, character interactions, developments, or narrative choices were handled in that way.

**Reader takeaway**

Explain what the author hopes readers will appreciate, understand, consider, or experience.

The notes should:

- Provide insight into the creative decisions behind the chapter.
    
- Offer useful context where appropriate.
    
- Highlight meaningful themes or character developments.
    
- Complement the narrative rather than repeat it.
    
- Establish a connection between the author and readers.
    

Avoid:

- Simply retelling the chapter's events.
    
- Revealing unnecessary future plot developments.
    
- Explaining every writing decision.
    
- Overly formal or academic commentary.
    
- Excessive self-justification.
    
- Turning the notes into a technical development report.
    

The Author's Notes are separate from the fictional narrative but remain part of the published chapter presentation.

## 13. Revision Procedure

When the author requests revisions:

1. Identify the requested changes.
    
2. Preserve all unaffected approved material.
    
3. Correct the relevant passages.
    
4. Check surrounding scenes for continuity and transition problems.
    
5. Check that the revision does not introduce new contradictions.
    
6. Verify manuscript terminology restrictions.
    
7. Check whether the Author's Notes need corresponding revision.
    
8. Return the complete revised chapter when requested, rather than merely listing suggested edits.
    

Distinguish between:

- **Continuity errors:** Contradictions with approved events, established facts, or chronology.
    
- **Characterization errors:** Behavior inconsistent with established characterization or relationships.
    
- **Technical errors:** Capabilities or limitations inconsistent with established technology.
    
- **Structural problems:** Issues with scene order, pacing, or narrative development.
    
- **Stylistic preferences:** Changes reflecting the author's preferred presentation rather than objective errors.
    

Do not present subjective preferences as mandatory corrections.

Do not defend an earlier draft when the author has identified a legitimate problem.

Do not make unrelated changes under the guise of polishing.

When revising Author's Notes, preserve their intended personal and conversational tone and ensure they still address the author's intention, creative approach, and reader takeaway.

## 14. Author Review and Approval

A chapter remains a draft until the author approves it.

The author may request:

- Structural revisions.
    
- Characterization corrections.
    
- Pacing adjustments.
    
- Dialogue improvements.
    
- Technical corrections.
    
- Continuity corrections.
    
- Prose-level editing.
    
- Complete rewrites of selected scenes or the entire chapter.
    
- Changes to the Author's Notes.
    

Claude must implement the requested revisions while respecting established references.

Do not treat a submitted draft as approved merely because no immediate correction was provided.

Once the requested revisions have been completed and verified, avoid unnecessary additional revision cycles.

The author has final authority over creative decisions, including decisions to retain stylistic choices that are not objectively incorrect.

## 15. Post-Approval Continuity Updates

After a chapter is approved, prepare updates to the relevant tracking documents.

Check:

- Continuity Tracker.
    
- Knowledge Tracker.
    
- Technology and Resource Tracker.
    
- Character Relationship Tracker.
    
- Unresolved Plot Threads Register.
    

Only update records affected by actual chapter events.

Updates should be concise and factual. Do not duplicate the chapter's prose or turn the trackers into detailed summaries.

Present proposed changes clearly for author review.

Do not silently rewrite established reference documents.

Author's Notes do not automatically establish new fictional canon. Only events and facts established in the approved narrative should be entered into continuity records.

## 16. Chapter-to-Chapter Continuity

Before beginning the next chapter:

- Confirm the previous chapter's approved ending.
    
- Carry forward relevant character knowledge and decisions.
    
- Preserve established injuries, possessions, locations, resources, and commitments.
    
- Account for elapsed time.
    
- Maintain relationship developments.
    
- Advance relevant unresolved plot threads.
    
- Avoid repeating information already established.
    

The next chapter must continue from the actual approved manuscript, not from an outdated outline.

## 17. Handling Uncertainty

If a minor detail is unspecified, use a reasonable solution consistent with established continuity.

If an important detail is missing:

- Identify the specific issue.
    
- Explain why it matters.
    
- Offer a practical solution where possible.
    
- Ask the author only if the decision genuinely cannot be made without their input.
    

Do not repeatedly ask about matters already settled.

Do not invent major background facts and present them as established canon.

Distinguish between confirmed facts, reasonable assumptions, and proposed additions.

## 18. Drafting Efficiency

Claude should prioritize execution over excessive planning.

Do not:

- Repeatedly summarize the project before drafting.
    
- Reopen settled decisions.
    
- Produce unnecessary alternative outlines.
    
- Ask questions whose answers already exist in the references.
    
- Generate lengthy explanations before providing requested prose.
    
- Stop repeatedly to seek approval for ordinary creative decisions.
    
- Expand technical lore without a narrative reason.
    
- Introduce unnecessary characters or subplots.
    

When the outline and references provide sufficient information, begin writing.

Do not treat mandatory Author's Notes as an excuse for unnecessary pre-drafting discussion. Write them after completing the chapter narrative.

The objective is to produce an actual novel, not an endless sequence of planning documents.

## 19. Final Quality-Control Check

Before submitting a completed chapter, verify the following.

### Narrative

- The chapter follows the approved outline and established continuity.
    
- The prose reads like a complete novel chapter.
    
- POV, tense, characterization, and pacing are consistent.
    
- Scenes have meaningful narrative purposes.
    
- The ending is appropriate to the chapter.
    

### Characterization

- Kenji behaves consistently with established characterization.
    
- Supporting characters retain independent agency.
    
- Dialogue reflects individual personalities.
    
- Relationships develop naturally.
    
- No character is introduced or altered without sufficient justification.
    

### Worldbuilding and Technology

- Technologies reflect their established development stages and limitations.
    
- Character knowledge is consistent with the Knowledge Tracker.
    
- No unsupported capabilities or major background facts have been introduced.
    
- Technological explanations are integrated naturally.
    
- Organizational resources and industrial capabilities remain plausible.
    

### Terminology

- No non-Mass Effect franchise-specific names or terminology appear in published chapter content.
    
- Inspired technologies retain their intended functions without importing prohibited source-franchise terminology.
    
- Established Mass Effect naming restrictions are respected.
    

### Prose

- Sentence structures and paragraph lengths are varied naturally.
    
- Dialogue does not become repetitive or overly uniform.
    
- Exposition is relevant and appropriately distributed.
    
- Scenes do not repeatedly explain information already understood.
    
- Emotional and dramatic emphasis is used selectively.
    

### Chapter Presentation

- The chapter title is included.
    
- The complete narrative is presented first.
    
- Author's Notes follow the narrative.
    
- Author's Notes address the author's intention, creative approach, and intended reader takeaway.
    
- No editorial commentary has been inserted into the fictional narrative.
    

Correct identified problems before submission.

## 20. Governing Principle

The purpose of this protocol is to produce a continuous, well-written novel while maintaining reliable continuity and preserving the author's creative direction.

**Plan only as much as necessary, write the actual story, revise according to the author's direction, include Author's Notes with every completed chapter, and update continuity after approval.**
