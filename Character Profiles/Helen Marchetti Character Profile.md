# Helen Marchetti: Character Profile

Name, age, and history are placeholders. Items marked **(decision)** are invented gap-fills.

## 1. Basic Identity & Demographics
- **Name & nicknames.** Helen Marchetti. Partners call her "Helen." Kenji says "Ms. Marchetti" at first, then "Helen." Clients call her "Counselor," which she dislikes. **(decision)**
- **Age.** Born 1999; fifty-two in 2051, kidneys failing. **(decision)**
- **Appearance.** Small, upright, short gray hair, heavy dark-framed glasses. Tailored dark suit and low heels always, even in a hospital room. A black fountain pen she caps and uncaps while she listens; when it is bad she sets the pen down and puts both hands on the page.
- **Voice & mannerisms.** Gulf Coast accent that goes flat in court; fluent Spanish from her mother. Careful, complete, slow sentences, each word chosen as if it will be read back to her. Asks "Who signs that?" before agreeing to anything. Dry, legal humor ("I'd call that an oral agreement, but that would imply it was ever spoken"); never jokes about a client's illness.

## 2. Psychology & Internal World
- **Core goal.** Immediate: the foundation filed before the quarter closes so the first gift is accepted tax-free. Overall: one structure nobody can break, still standing.
- **Needs vs. wants.** She wants a flawless structure. She needs to be needed for something other than saying no.
- **Internal conflict.** The foundation against Kenji. As trustee she can stop him and keeps that duty even when he wants something fast. She keeps an undated resignation letter in her desk, never used. **(decision)**
- **Flaws.** Assumes people act from what the paper makes them do, slow to see some stay because they want to; assumes a person who gives her everything is hiding something; underrates how much of her caution is fear of being wrong. Honesty 4 of 5: never says a false thing but chooses words so she is never asked a question she cannot answer.
- **Fears & secrets.** Fear: a gap in a contract that someone else finds first. She will not sign what she has not read to the end; only a patient dying in the next room could make her, and then she signs with a note of exactly what she did not read.
- **Worldview.** Trusts a good contract more than a good cure, and is surprised she trusts both. Does not ask where the designs come from because her client told her not to. **(decision)**

## 3. Backstory & History
- **Upbringing & family.** Pasadena, Texas, a refinery town; her father ran a small insurance office, her mother kept the books. **(decision)**
- **Defining moment.** Early in her career a client's company failed because she missed one clause in a sale; the gap is always in the line you read last. **(decision)** Memory: her father's office, every policy in a drawer.
- **Education & occupation.** Foundation and corporate lawyer, partner in a medium Houston firm; second-best, arbitrator. Object: the black fountain pen, her father's gift when she passed the bar.
- **Prior relationships.** Owes Kenji for her kidneys and years, and Ortega, her partner, dying the same autumn.

## 4. Story Role & Stakes
- **Function.** Main-cast POV (A2.2); the Janus Foundation's trustee, the one person who can stop Kenji.
- **External conflict.** Cerberus. In 2060 an operative who names his group Cerberus tries to turn her; she and Whitcombe are conditioned, so it fails, and she tells Kenji and Vance the same night (A2.18, not drafted). Oyama's three offers of 2058 are then understood to have been Cerberus's.
- **Stakes.** The foundation and the people on its terms; a gap found and used against them.
- **Arc and facts.** Recruited October 2051 in a Houston hospital room with Ortega, Vance and Ashby at the door; asks who owns the design and Kenji gives "Janus" (A2.2); files the foundation December 2051 and is named trustee. Seven-year term, renewable by choice. She asks who signs and what happens if that person disappears; goes to the paper first when someone she is responsible for is threatened; reads contracts aloud to anyone frightened of them.
- **Rules for the text.** No scenes where she is only the lawyer who says no, no long legal explanations, no sudden affection for Kenji.

## 5. Dialogue Voice Guide **(decision; built from this profile; sample lines are mine, not canon)**

**1. Vocabulary & Word Choice.**
- Paper words in plain use: signs, dated, written down, term. Her standard questions: "Who signs that?" "What happens if...?" She pins a vague word: "You said 'soon.'"
- Mr. and Ms. for others. Spanish from her mother, rare and warm. Dry humor about paperwork, never about illness. She dislikes "Counselor."
- Samples: "Who signs for it? I will want the name, the date, and what is being taken, in that order." / "You said 'soon.' Soon is not a date I can write down." / "I'll read it to you. Slowly, and all of it."

**2. Sentence Rhythm & Punctuation.**
- Careful, complete, slow sentences, each word chosen as if it will be read back to her. That completeness is part of who she is, so do not loosen it.
- One condition per sentence. She finishes her sentence when interrupted. She asks before she agrees.
- No contractions when negotiating, a few in ordinary talk with clients. Her accent goes flat in court.
- Reply length: two to four full sentences. A one-line question such as "Who signs that?" is about one in five.

**3. Nervous Habits & Tells.**
- She never says a false thing. She words an answer so that no follow-up she cannot answer can follow: "I am not going to answer that tonight."
- Afraid, she asks for the page. Angry, she gets more formal and says "Mr. Himura." With a frightened client she drops the formality and reads aloud.
- She caps and uncaps the pen while someone talks.

**4. Dialogue Traps (What to Avoid).**
- No chains of legal terms, no "objection," no Latin, no long explanations of law.
- No "I'm just the lawyer," and no warmth toward Kenji.
